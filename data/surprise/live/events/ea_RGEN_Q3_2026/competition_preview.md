## 2. Key Metrics to Watch — Ranked

**1. Capital equipment orders and book-to-bill (highest signal for 2027).** Q2 showed a "significant sequential pickup" with book-to-bill "well above 1.0," a second RFP win and a third "expected soon" — while equipment *revenue* stayed stable/down y/y. The entire bull case is that this is deferred purchasing converting in 2027, not demand destruction. A second consecutive >1.0 quarter plus the third RFP is the single most important disclosure on the call.

**2. Gross margin trough confirmation.** Does Q3 land at ~53% and does management reaffirm 53.7–54.2% for the year? A sub-52.5% Q3 breaks the Q4-leverage story and puts the FY EPS guide at risk.

**3. BioLife guidance bridge.** The $1.5B EV deal ($31.00/share = $11.25 cash + 0.1442 RGEN shares, ~24% premium to 90-day VWAP) closed October 6, 2026, and the company explicitly deferred the 2026 outlook impact to this call. What you need: the Q4 revenue stub (BioLife did $28.5M in Q2, +21% y/y, so a ~$29–30M stub is the arithmetic), the share-issuance dilution, lost interest income on roughly half the $810M cash balance, synergy phasing (≥$20M yr-1, ≥$30M yr-2), and a reaffirm of ≥$0.05 yr-1 / ≥$0.25 yr-2 accretion.

**4. Franchise mix, especially filtration.** FY targets set at Q2: proteins mid-teens (after +50% in Q2), analytics ≥25% (after +30%), chromatography >20% (large-scale columns +18% in H1), **filtration mid-single-digit** — the weakest franchise, hurt by the Polymem divestiture, gene therapy headwinds and customer inventory destocking. Filtration is ~the largest franchise; an inflection here is the cleanest upside surprise available.

**5. China / APAC durability and customer mix.** APAC +40% in Q2, China +60% in H1, with management citing 40% of the global cell therapy funnel in China. Also watch emerging biotech (five straight quarters of recovery, +high teens in Q2), CDMO strength, and new modalities (+~10%, best quarter since early 2025).

**Secondary:** tariffs (reduced to "minimal impact" for FY from an earlier 50bp assumption; $1M revenue headwind in Q2), pricing execution, and free cash flow (Q2: $33M operating cash flow, $5M capex).

## 4. Catalyst Checklist

1. **Revenue vs. $206.71M and vs. management's "slightly up sequentially"** — a print below $206M means Q3 came in under the company's own July framing, which is a different and worse signal than a consensus miss.
2. **Gross margin ~53% confirmed as the trough, with FY 53.7–54.2% reaffirmed** — the whole FY EPS guide ($2.03–2.09, H2 implied $1.01–1.07 against $1.02 booked in H1) rests on Q4 margin recovery.
3. **Capital equipment book-to-bill >1.0 for a second quarter + third RFP win** — the buy-side wants a quantified 2027 backlog, not adjectives.
4. **The BioLife FY26 bridge** — revenue stub, diluted share count, interest-income drag, synergy timing, and whether ≥$0.05 yr-1 accretion survives contact with purchase accounting. Any early 2027 revenue framing (consensus already sits at $1.08B, +29%) is a live risk either way.
5. **Filtration guidance revision** — mid-single-digit FY is the low bar in the portfolio; raising it is the highest-quality beat available, cutting it is the highest-quality warning.

## Positioning View

The bar for this quarter is low by construction: consensus sits at the bottom of management's sequential revenue framing, and the EPS number already embeds the company's pre-announced margin trough. The likely outcome is a modest beat that the stock does not pay for, because at 85x the debate has already moved past Q3 to two questions this print can only partially answer — whether the Q2 equipment order pickup is a trend, and what BioLife actually does to 2027 EPS. **Risk/reward into the event is unattractive for new longs**; the better expression is to wait for the guidance bridge and the order-book disclosure on the 8:00 AM call, where the real information is.
