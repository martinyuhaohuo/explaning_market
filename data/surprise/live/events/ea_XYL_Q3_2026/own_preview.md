## Prior Quarter (Q2 2026, reported Jul 28)

- Adj. EPS beat; revenue roughly in line to slightly light (sources disagree: $2.336B–$2.34B).
- FY EPS guide raised; FY revenue guide trimmed.
- **Water Infrastructure:** $683M, +5% (organic +3%). Transport strong; Treatment and China weak.
- **Applied Water:** $501M, +4% (organic +3%). Commercial strength.
- **Measurement & Control Solutions:** $508M, −6%. Full-year outlook cut to low-single-digit growth on electric metering project delays, utility affordability concerns, and cautious utility spending.
- **Water Solutions & Services:** $644M, +3%. Orders +41% organic in total and +147% in this segment, which is a key forward indicator.
- **China:** revenue −27%.

## Key Metrics to Watch (ranked)

1. **Measurement & Control (M&C) organic growth and metering timing.** The Q2 cut is the main risk. Does the shortfall persist, and is the electric metering delay a timing issue or lost share?
2. **Orders and backlog, especially Water Solutions & Services.** The Q2 order surge needs to convert into Q3 and Q4 revenue.
3. **Organic growth vs. the ~3% guide**, with a breakdown by Treatment, Transport, and Applied Water.
4. **Adj. EBITDA/operating margin expansion.** Q2 margins expanded, and management has leaned on this for the EPS raise.
5. **China trajectory.** A −27% decline is a drag; any stabilization is upside.
6. **Full-year guide.** Whether management holds the $5.55–$5.70 EPS range (or raises it again) and reaffirms revenue of ~$9.2B.
7. **Free cash flow and buyback pace.** Share repurchases are contributing to the EPS raise.

## Catalyst Checklist

1. **M&C revenue vs. the −6% Q2 print and the lowered segment outlook.** Why it matters: it drove the Q2 guide trim and is the most likely source of a negative reaction.
2. **Water Solutions & Services orders vs. the +41% organic Q2 print.** Why it matters: orders are the best read on FY2027 revenue.
3. **FY2026 EPS guide ($5.55–$5.70) and revenue (~$9.2B).** Why it matters: the buy side typically trades on the full-year outlook, not the quarter.
4. **Margin progression.** Why it matters: margin gains are the main EPS driver, and sustaining them supports the raised guide.
5. **Narrative: China, buybacks, capital allocation.** Any strategic or M&A commentary would shift the story. None was found in my searches.
