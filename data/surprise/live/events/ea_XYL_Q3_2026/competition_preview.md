## 1. The Setup in One Paragraph

Xylem is a **beat-the-quarter, lose-the-multiple story**. Operationally the company is executing: Q2 FY26 adjusted EPS of $1.46 beat the $1.34 consensus by ~9%, adjusted EBITDA margin expanded 150bps to 23.3%, and management *raised* FY26 EPS guidance to $5.55–$5.70. Yet the stock is down ~25% YTD and sits ~10% above its 52-week low. The market has stopped paying for margin self-help and is now exclusively focused on **top-line growth**: FY26 organic growth of just 2–3%, an electric-metering air pocket, utility capex uncertainty, a ~$250M international metrology divestiture dragging reported growth, an abrupt CFO change, and $1.5B of new debt for the $1.46B Cornell/Roper pump deal. Q3 is therefore not an EPS event — it is a **credibility event for the Q4 revenue ramp and the 2027 growth bridge**.

## 3. The Math That Matters: The Implied Q4 Ramp

This is the single most important number in the release, and it is not in any consensus table.

**Revenue bridge (company-reported actuals):**
- Q1 FY26: $2,125M · Q2 FY26: $2,336M → 1H = **$4,461M**
- Q3 guide ~$2,300M → 9M ≈ **$6,761M**
- FY guide "approximately $9.2B" → **implied Q4 ≈ $2,439M, or ~+5% reported** vs. Q4 FY25 (~$2,32xM)

Going from **flat reported in Q3 to ~+5% in Q4** requires a sharp inflection — even allowing for a partial-quarter Cornell/Roper contribution (~$65M/quarter, deal expected to close in Q4) and continued FX tailwind. Rounding slack in "approximately $9.2B" matters enormously here: a $9.15B landing implies Q4 of only ~$2,389M (+~2.8%). **Watch whether management narrows FY revenue to a point estimate or quietly leans on the low end.**

**EPS bridge:** 1H actual $2.58 (Q1 $1.12 + Q2 $1.46). FY guide $5.55–$5.70 → 2H = $2.97–$3.12. Less Q3 guide of $1.42–$1.47 → **implied Q4 adjusted EPS of ~$1.50–$1.70** — a steep step-up off a Q3 base of ~$1.45, with the top half of that range hard to reach organically.

**Cornell/Roper is roughly EPS-neutral in Q4, not a bailout.** ~$80M annualized EBITDA (>30% margin on >$260M revenue) ≈ $20M/quarter, against ~$20M/quarter of pre-tax interest on the new $1.5B of notes (5.25%/5.45%/5.85%) plus purchase-accounting amortization. Management's own framing — "accretive to adjusted EPS **in 2027**" — confirms the Q4 contribution is a wash at best.

## 4. Key Metrics to Watch (Ranked)

**1. Organic revenue growth and the FY revenue guide — the whole ballgame.**
Guide is ~+3% organic in Q3 after +0.0% in Q1 and +1.3% in Q2. This would be the first quarter of genuine organic acceleration all year. Reported growth will look ~3pts worse than organic because of the **international metrology/metering divestiture** (~$250M annual revenue, <10% EBITDA margin, closed around end of Q2 — it cost $31M of revenue in Q2 alone), partly offset by FX and bolt-ons (TriOS, WaterFleet). *Reconcile organic vs. reported carefully; do not read the reported figure as a miss without the bridge.*

**2. Orders and backlog — and normalize them.**
Q2 orders of $3.09B (+42% reported / +41% organic, 1.32x book-to-bill, backlog $5.3B) were **almost entirely one contract**: the $850M, 23-year Dow outsourced-water award, management's largest order ever. Ex-Dow, underlying Q2 order growth was only ~+3%. That backlog is long-duration and converts over decades, not quarters. **The Q3 question is whether core orders inflect to mid-single-digits without a mega-deal** — and whether the "robust pipeline" of similar 20-year outsourcing commitments has converted into anything new.

**3. Measurement & Control Solutions (MCS) — the problem child.**
Q2 revenue $508M, **‑1% organic**. Management cited electric-metering affordability concerns and utilities deferring capital spend "ahead of upcoming elections" (U.S. midterms are Nov 3, 2026 — one week after this print). Water-side orders grew double digits, partly offsetting. **Watch:** does electric stabilize, does the AMI 2.0 refresh cycle show up in orders, and does the post-divestiture margin profile step up materially now that the sub-10%-margin metrology business is gone?

**4. Margin durability as the 80/20 lever fades.**
Adj. EBITDA margin has expanded on price + productivity + mix. But management flagged that **"walkaway revenue" peaked in 2026 at nearly 2% of sales and will be "significantly lower" in 2027** — the 80/20 program is shifting from a margin lever to a growth enabler. Q3 margin at/above 24% with ~+3% organic would be a genuinely strong combination; margin beat *plus* revenue miss again would be read as more of the same and likely sell off.

**5. Data centers and the AI-adjacent water narrative — the only multiple support.**
Applied Water orders +9% with **data-center orders up >300%**; management projects data-center revenue up ~200% in 2026 to ~2% of total sales (~$185M) by year-end. CEO Matthew Pine has positioned water as "increasingly strategic in the AI ecosystem," extending to semis, power gen, and mining. Any sizing of the 2027 data-center opportunity is the most plausible re-rating catalyst in the deck.

**6. Segment detail:** Water Infrastructure ($683M, +3% org., **+480bps margin**, double-digit U.S. municipal vs. **China ‑40%**); Applied Water ($501M, +3% org.); Water Solutions & Services ($644M, +1% org.). Watch China stabilization and U.S. municipal durability.

**7. Balance sheet and capital allocation.** Pro forma total debt rises from $2.93B to **$4.43B** post-offering (vs. $10.4B equity); roughly ~2x gross / ~1.5x net on ~$2.2B of pro forma EBITDA. After repurchasing **$563M of stock in Q1** and buying back opportunistically in Q2, the key question with the stock near 52-week lows is whether the buyback is paused to fund the deal — or leaned into. Also confirm the Cornell/Roper closing date and any regulatory delay.

**8. New CFO's first call.** Andrea van der Berg became EVP & CFO effective Sept 1, 2026 (succeeding Bill Grogan, who left for GE HealthCare; announced Aug 13/18). She was previously SVP Finance of Water Infrastructure, with 13 years at Honeywell. Guidance was reaffirmed at the time of the transition. **Tone, specificity, and any reframing of the long-term algorithm will be scrutinized** — new CFOs are often suspected of resetting bars, and the market will read conservatism as a signal.

## 6. Catalyst Checklist (in order of stock impact)

1. **FY26 revenue guide — point estimate or hedge?** "Approximately $9.2B" implies a ~+5% reported Q4 off a flat Q3. Any softening to ~$9.1B confirms the growth bears and is the clearest path to a double-digit drawdown.
2. **Organic growth ≥3% vs. guide** — the first real acceleration of 2026. Below 2% organic is a thesis break for the "margin story is done, growth is next" bulls.
3. **Core orders ex-Dow / book-to-bill** — buy-side needs evidence the 42% Q2 headline wasn't a one-off. MSD+ underlying order growth with book-to-bill >1.0x is the bull trigger.
4. **MCS electric metering trajectory + AMI 2.0** — the specific line item most responsible for the derating. Post-divestiture MCS margin step-up is a partial offset worth watching.
5. **2027 bridge**: lower walkaway revenue (<2% drag), Cornell/Roper accretion sizing, data-center scaling, interest expense from the $1.5B notes. Street is at ~$6.20–$6.25 — any directional comment moves the stock more than the Q3 print itself.
6. **Capital allocation**: Cornell/Roper close timing, buyback posture near 52-week lows, and whether the ~$1B/year M&A cadence is reaffirmed or paused.
