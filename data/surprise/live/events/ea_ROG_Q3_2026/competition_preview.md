## 1. The Setup in One Paragraph

Rogers is a ~$850M-revenue engineered-materials company that just re-rated violently on a story, then gave most of it back. At the **September 30, 2026 Investor Day** management laid out a 2030 bridge of **>$1.5B revenue at 26% adjusted EBITDA margin and ~$14 adjusted EPS** (vs. $811M / 14.2% / $2.39 in 2025), with ~$450M of the $700M revenue gap coming from three adjacencies **led by AI data centers**. The stock jumped **+10.7% to $153** that day. It has since fallen to $127.72 — **down ~17% from the Investor Day close and ~24% from the June 22 high of $169.00** — on valuation compression (still ~77–80x trailing EPS) and no new news. ROG now sits below both its 50-DMA (~$136) and 200-DMA (~$137).

This print is therefore **not primarily about the quarter**. The quarter is well-telegraphed. It is about whether the Q3 margin step-up validates the 2028/2030 bridge, and whether management puts a date on the first AI data center design win they promised "within two quarters."

## 3. Key Metrics to Watch — Ranked

**1. AES segment gross margin (highest importance).**
Q2'26: Advanced Electronics Solutions did **$117.5M at 28.5% GM** vs. Elastomeric Material Solutions at **$94.8M at 36.9%**. AES is the dilutive half and the half exposed to **intense Asian competition in EV power substrates**. Consolidated GM of 33.2–34.2% is essentially unreachable unless AES moves toward 30%. *This is what broke the stock after Q2 — the market punished a quarter that beat on adjusted EPS because the margin trajectory disappointed. Watch this line first.*

**2. Any named AI data center design win, or a hard date.**
At Investor Day (Sept 30) management said first design wins would land **within two quarters**, with revenue starting **2H 2027**. Q3 is checkpoint one of two. A named win — or even "we have won, customer confidential" — is the highest-torque possible disclosure. Silence with no reaffirmed timeline is the single biggest bear trigger, because ~$450M of the $700M 2030 revenue bridge rests on adjacencies led by this.

**3. Q4'26 guidance vs. ~$0.97 implied / ~$240M.**
Q4 is seasonally softer (Q4'25: $201.5M, $0.89 adj EPS). The bar is low in absolute terms but the *direction* matters: after a $238M Q3, a Q4 guide that steps **down** to ~$215–225M reads as Q3 having been a pull-forward, not an inflection.

**4. Consolidated gross margin vs. 33.2% low end.**
Sensitivity: at $238M revenue, **each 100 bps of GM ≈ $0.10 of adjusted EPS**. Each $5M of revenue at ~34% incremental GM ≈ $0.07. So the $1.10–$1.30 EPS range is roughly ±1 GM point and ±$5M revenue combined — i.e., the guide range is narrow relative to how much GM can actually swing.

**5. Reaffirmation of the 2028 checkpoint ($1.05B revenue, $6 adj. EPS).**
Four weeks after setting it, any softening of the 2028 interim target would be read as the 2030 number being aspirational. Conversely, an explicit reaffirm is cheap and supportive.

**6. End-market detail — aerospace & defense and general industrial.**
Management specifically guided growth "across all major end markets, **led by aerospace and defense and general industrial**." A&D is the credible, backlog-driven piece; industrial is the cyclical piece. If the beat is A&D-led, it's higher quality than an EV/portable-electronics-led beat.

**7. FX and free cash flow.**
Q2 carried a **$5.3M currency tailwind** — ask how much of the Q3 YoY growth is FX versus volume. FCF was $18.3M in Q2 on $24.4M operating cash flow and $6.1M capex; FY26 capex guide is $30–35M. Balance sheet is a genuine asset: **~$181.4M cash plus $30.0M short-term investments, no meaningful debt.**

**What each requires operationally, and what management language signals it:**

- **Bull.** Needs the sequential volume step-up to be real and incremental margins near 40%. Listen for: *"utilization improved across our [specific] facilities," "we have been selected by a leading hyperscaler," "we are raising our view of the 2028 checkpoint."* The Investor Day reaction (+10.7% on a story alone) is the template — this stock pays up aggressively for data center validation, and the 24% drawdown since June has reset positioning.
- **Base.** The quarter lands on the guide, AES grinds slowly higher, data center stays a 2027 narrative. Listen for: *"on track," "pleased with progress," "first design wins expected in the coming quarters."* Because the stock has already given back the entire Investor Day move plus more, an in-line print with an intact narrative has **modest upward skew** — the derisking has largely happened.
- **Bear.** EV power substrate pricing is the named structural risk, and it sits inside the bigger of the two segments. Listen for: *"competitive pricing environment in power electronics," "timing of design wins," "customer qualification cycles are longer than anticipated,"* or any reference to restructuring execution. At ~33x FY26 consensus EPS, there is no valuation floor near current levels; the floor is the 200-DMA (~$137, already broken) and then the pre-Investor-Day base around $110–120.

**Historical calibration.** The surprise record is volatile and the *sign of the surprise has repeatedly failed to predict the stock reaction* — the clearest example being Q2'26, where adjusted EPS beat by ~16% ($0.92 vs. $0.79) and revenue beat by 2.8%, yet the dominant post-print narrative was margin disappointment.

## 5. Catalyst Checklist

1. **AES gross margin vs. ~28.5% in Q2** — the segment is 54% of sales at the lowest margin; consolidated guidance is unachievable without improvement here. This is the line the market traded on last quarter.
2. **Revenue vs. $238.0M (= guide midpoint, zero cushion)** — a $238M quarter is a 3-year high and needs +9.8% sequentially. Given the revenue-miss history, this is the fragile line.
3. **AI data center design win: named, dated, or silent** — checkpoint one of the two quarters management promised on Sept 30. The entire ~$450M adjacency bridge and the 77x multiple rest on this.
4. **Q4'26 guide vs. ~$240M / ~$0.97 implied by FY26 consensus of $3.83** — tests whether Q3 is an inflection or a pull-forward.
5. **2028 checkpoint reaffirm ($1.05B / $6 adj. EPS)** — set four weeks ago; any hedging is disproportionately damaging this soon after.
6. **Opex trajectory** — since only ~20 bps of the 250 bps YoY EBITDA margin expansion is gross margin, SG&A/R&D leverage is doing the work. Confirm it's structural (restructuring, factory cost) rather than timing.

## 7. Data Quality Caveats — Read Before Trading This

Aggregator data on ROG is unusually noisy this cycle. Three specific conflicts I could not fully reconcile:

1. **Q2'26 one-day reaction is reported three different ways.** Sources variously claim **-7.8%** (margin concerns), **+5.18%**, and "mixed." The +5.18% figure appears in a Yahoo/Simply Wall St piece dated **Aug 11** — two weeks after the July 28 print — and the same +5.18% number is separately attributed to the **Q1** reaction by another outlet, so it is likely misattributed. Verify the actual July 29 close before relying on any claimed Q2 reaction.
2. **Q2'26 EPS beat vs. miss.** Investing.com shows **$0.92 vs. $0.79 (+16%)** on an adjusted basis; MarketBeat says ROG **"missed EPS consensus by $0.07."** The reconciliation is almost certainly **adjusted ($0.92) vs. GAAP ($0.76)** — the company reported both. StockStory's "significant miss of analysts' EPS estimates" is likely the GAAP comparison. **Adjusted is the right basis for the Q3 guide ($1.10–$1.30) and for consensus.**
3. **Consensus price target ranges from $173 to $225** across sources. The high end is stale: **B. Riley raised to $225 on Oct 1, then cut back to $195 on Oct 22** while keeping Buy. MarketBeat's "$225 consensus" (Oct 22) looks like it captured the pre-cut number. The defensible range is **$173–195**, implying ~35–53% upside — but on only 3–7 covering analysts.

Separately, one GuruFocus item referenced "mixed Q3 earnings" as a reason for the October decline. **Q3 has not reported** — that is a conflation and I have excluded it. The October drawdown is best explained by multiple compression and the B. Riley target cut, not by any company disclosure.
