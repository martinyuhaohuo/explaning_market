## 1. The setup in one paragraph

This is **not primarily an EPS print — it's a guidance and capital-plan event.** CNP's Q3 release is historically where management initiates the following year's EPS guidance and unveils the refreshed 10-year capital plan: at Q3 2025 (reported Oct 2025) the company raised 2025 guidance, **initiated** 2026 guidance of $1.89–$1.91, and announced a record **$65B** 10-year plan in the same release. Expect the same architecture on Oct 27: **initial 2027 guidance plus a roll-forward of the capital plan to 2027–2036**. The quarter itself should be fine-to-good — summer 2026 was the hottest contiguous-U.S. meteorological summer on record and ERCOT set an all-time peak of **91,134 MW on July 22, 2026** (vs. 85,464 MW in 2023) — so the risk isn't the Q3 number. The risk is that **sell-side 2027 consensus of $2.08 sits at or slightly above the arithmetic top of management's own framework**, and that the Batch Zero load pipeline converts more slowly than the narrative implies.

## 3. Key metrics to watch — ranked

**1. Initial FY2027 non-GAAP EPS guidance (highest importance by a wide margin).**
Management has guided to the **"mid-to-high end of 7–9% annual growth through 2028."** Applied to the $1.90 FY26 midpoint: 8% = $2.05, 9% = $2.07. **Street is at $2.08.** A guide of $2.04–$2.06 is a mechanical "miss" versus consensus even though it is fully consistent with the stated algorithm. Watch for the trademark **"at least the midpoint"** qualifier — its presence is worth roughly a penny of credibility; its absence is a negative signal.

**2. 10-year capital plan roll-forward (2027–2036).**
Current plan: **$66.7B for 2026–2035**, raised $1.2B at Q2 on large-load demand and the Downtown Houston Revitalization project — notably **without increasing equity financing guidance**. Truist expects management to unveil a **second-half transmission study covering 2028–2032** that adds "meaningful investment" against existing commentary of **"at least $10B of upside capex."** A roll-forward to **$70B+** with no new equity is the most bullish outcome available.

**3. Batch Zero / large-load conversion.**
Submitted **>17 GW**; **~14 GW** expected eligible (**~10 GW** base load with all ERCOT studies approved, **~4 GW** studied load, **3 GW** still pending study approval). That 14 GW is a **>65% increase on the current ~21 GW Houston peak**, backed by signed facility extension agreements and **~$900M of customer cash deposits and commitments**, with nearly all projects energized **by end-2030** and ~50% load growth by end-2029. Demand charges run roughly **$6M per GW per month** and — critically — **are not yet in the financial forecast.** Watch: base-load designations confirmed, the 3 GW pending bucket, any first-time inclusion of demand-charge revenue in guidance, and the +2 GW distribution-level pipeline. Context: ERCOT is evaluating **>200 GW** of large-load requests, two-thirds from data centers.

**4. Financing and credit.**
FFO/debt was **13.4%** at Q2 with **Moody's still on negative outlook**. The Ohio sale delivers **$1.42B of cash in 2026** and **$1.20B in 2027 via seller note**, at ~1.9x 2024 Ohio rate base (~5,900 miles of pipeline, ~335,000 customers). The standing bear case is equity dilution funding a $66B+ plan; any ATM upsizing or equity block alongside a bigger capex number flips this from tailwind to headwind.

**5. Weather flow-through and O&M/interest drag.**
Q2 carried **-$0.01 from unfavorable weather and -$0.01 from higher interest**. Q3 should swing the other way: August 2026 was the second-hottest August on record for ERCOT and delivered the **highest monthly energy ERCOT has ever supplied**, with daily peaks beating the 2023 record on **21 consecutive days (Aug 11–31)** and load above the 2023 record in 187 hours across 34 days. Houston Electric residential/commercial delivery revenue is volumetric and unhedged for weather, so Zacks' modeled **+1.5%** throughput looks conservative. If throughput prints +3% or better and EPS only matches, the market will read it as an underlying O&M/interest miss.

**6. Regulatory.** Houston Electric incremental revenue requirement of **~$73M expected November 2026**; Texas gas **+$62M** already effective June 2026; active DCRF (Dockets 59424/59981) and TCOS filings. The settled four-year Houston Electric rate case runs **~$50M/year lower revenue through 2028**, and the **>$3B 2026–2028 Systemwide Resiliency Plan** settlement — the largest grid-resiliency investment in company history, pending PUCT approval — is the recovery vehicle to track.

## 5. Catalyst checklist

1. **Initial FY2027 EPS guidance vs. $2.08 consensus.** The whisper is effectively "$2.08 with upside"; the algorithm says $2.05–$2.07. Most likely source of a negative surprise in an otherwise constructive print — and it would be a *framing* miss, not a fundamental one, which sets up a buyable dip.
2. **Capital plan roll-forward to 2027–2036 and the 2H transmission study.** Buy-side wants $70B+ and a credible path to the "$10B+ upside capex." Silence or deferral is a de-rating risk given the premium rests on rate-base growth.
3. **Equity financing commentary.** Repetition of "no additional equity needed," helped by $1.42B of Ohio proceeds, is worth more than a 2-cent EPS beat. New equity is the clearest downside trigger.
4. **Batch Zero conversion milestones** — base-load designations, the 3 GW pending ERCOT studies, deposits above $900M, and whether demand charges enter guidance for the first time.
5. **Narrative/structural** — post-close capital allocation for the Ohio divestiture, Houston Electric's ~$73M November filing, and SB 6 cost-allocation mechanics (75 MW+ large loads bear interconnection and reliability obligations), a regime that is a genuine structural positive for CNP and underappreciated versus the data-center hype. Also note the forecast **>$5B of residential/commercial delivery-charge savings** over the decade as large loads absorb system costs — the political cover that makes the capex plan durable.
