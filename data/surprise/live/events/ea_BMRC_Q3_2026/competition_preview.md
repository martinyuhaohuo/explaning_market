## 1. Setup in One Paragraph

BMRC is three quarters into a credible post-restructuring earnings recovery. The Q4 2025 repositioning — selling $593M of low-yielding AFS securities for a $69.5M pre-tax loss, funded by a $45M 6.75% sub-debt issue — reset the asset yield and produced two clean quarters: $0.53 in Q1 and $0.58 in Q2, with NIM up 14bp sequentially to 3.38% and a June exit rate management pegged at 3.44–3.45%. The market has only partly re-rated it: trailing GAAP EPS is still **–$0.90** because the Q4 2025 loss doesn't roll off until the January 2027 print, so the stock screens as "unprofitable" and remains under-owned by quant/screen money. The Q3 print is a continuation test, not a turnaround test. **The key tension: consensus wants revenue up ~3% sequentially but EPS flat — a combination that requires NIM expansion the balance sheet may not support.**

## 3. Key Metrics to Watch — Ranked

**1. Net interest margin vs. the 3.44–3.45% June exit rate.** This is the entire story. Q2's 3.38% was flattered by one-way deposit sales that shrank the denominator. Management guided to ~20bp of loan-yield benefit over the next 12 months (~5bp/quarter) from repricing. **Below 3.40% = thesis damage. Above 3.48% = re-rating.**

**2. Earning-asset trajectory.** Loans $2.101B (–$14.7M QoQ), deposits $3.370B (–$58.2M QoQ). NIM has been expanding partly *because* the balance sheet is shrinking — a quality-of-earnings question. Q2 originations were $98M in commitments ($63M funded), +22–23% QoQ, at a 6.53% weighted-average rate above the payoff rate, with YTD commitments near double the prior year. Q3 is when that pipeline should convert. Flat-to-up loans with NIM intact is the clean bull outcome.

**3. Deposit costs and the NIB mix.** Cost of deposits 1.28% and falling (–7bp QoQ); noninterest-bearing at **36.7%** of total is the franchise's core asset. Management said there's "no real upward pressure" on pricing — but the Fed *hiked* 25bp on Sept 16 to 3.75–4.00%, the first hike since July 2023. Watch whether that claim survives contact with a tightening cycle.

**4. Credit quality.** Nonaccruals $8.5M (0.40% of loans), classified $19.9M (0.95%), ACL 1.07%, net charge-offs just $39k. Non-owner-occupied CRE is ~65% of loans, office ~26% of that — but San Francisco office is only ~2% of total loans at 62% LTV and 1.72x DSCR, and 88% of loans carry owner guarantees. The bar here is simply "no new migration."

**5. Capital, buyback, and M&A.** TCE 8.52% (+19bp), total risk-based 15.58%, TBVPS $19.92 (+$0.15), Tier 1 leverage 8.66%. $23.9M of buyback authorization is unused and management said repurchases are "not imminent" due to capital restrictions from the restructuring losses. Any change in that language is a real signal. M&A remains stated as the priority over "episodic buybacks."

## 4. Scenario Analysis

| Scenario | Revenue | EPS | Key Driver | Stock Reaction |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Bull** (25%) | $35.3–35.8M | $0.62–0.65 | NIM ≥3.50%, loans grow $30M+, NIE ≤$21.8M, another provision reversal | **+6% to +10%** |
| **Base** (50%) | $34.2–34.6M | $0.55–0.58 | NIM 3.42–3.46%, loans flattish, NIE ~$22.0M, small provision build | **–2% to +3%** |
| **Bear** (25%) | <$33.8M | $0.43–0.50 | NIM flat/down, deposits keep running off, NIE >$22.5M, a classified credit migrates | **–8% to –12%** |

**Bull — what must happen:** Deposit outflows stop, the near-doubled pipeline funds, and the Sept Fed hike shows up in asset yields faster than deposit betas. Management language to listen for: a Q4 NIM guide with a 3.5-handle, and any softening of the "buybacks not imminent" stance.

**Base — what must happen:** Essentially a repeat of Q2 with modest margin carry-through and the guided expense step-up. This produces an **EPS in line but revenue optically short of $35.11M** — the most likely literal outcome, and one that headline-scraping algorithms may read as a miss before the quality of the NIM print is understood. Expect intraday volatility that reverses.

**Bear — what must happen:** The one-way deposit sales that helped Q2's mix turn into genuine funding pressure, or office credit cracks. A provision above ~$1.5M would be the tell. Note the restructuring thesis is *not* broken by one soft quarter, but at 1.36x tangible book the stock no longer has deep-value support.

**Historical calibration:** BMRC's earnings reactions are large and directionally unreliable. Q2 2026: beat, **+17.4%** pre-market. Q4 2024: beat, **–3.0%**. Q3 2024: beat, **+1.0%**. Q2 2024: miss, **+9.0%**. With ~149k shares of average daily volume on a $439M cap, gap risk is structural rather than informational.

## 5. Catalyst Checklist

1. **NIM vs. 3.44–3.45%** — the June exit rate is the real benchmark, not the 3.38% reported Q2 average. Everything in the FY2027 $2.42 number keys off this.
2. **Revenue vs. $35.11M** — the most likely source of a bad headline. Judge the composition, not the number: a miss driven by a smaller balance sheet at a higher margin is benign; a miss driven by NIM is not.
3. **Q4 NIM commentary post-Fed hike** — BMRC reports Monday; the FOMC decides Wednesday, Oct 28 (market-implied ~86–92% hold). Management will have to frame rate sensitivity 48 hours ahead of a live meeting, and with 36.7% NIB deposits a restarting hike cycle is a tailwind they may be reluctant to claim.
4. **Loan growth inflection** — three quarters of shrinking earning assets against a pipeline management says is nearly double last year. If it doesn't fund in Q3, the growth story slips to 2027.
5. **Buyback language / M&A** — $23.9M authorized, TCE 8.52% and building. Removal of the restructuring-related capital constraint is an under-priced catalyst at 1.36x TBV.

### Bottom Line

The asymmetry is better than the Hold consensus implies, but it is not free. At 11.9x forward and 1.36x tangible book with a 3.7% yield, BMRC is priced for the recovery to continue, not to accelerate. The single most likely outcome is an in-line EPS print paired with a revenue number that reads light against $35.11M — which creates a tradable dislocation if the NIM lands at or above 3.44%. The real risk is that NIM expansion has been borrowed from a shrinking balance sheet, and Q3 is the quarter where that bill either comes due or gets refinanced by loan growth.
