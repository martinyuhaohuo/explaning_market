## 2. Key Metrics to Watch (ranked)

**1. FTE NIM and the Q4 trajectory — the whole thesis.** Q2 printed **4.43% FTE** (+13bp YoY), an outlier vs. regional peers (Regions 3.68%, BankUnited 3.16%). Management guided to stability-to-expansion on back-book repricing: **$1.3B of loans reprice in H2'26 at a 5.8% yield**. But CFO Ciroli explicitly flagged *intermediate-curve* sensitivity — sharp rate increases compress spreads. The Fed **hiked 25bp on Sept 16, 2026 to 3.75–4.00%** (first hike since 2023) with another likely in December. That is the opposite of the "stable-to-slightly-down" rate path Ciroli said would help spreads. Watch whether deposit cost (1.10%, 36% noninterest-bearing) stays anchored.

**2. Loan growth — the persistent soft spot.** Average loans $11.6B, only **+1.1% YoY**; +3.9% annualized QoQ, or 6.5% ex-deemphasized consumer. Period-end balances exited above average, so Q3 average should carry momentum. Need ≥5–6% annualized ex-consumer plus commercial pipeline confirmation. CRE is 41% of the book ($4.8B).

**3. Excess capital deployment — where the valuation argument is settled.** CET1 **28.6%** vs. a 13.5% long-term target = **$1.9B / $7.98 per share** of excess. The company repurchased only **$7.6M (280K shares)** in Q2 against a fresh **$100M** authorization. Management's own framework: deploying at a 13% gross ROIC supports ~1.8x TBVPS. At 2.37x P/TBV the stock *already* prices more than full deployment — so buyback pace and M&A language are the swing factor, not a nice-to-have.

**4. Fee income / wealth.** Noninterest income $69.6M (+38.9% YoY); wealth $23.2M (+20%) with **AUA $17.3B vs. $14.2B** YoY. Fee ratio 24.6%. Equity markets were supportive into September, so AUA-driven revenue should build. Watch early traction in the new private bank (CEO Ross: "too early to really call the success").

**5. Credit — watch the drift, not the level.** NCOs steady at 10bp, delinquencies 22bp, ACL 1.29% of loans. But NPLs rose 45bp → **49bp** QoQ (NPAs $54.8M → $60.2M). Management called the Q2 commercial downgrade borrower-specific; a second quarter of migration undercuts that.

**Operationally, what each requires:** The bull case needs the $1.3B at 5.8% to land without a matching deposit repricing — i.e., the 36% NIB base holds through a hiking cycle. The bear case is the one management handed us: Ciroli said rising intermediate rates squeeze spreads, and loan pricing competition is "intense" for the best credits. A flat NIM plus a third quarter of ~1% YoY loan growth makes 2.37x TBV hard to defend.

**Management commentary to listen for:** explicit Q4 NIM framing (peers gave point guides — Regions ~3.70%, BankUnited ~3.20%; CBC has not guided quantitatively); any shift from "opportunistic" to "programmatic" on the buyback; a change in M&A tone from valuation discipline toward active pipeline.

**Historical print reactions (all three as a public company):**
- Q4'25 (Jan 27, 2026) — EPS $0.47 vs. $0.46 est, revenue $272.2M. Beat.
- Q1'26 (Apr 28, 2026) — EPS $0.46 vs. ~$0.43–0.45, revenue $273.7M vs. $270.7M. Beat; stock ~flat (–0.04% pre-market).

## 4. Catalyst Checklist

1. **FTE NIM vs. ~4.45% whisper** — the only line that justifies the multiple. Below 4.40% breaks the expansion narrative just as the Fed turns hawkish.
2. **Average loans ex-other-consumer, annualized** — buy-side wants ≥6%. Sub-3% revives the "great margin, no growth" critique that has capped the sell-side.
3. **Q3 buyback dollars + Q4 capital intent** — $7.6M of a $100M authorization was not a deployment signal. With the stock at 2.37x TBV, management may reasonably *not* buy back, which then puts all the weight on M&A.
4. **NPL/NPA direction and single-name color** — confirmation that the Q2 downgrade was idiosyncratic.
5. **FOMC, Oct 28, 2:00 p.m. ET** — a hold is ~91% priced, with a December hike expected. CBC's print will be read through a rates lens for a full day before the Fed speaks; the +100bp static disclosure is ~$6.5M incremental quarterly NII.

Sizing, not direction, is the risk here.

**Bottom line:** Base case in line, muted reaction. The trade is not the EPS line — it is whether management converts $7.98/share of idle capital into a deployment story, because the current multiple already assumes they will.
