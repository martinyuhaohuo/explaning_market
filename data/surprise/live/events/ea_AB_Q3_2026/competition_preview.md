## 1. Setup in One Paragraph

AB goes into this print at a **52-week low** ($33.91 low; -20% from the $44.11 high) on a **~9.6% distribution yield** and **~10x forward earnings** — a valuation that says the market no longer believes AUM growth converts into fee growth. The evidence supports that worry: average AUM should be up ~8% year-over-year, but base fees likely up only ~3%, because the effective base fee rate has compressed from **40.6 bps in Q3'25 to an estimated ~38.7 bps**. Meanwhile the two genuinely large positives are *not* in this quarter's numbers: the **Corebridge/Equitable merger** (closing targeted by year-end 2026) which is slated to migrate **$100bn+** of general and separate account assets to AB over time, and the **CEO transition** to Onur Erzan on 1 April 2027. This is a quarter where the P&L is probably fine-to-dull and the **commentary is the trade**.

## 3. My Estimate Build (Base Case)

**Average AUM.** Monthly disclosures give: Q2 exit $905.5bn → Jul 31 **$909bn** → Aug 31 **$919bn** → Sep 30 **$900bn**. Midpoint-averaging the months → **~$910bn average AUM**, +3.3% QoQ and **+8.2% YoY** (vs $840.8bn in Q3'25).

**Effective base fee rate** (base fees × 4 ÷ average AUM — my calculation from SEC 8-K figures):

| Quarter | Base fees | Avg AUM | Implied rate |
|---|---|---|---|
| Q3'25 | $852.5M | $840.8bn | 40.6 bps |
| Q1'26 | $849.2M | $865.0bn | 39.3 bps |
| Q2'26 | $860.3M | $881.2bn | 39.1 bps |
| **Q3'26E** | **$880M** | **~$910bn** | **~38.7 bps** |

That ~1.9 bps of YoY compression is worth roughly **$40M of annualised base fees** — it is the entire reason the stock is at a low.

**Performance fees — the quiet problem.** Management raised FY26 performance-fee guidance to **$115–135M** at Q2. First-half actuals were already **$112.6M** ($66.0M + $46.6M). The guide therefore implies only **$2–22M across the whole of 2H26**. I model **$10M** for Q3 (vs $22.0M in Q3'25). Anything above ~$20M is a positive surprise that almost certainly comes with a guidance raise.

**Other revenue** (seed/investment results, net interest) has been a persistent negative in 1H26 (-$44.1M in Q1, -$19.4M in Q2, vs **+$10.2M** in Q3'25). I model **-$5M**.

**Expenses.** Compensation ratio held at **48.5%** of adjusted net revenues. Non-compensation guided to **$620–640M** FY26, lowered at Q2; 1H26 ran ~$322M, implying ~$155M/quarter in 2H. ABLP tax rate guided down to **5–6%**.

**Headline flows are already public and they flatter the quarter.** AB pre-announced **~+$6.0bn preliminary firmwide net inflows** for Q3 on 12 October. But July included **~$12bn of commercial mortgage loans onboarded** from the Equitable private-markets relationship. Strip that one-time institutional onboarding and underlying organic flows were roughly **-$6bn**, consistent with "net outflows" language in both the August and September monthly releases (retail and private wealth both negative in September).

## 4. Key Metrics to Watch — Ranked

1. **Base fee dollars and the effective fee rate (~38.7 bps est.).** Everything else is noise. A rate at or above 39 bps breaks the compression narrative; below 38.5 bps confirms it.
2. **Flow composition ex-the-$12bn-onboarding, and active equity specifically.** Active equity bled **-$10.6bn** in Q2, driven by retail redemptions concentrated in APAC where allocators are rotating to local markets. Is that decelerating or not?
3. **Investment performance.** Only **23% of equity AUM** outperformed over the trailing year at Q2 — management attributed it to narrow AI-led leadership penalising their quality bias. This is the leading indicator for 2027 flows; a 1-year number still in the 20s means retail outflows persist regardless of markets.
4. **Corebridge onboarding mechanics.** Merger approved by both shareholder bases 30 July 2026, targeted close by year-end 2026, with **$100bn+** of Corebridge assets earmarked for AB over time. The market needs three things: *timing, phasing, and fee rate*. At general-account fee rates this is dilutive to the blended rate but highly accretive to dollars; vagueness here is worth more downside than any P&L line.
5. **Performance fee and expense guidance.** FY26 performance fees are effectively spoken for; watch for a FY26 raise and any first framing of FY27. Non-comp guide at/below $620–640M and the comp ratio holding at 48.5% support the margin.
6. **Private markets AUM.** $91bn at Q2, already through the low end of the $90–100bn 2027 target more than a year early; >$100bn including the July CMLs. A target reset upward would be a genuine positive.
7. **CEO transition commentary.** Announced ~25 September 2026: Erzan (President since Jan 2026, architect of the private-alts/private-wealth/insurance pivot) takes over 1 April 2027; Bernstein retires 31 March 2027 and stays on the Board. One of Bernstein's last calls — expect succession and strategy questions.

## 5. Scenario Analysis

| Scenario | Adj. Net Revenue | Base Fees / Fee Rate | Adj. EPU | Margin | Key Driver | Expected Reaction |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Bull** (~25%) | ~$922M | $887M / 39.0 bps | **~$0.91** | ~34.9% | Performance fees ~$25M + FY26 guide raised; other income swings positive; fee rate stabilises; Corebridge onboarding dated and sized on the call | **+5% to +9%** |
| **Base** (~50%) | ~$885M | $880M / 38.7 bps | **~$0.85** | ~34.0% | In-line EPU, flat YoY revenue, +$6bn flows flattered by CML onboarding, fee rate drifts lower, Corebridge "on track, by year-end" with no detail | **-2% to +2%** |
| **Bear** (~25%) | ~$851M | $873M / 38.4 bps | **~$0.79** | ~32.7% | Performance fees <$5M, negative seed/other income, active equity outflows re-accelerate past -$11bn, fee rate steps down on passive/indexed mix, Corebridge timing slips into 2027 | **-6% to -11%** |

**What each would require operationally:**

- **Bull** needs the private-markets book to mark well enough to crystallise fees management had already guided away from, *plus* a credible Corebridge timetable. The margin does the work: at 48.5% comp, every incremental revenue dollar drops ~51 cents to operating income.
- **Base** is simply the quarter the monthly AUM releases already told you about. The +$6bn flow headline and the $900bn exit AUM are known; there is little left to surprise on except fees and guidance.
- **Bear** is really a *fee-rate and mix* story, not an AUM story. Q2's flow mix — passive fixed income **+$8.9bn**, alternatives/multi-asset **+$4.2bn**, active equity **-$10.6bn** — is structurally rate-dilutive. A repeat makes the de-rating look justified rather than overdone.

## 7. Catalyst Checklist — The Five That Decide the Print

1. **Base fees vs ~$880M and fee rate vs ~38.7 bps.** The only line where a surprise changes the thesis. Mix shift is the bear case made concrete.
2. **Active equity flows vs -$10.6bn in Q2, and the APAC retail trend.** Determines whether +$6bn headline flows are a turn or a one-off.
3. **FY26 performance fee guidance.** 1H26 already delivered $112.6M of a $115–135M guide. A raise = bull case; silence = the 2H is empty, as guided.
4. **Corebridge: close date, phasing, and fee rate on the $100bn+.** Highest-variance item on the call. Vagueness here costs more than any missed line item.
5. **Margin and FY27 framing from Erzan.** Comp ratio at 48.5%, non-comp at/below $620–640M, operating margin above the midpoint of the 30–35% target set for 2027. Early FY27 commentary from the incoming CEO is the first read on whether the private-markets pivot offsets fee-rate decay.

### Methodology & Caveats

- Financial actuals are taken from AB's SEC Form 8-K earnings releases (Q3'25, Q1'26, Q2'26) and monthly AUM 8-Ks (Jul/Aug/Sep 2026). Where secondary sources conflicted with the filings on base fees and fee rate (one quoted $829M base fees and a 37.7 bps "firmwide fee rate" for Q2'26 against the filed $860.3M), **I used the filed figures** and computed fee rates myself as base fees × 4 ÷ average AUM. That definition may differ from AB's own disclosed fee-rate metric, so compare like-for-like against the company's table on the day.
- Average AUM for Q3'26 (~$910bn) is my estimate from month-end disclosures, not a company figure; AB computes it from daily averages, so expect a variance of a few billion.
- Consensus figures are third-party aggregations dated ~20 October 2026 and will have drifted. Refresh before the print.
- Scenario probabilities are judgement, not model output. No options-implied move was obtainable from public sources.
