## 2. Key Metrics to Watch — Ranked

**1. Core revenue (ex-Safe Harbor) — the single most important line.**
Q2'26 core was ~$207.6M ($291.9M less $84.3M Safe Harbor). Q3 guided core is **~$230M, +10% q/q**. Safe Harbor is contracted, lumpy, and non-recurring — it flatters the headline and tells you nothing about end demand. If headline is $305M but Safe Harbor came in at $90M, core *missed* at $215M. **Do the subtraction before reacting to the headline.**

**2. Q4 guidance vs. ~$300M consensus.**
Q3 includes ~$75M Safe Harbor, stepping down from $84.3M. The 5% ITC-method bucket is only $202M of the ~$1.1B signed YTD; the **$878.6M Physical Work Test tranche does not begin recognizing revenue until 2028.** So the Safe Harbor tailwind thins out from here. For Q4 to hit $300M, core has to carry roughly $240M+ — i.e. continued double-digit sequential core growth into a seasonally softer US quarter. This is where the guide is most likely to disappoint.

**3. US sell-through and channel inventory.**
US sell-through fell **−34% y/y** in Q2 as the 25D residential credit expiration bit. Management guided to ~$15M of deliberate **undershipment** in Q3 to protect channel inventory. Watch: does undershipment widen (demand worse than modeled) or close (stabilization)? Permits and upstream sales were running ~30% below prior-year levels as of June.

**4. Battery MWh and attach rate.**
Guide 130–150 MWh. Storage is the structural offset to lost solar-only demand — post-25D, the economics case is storage-led. A sub-130 print would be a genuine negative surprise; 150+ with the Gen-5 battery on schedule for late Q4 (50% higher energy density, ~40% lower $/kWh) is the bull's best evidence.

**5. Propel (TPO) origination run-rate.**
Exit Q2: 6 states, ~200 originations/week, ~75% battery attach. Targets: 12 states by end of Q3, **500/week by year-end**. Mizuho's downgrade explicitly cited limited visibility here. Missing the state count or the run-rate undercuts the entire "we're taking matters into our own hands" post-ITC recovery thesis.

**6. Gross margin bridge and 45X.**
Non-GAAP GM 44–47% guided, including ~2pts of reciprocal tariff (improved from 5pts). ~$193.5M of 45X PTCs sat on the balance sheet YTD. Watch the GAAP/non-GAAP spread — Q2's 60.0% GAAP GM was flattered by a $45.4M tariff refund that will not repeat.

**7. Free cash flow and cash.** Q2 FCF $25.9M; cash and securities $937.7M. Safe Harbor deals can consume working capital ahead of recognition.

## 4. Catalyst Checklist

1. **Core revenue ex-Safe Harbor vs. ~$230M guided** — strips out the contracted, non-recurring revenue and is the only clean read on post-25D end demand. The whole debate is whether residential solar has found a floor.
2. **Q4 revenue guide vs. ~$300M consensus** — with Safe Harbor stepping down and $878.6M of it deferred to 2028, the math requires core to accelerate again. A sub-$280M midpoint confirms the bear case that 2026 consensus was never achievable.
3. **Battery MWh vs. 130–150 MWh and Gen-5 timing** — storage attach is the structural offset; a slip in the late-Q4 Gen-5 ship date removes the 2027 cost-curve argument.
4. **Propel origination run-rate vs. the 500/week year-end target** — the named reason for the Mizuho downgrade. This is management's self-help answer to the ITC loss.
5. **SST / AI data center substance** — Enphase demonstrated 13.8kV AC→800VDC conversion **today, Oct 27** (132 power modules, 44 in series per phase), with 34.5kV "in coming weeks," MW-scale racks by end-2026, pilots 2027, commercial shipments 2028. Infineon 900V GaN bidirectional switches announced Oct 26. **This is why the stock is +4% into the print.** The risk is asymmetric: the narrative is partly pre-priced, revenue is three years out, and there is no disclosed TAM, pricing, or customer. Management converting this into a dated, counterparty-backed commitment is the biggest upside lever; a vague reiteration deflates a rally that already happened.
