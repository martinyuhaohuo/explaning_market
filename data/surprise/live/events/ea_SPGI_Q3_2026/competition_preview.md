## ⚠️ Read This First: The Comp Base Is Broken

**This is the single most important thing about tomorrow's print.** S&P Global completed the spin-off of Mobility Global (NYSE: MBGL) on **July 1, 2026**, and Mobility is now treated as discontinued operations *for all of 2026*. The company simultaneously **recast** its segments: 451 Research and Maritime & Trade moved from Market Intelligence into Energy, and corporate/shared-service cost allocation shifted to a consumption basis.

Most headline framing has not caught up. The widely quoted "revenue -6.5% YoY, EPS -6.6% YoY" compares Q3 FY26 estimates against **as-reported Q3 2025, which included Mobility**. On the correct recast basis:

| Q3 2025 basis | Revenue | Adj. EPS |
|---|---|---|
| **As reported** (incl. Mobility) | $3,888M | $4.73 |
| Less Mobility | ($445M) | (~$0.49) *derived* |
| **Recast / continuing ops** | **$3,443M** *(official)* | **~$4.24** *(derived)* |

**Therefore consensus of $3.64B / $4.42 is roughly +5.7% revenue and +4.3% EPS growth — not a decline.** Same artifact at the FY level: FY25 pro forma adj. EPS was **$15.85**, so FY26 guidance of $17.50–$17.75 is **+10.4% to +12.0%** growth, consistent with management's "double-digit" characterization — *not* the "flat vs. $17.83" or "guidance cut 9.7%" that screens and several outlets have reported.

**This already cost shareholders once.** On the Q2 print (Jul 28), adj. EPS of $4.83 *beat* the clean Zacks consensus of $4.49 and the settled consensus of $4.81, but *missed* stale Mobility-inclusive estimates of $5.02. Headlines read "profit miss" (several compared GAAP $4.08 to an adjusted $4.75 consensus). The stock opened **-4.8%**, closed **-3.5%**, and traded **-7.7%** intraday. Expect data providers to be cleaner this quarter — but not uniformly so.

## Key Metrics to Watch (ranked)

1. **Market Intelligence organic growth and ACV/renewals** — the multiple's swing factor. Q2 was +6% with management conceding "longer renewal cycles with larger, more sophisticated clients" amid AI and IP-protection negotiations. Newly reorganized under new leadership into *Kensho Data & Platforms* and *Enterprise Solutions*. ≥7% organic with stable ACV re-rates the stock; <5% confirms the bear case that AI is a demand *risk*, not just an opportunity.
2. **Ratings billed issuance and Q4 tone** — Q2 billed issuance was **$1,268B, +25% YoY** (IG $560B +27%, HY $169B +15%, structured $539B +25%). FY26 assumes mid-to-high-single-digit billed issuance growth and **$250–300B of hyperscaler infrastructure issuance**. Q3 2026 was a two-sided quarter: hyperscalers issued $200B+ of long-term bonds in 2026, but a September rate shock pushed IG OAS +8bp to 84 and HY +36bp to 308, with the Morningstar US Core Bond index -3.4% (worst quarter since Q3 2022). Watch for a late-quarter air pocket in opportunistic and HY supply.
3. **FY26 guidance action** — $17.50–$17.75 implies a brutal H2 deceleration (see math below). Any narrowing to the top half or a raise is the cleanest bull catalyst.
4. **Energy inflection** — only +2–3% in Q2 on Middle East/Iran-related headwinds that management called "transitory," with a return to 6–8% expected in 2027. Consensus of +0.2% against the recast base is a low bar, but it is also the segment most likely to miss the FY guide.
5. **Indices durability** — 13 consecutive record-revenue quarters, >$2B TTM, ETF AUM $6.35T, $600B+ net inflows YTD. Highest-margin division (~70%). Mostly a beat-and-move-on line, but a decelerating asset-linked fee line would hurt disproportionately.
6. **Margin and capital return** — adjusted operating margin expansion guided 35–60bp as reported (management cited 75–100bp *excluding OSTTRA*, sold to KKR Oct 2025); buyback raised to **>$7B** (~5% of market cap) with only $1.5B done at H1 — implying a heavy H2 pace that supports EPS.

## The Guidance Math (why I think consensus is low)

Working on the continuing-ops basis:

| Period | Adj. EPS | YoY |
|---|---|---|
| Q1 2026 recast | ~$4.48 *(derived from $4.97 reported less ~$0.49 Mobility)* | ~+13% |
| Q2 2026 pro forma | **$4.83** *(official)* | +23% |
| **H1 2026** | **~$9.31** | **~+18%** |
| FY26 guide | $17.50–$17.75 | +10–12% |
| **Implied H2 2026** | **$8.19–$8.44** | **+2.8% to +5.9%** |

Guidance embeds a collapse from +18% in H1 to +3–6% in H2. Some of that is real — tough comps (recast Q3/Q4 2025 were the strongest quarters of 2025), the loss of OSTTRA, the EDM/thinkFolio divestiture, and soft Energy. But a decline of that magnitude is hard to square with +25% billed issuance momentum, record Indices, and a $7B buyback with $5.5B still to execute. **Consensus of $4.42 looks beatable; I'd center Q3 at $4.50–$4.62.** Caveat: the Q1 recast EPS above is my derivation, not a company-disclosed figure, so the H2 bridge carries ±$0.05–0.10 of uncertainty.

| **Bull** (25%) | $3.78B+ (+9.8%) | $4.70+ | Ratings >$1.35B on hyperscaler + M&A issuance; MI organic ≥7% with clean renewals; guide raised to/above $17.75 | **+4% to +7%** |
| **Base** (50%) | $3.68–3.72B (+7–8%) | $4.50–4.62 | Ratings and Indices carry the quarter; MI ~6%; Energy soft but framed as transitory; guide narrowed to upper half | **+1% to +3%** |
| **Bear** (25%) | $3.58–3.63B (+4–5%) | $4.30–4.40 | September issuance air pocket; Energy flat; MI decelerates below 5% on renewal slippage; guide held at midpoint with cautious Q4 tone | **-5% to -9%** |

**Bull requires:** confirmation that AI infrastructure financing is a multi-year Ratings annuity rather than a 2026 pull-forward, plus evidence the MI reorg is converting AI demand into ACV (management cited Kensho LLM-ready APIs at 500+ clients with API volume >5x Q1, and AI-adopting customers growing ACV ~60% faster in MI and ~3x in Energy). Listen for an early, confident 2027 framing against the $19.98 consensus.

**Bear requires:** management attributing a Q4 slowdown to issuance timing, or any repeat of the Q2 language about elongating large-client renewal cycles. Energy below the +4.5–6% guide would be the clearest tell.

**Historical calibration:** beats in 7 of the last 8 quarters (MarketBeat) / 3 of last 4 with a 4.4% average surprise (Zacks). OptionsLam rates SPGI's earnings volatility a low 2.0/10 — but the last print broke that pattern with a -7.7% intraday swing, driven by basis confusion rather than fundamentals. Zacks Earnings ESP is **-0.56%** with a Rank 3 (Hold), so the quant signal does not predict a beat.

## Catalyst Checklist

1. **Market Intelligence organic revenue vs. ~$1.27B** — and critically, *on which base it's reported*. Against recast $1,183M this looks like a 7% quarter; against the old $1,236M it looks like 2–3%. Watch for the first-30-minutes misprice.
2. **FY26 adj. EPS guide vs. $17.50–$17.75** — the buy-side wants the top half. Holding the midpoint after an H1 that ran +18% would read as a de facto H2 warning.
3. **Billed issuance growth and Q4 pipeline commentary** — specifically whether the $250–300B hyperscaler assumption is reaffirmed or raised, and whether the September rate move disrupted anything.
4. **Energy vs. the +4.5–6% full-year guide** — the one segment where consensus and guidance are visibly incompatible.
5. **Narrative items:** MI reorg execution and stranded-cost/TSA offsets post-spin; bolt-ons (datacenterHawk, majority stake in Agusto & Co.); H2 buyback pacing against >$7B; any early 2027 guardrails.

## Bottom Line

The fundamental setup is better than the tape and the headline growth rates suggest: ~+6% revenue growth, double-digit EPS growth, two-thirds of revenue in benchmark franchises generating >80% of operating profit, and an H2 buyback acceleration. **The asymmetry tomorrow is informational** — guidance mechanically implies an H2 deceleration to +3–6% that the issuance and Indices data do not obviously support, so I lean to a modest beat and a guide narrowed to the upper half.

Two caveats temper that into a modest-positive rather than aggressive stance. First, the +16.6% pre-print rally has already priced some of the re-rating, and multiple expansion rather than estimate revision did the work. Second, Market Intelligence remains the genuine unknown: if AI-driven renewal friction with large clients is structural rather than timing, the division that anchors the "data compounder" narrative is also the one most exposed, and no Ratings beat will offset that in the multiple.

*Note on estimate vintage: consensus figures are as of Oct 20–26, 2026 from Zacks, MarketBeat, and Barchart/Yahoo and differ by vendor ($4.42 vs. $4.43 EPS; $3.64B vs. a $3.72B segment sum). Post-spin dispersion is unusually wide — Q2 "consensus" was variously cited at $4.49, $4.75, $4.81, and $5.02 for the same quarter. Figures labeled "derived" are my calculations from disclosed data, not company-reported.*
