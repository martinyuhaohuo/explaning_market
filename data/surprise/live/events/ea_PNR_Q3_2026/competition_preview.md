## The Setup in One Paragraph

This is a **credibility quarter, not an earnings quarter**. On July 14, Pentair pre-announced a severe Q2 miss (adj. EPS ~$1.12 vs. $1.47–$1.50 guided) driven by ~$170M of unanticipated Pool channel destocking, cut FY26 adjusted EPS guidance from $5.30–$5.40 to $4.60–$4.80, and disclosed its CFO had departed — all on the same day. The stock fell 15% the next session and is now down ~50% year-over-year against an industry down 2.5%. Management's central promise was specific and falsifiable: Pool channel inventory would be **"optimized by the end of Q3."** Q3 is that quarter. The print itself is nearly pre-ordained (consensus sits on the guidance midpoint); the stock will trade on whether destocking is genuinely finished and whether the 2027 Pool recovery framework survives contact with a pool market that peers describe as flat.

## Key Metrics to Watch (ranked)

**1. Pool destocking — is it actually over? (highest importance by a wide margin)**
CEO John Stauch committed to inventory being "optimized by the end of the Q3, setting us up nicely for the 2027 pool season." Watch for (a) explicit confirmation destocking is complete rather than "substantially complete," (b) the **sell-in vs. sell-through** reconciliation Stauch walked through last quarter (ship-in plus change in channel inventory = true dealer demand), and (c) whether the FY impact stays at ~$250M or creeps higher. Any language that pushes normalization into Q4 or H1'27 is the single most damaging outcome available.

**2. The 2027 Pool framework — $1.45B or not?**
Interim CFO Bob Fishman pointed to ~**$1.45B of 2027 Pool revenue** versus ~$1.25B in 2026 — roughly **+16%**. This is the entire bridge back to a $5+ EPS story and the reason the sell-side still carries a $70.80 average target. It is also directly challenged by the read-across below. Reaffirmation with channel-inventory evidence is the bull trigger; silence or "we'll frame 2027 in February" is a de-rating.

**3. FY26 guidance and the Taco bridge**
The $4.60–$4.80 guide **excluded** Taco Group Holdings, which closed **October 1, 2026** — zero Q3 contribution, but a full Q4. Management must now hand the street a Q4 bridge: ~$135M of incremental sales (into Water Solutions), $30M of eventual run-rate synergies, against interest on the **$1.4B of term loans** (a $400M tranche 1 plus $1.0B tranche 2, signed Sept 1). Taco is guided to $0.10–$0.15 accretive in FY27; Q4 is realistically neutral-to-slightly-positive. Watch whether the headline FY number goes *up* mechanically while the underlying ex-Taco guide quietly goes *down*.

**4. Margin durability ex-Pool, and how much is tariff refunds**
Q2's segment margins were extraordinary — Flow ROS 26.5% (+470bps), Water Solutions 30.0% (+560bps) — but were flattered by **~$35M of IEEPA tariff refunds** ($18M in Water Solutions alone), against a FY expectation of $35–$50M. Strip those out and the question is how much of the ~$55M full-year productivity savings (net of investment) is structural. Pool ROS fell to 23.4% from 35.7% on volume deleverage; the incremental margin on the 2027 recovery is the real valuation swing factor.

**5. Cash, leverage, and capital allocation**
FCF was strong YTD (~$553M through H1, aided by tariff refunds and working capital release from the destock itself). Net leverage was guided to **~2.4x at Taco close**, with a sub-1.5x target within two years. Expect the $150M/quarter buyback pace (2.0M shares in Q2) to be paused in favor of deleveraging — confirmation matters for anyone modeling share count.

**6. Management and litigation overhang**
Bob Fishman (interim) still presents this call; **Bob Hau** — ex-Fiserv, TE Connectivity, Lennox CFO — takes over **November 1**, with a new Pool CEO, **Josh Cowley**, effective November 9. A securities class action is pending in SDNY covering purchasers between April 28 and July 14, 2026, alleging non-disclosure of the Pool destock. New leadership has both the incentive and the cover to reset the bar one more time — a classic kitchen-sink risk.

## The Read-Across That Matters

**Pool Corporation reported Q3 on October 22** (five days before PNR): sales +4% on +3% price / +1% volume, equipment +5% on "healthy replacement activity," but gross margin **−60bps to 29.0%** on the worst inbound freight costs since COVID. Critically, management said it expects **no material macro improvement in 2027** — CEO Watwood: *"It's really going to be about what you can do internally."*

That is a constructive signal for Pentair's *aftermarket/replacement* Pool volumes but a direct problem for a **+16% 2027 Pool growth** assumption. It is the most likely explanation for the wave of October target cuts.

**What each requires operationally.** *Bull:* channel partners complete right-sizing on schedule and begin normal early-buy ordering for the 2027 season, with Water Solutions volume decline moderating from −2.7%. *Base:* Pool lands in the guided band and management buys time — the most likely path, given a new CFO arriving four days after the call has every reason not to underwrite 2027 yet. *Bear:* dealer sell-through, not just channel inventory, proves to be the problem — which would reframe this from a one-time inventory correction into a demand reset and put the ~$1.25B 2026 Pool base itself in question.

**Historical calibration.** Over the last 8 reports, options priced an average **±2.3%** move while the stock actually moved **4.4%** — the options market has persistently *underpriced* PNR earnings. Actual moves ranged from **−10.2%** (April 2026) to **+9.2%**. Note the July sequence carefully: the −15% came on the **July 14 pre-announcement**, and the July 28 print itself was **+5.7%** — a relief rally against a pre-reset bar plus the Taco announcement. There has been no equivalent pre-announcement this time, which cuts both ways: no pre-release warning is mildly reassuring, but it also means the full surprise lands on October 27.

## Catalyst Checklist

1. **Pool segment revenue vs. $266M consensus / −23% to −25% guide, plus explicit "destocking is complete" language.** Consensus is already at the weak end, so the number is less important than the characterization. This is the stock.
2. **Reaffirmation (or not) of ~$1.45B 2027 Pool revenue.** The buy-side needs this to underwrite any recovery; POOL Corp's flat-2027 commentary makes it contestable. Watch for a pivot to "we'll give 2027 in February."
3. **Updated FY26 guidance including Taco, with a clean Q4 bridge.** Specifically: ex-Taco adjusted EPS versus the standing $4.60–$4.80, interest expense on $1.4B of term loans, and whether the Pool destock impact remains ~$250M.
4. **Adjusted EPS vs. the ~$1.05 whisper, and segment ROS ex-tariff-refunds.** A $1.07 "in-line" print built on $20M+ of refunds is a lower-quality beat than it looks; quantify the refund by segment.
5. **Narrative/leadership reset risk.** Hau (Nov 1) and Cowley (Nov 9) arrive immediately post-print. Any hint of a kitchen-sink reset, deferred guidance, or a strategic review of Pool is a second leg down; conversely, a credible new-management framing with deleveraging priorities is the floor-setting outcome.

## Bottom Line

Base case is an in-line-to-slightly-soft print — consensus is pinned to the guidance midpoint, and the revenue bar has been quietly de-risked — with the stock's reaction determined almost entirely by three words: whether destocking is **"complete"** or **"substantially complete."** The risk is skewed to the downside relative to the modest relief a clean in-line print would generate, because a new CFO and a new Pool CEO arriving within two weeks of the call create a natural, well-telegraphed opportunity to reset 2027 expectations that this management team has not yet taken.
