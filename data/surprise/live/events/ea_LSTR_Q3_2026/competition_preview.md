## 1. The Setup in One Paragraph

Landstar is reporting into a genuine freight upcycle — the first in four years — and the stock has been de-rating anyway. Q2 FY2026 delivered the largest sequential jump in truck revenue per load in 15 years (+14.4%) and an 18% revenue beat, and the stock fell ~7% across the session and after-hours because EPS missed on insurance and claims. That is the whole story of this print: **the top line is being driven by a tightening truck market that management does not control, and the bottom line is being driven by a post-*Montgomery* liability environment that management controls even less.** Consensus has the easy half right. The hard half is a coin flip.

Two things matter here. First, **the volume growth is all BCO** — BCO loads +10% against brokerage loads down ~5%, a direct consequence of the carrier purge. That mix shift is margin-*accretive* (Q1 FY2026 VC margin expanded to 14.7% precisely on richer BCO mix). Second, it got swamped: VC margin fell 20 bps because the rate paid to brokerage carriers rose 136 bps and brokerage net revenue margin compressed **129 bps sequentially**. That is the textbook broker squeeze at a cycle inflection — spot costs repricing faster than customer rates.

**Management's Q3 framework (no formal guidance, citing a "fluid" freight, geopolitical, and litigation backdrop):**
- July truck loads **~+5% YoY** (dispatch basis) — slightly ahead of normal seasonality
- July revenue per load **~+26% YoY** (process basis) — pricing ~150 bps better than typical seasonality, *though less elevated than April–June*
- Pre-pandemic Q2→Q3 seasonality: RPL **+1.5%** sequential, volume **−1.5%** sequential → roughly **flat sequential revenue**
- AI tooling deploying into agent offices from mid-Q3
- Dividend raised 10% to $0.44/quarter (sixth consecutive year)

Note the gap: consensus $1.49B is **+4% sequentially**, above the "flat" seasonal framework. Consensus is implicitly crediting July's momentum.

| **Bear** (25%) | **$1.435B** (+19%) | **~$1.15** | 13.4% | $50M | Pricing fades to +3% seq as spot cools; brokerage margin compresses again; **another ~$10–12M adverse development** | **−10% to −16%** |

**What each scenario needs operationally**

- **Bull** — August and September RPL hold the July run-rate rather than mean-reverting; brokerage net revenue margin stabilizes sequentially (proof the pass-through lag has closed); BCO net adds accelerate past +68; management quantifies October-to-date at double-digit RPL growth and reinstates a formal Q4 range. *Commentary tell:* a specific, confident Q4 EPS bracket. Landstar guided explicitly as recently as Q3 FY2024 ($1.25–1.45 for Q4'24) and went dark in Q2'26 — **resuming guidance is itself the bull signal.**
- **Base** — the seasonal framework roughly holds, revenue beats modestly on price, EPS clears the low bar, but management stays unguided and non-committal on claims. *This is the Q2 outcome replayed, and Q2 traded down ~7% on it.* A base-case print is not a base-case stock move; revenue beats alone have stopped working on this name.
- **Bear** — a second consecutive quarter of reserve strengthening converts "lumpy claims" into "structurally higher cost of doing business post-*Montgomery*," which is a multiple story, not an EPS story. Watch for language about increasing *frequency* of litigated claims rather than severity of individual ones.

**Historical calibration:** Q2 FY2026 −3.29% regular / −4.22% after-hours (~−7.4% combined) on a revenue beat + EPS miss. Q1 FY2026 beat and rose. Q4 FY2025 beat by 4.2%. Q3 FY2025 missed by 1.6%. Peer read-through this season is split: Ryder beat revenue by 1.2% and rose 12.7%; Knight-Swift beat by 1.4% and fell 1%. Transports are −4.9% over the past month; LSTR is roughly flat.

## 6. Catalyst Checklist — Ranked

1. **Insurance and claims dollars, and specifically prior-period development** *(dominant)*. Q2 carried $10.5M adverse, with **three of five major claims arising in truck brokerage** — the business line *Montgomery* just exposed. At ~$0.022 of EPS per $1M, this line alone spans the entire bull-bear EPS range. Listen for whether management raises its reserving posture prospectively.
2. **Truck RPL cadence — August/September actuals and October-to-date** vs. July's +26%. July pricing was already described as *less* elevated than April–June, and DAT spot rates dipped 2–3 cents/mile in early October as load posts cooled. Is +26% the peak or the run rate? This determines Q4 and 2027 numbers far more than the Q3 print.
3. **Variable contribution margin and brokerage net revenue margin, sequentially.** Q2's −129 bps brokerage compression was the pass-through lag. If it compresses again, the "upcycle is good for Landstar" thesis needs rework — the asset-light model gives up margin on the way up before earning it back.
4. **BCO net truck adds and the approved-carrier count.** +68 was the best in four years and the cleanest evidence that the capacity rebuild is real. Separately, has the 35% carrier purge *stopped* at ~64,600, or is vetting still shrinking the brokerage book? Continued shrinkage caps volume growth.
5. **Guidance reinstatement + post-*Montgomery* strategy.** Any formal Q4 range, commentary on broker-liability renewal pricing (ran +3% at June renewal; auto liability flat), deliberate mix shift toward BCO/agent capacity, and any read-through on the C.H. Robinson verdict. Also watch capital allocation — YTD buybacks were only $24.1M against $280.9M net cash with the stock 27% off its high; a repurchase step-up would read as management calling the de-rating overdone.

## 7. The Legal Overhang — Why the Stock Is Down With Earnings Up

This is the non-consensus part of the setup and it explains the entire valuation disconnect.

- **May 14, 2026** — SCOTUS unanimously held in ***Montgomery v. Caribe Transport II*** that state-law negligent-hiring claims against freight brokers are **not preempted** by the FAAAA. CFO Jim Todd: cases that previously would have been dismissed on federal preemption grounds now have to be litigated. CEO Frank Lonegro has publicly called for "greater federal clarity around carrier vetting and selection standards."
- **July 23, 2026** — a Dallas County jury returned a **$604M** verdict against C.H. Robinson and co-defendants (*Leip v. Lupus Superior*, from a fatal 2021 Mississippi pileup) — the largest collectible nuclear verdict ever against a broker. **In October 2026 the judge confirmed it.** CHRW is appealing; CHRW fell ~4% on disclosure and 3PL stocks sold off broadly.

Landstar's defense is structural and genuinely differentiated: ~8,544 BCO trucks it effectively controls, a 35% smaller and more heavily vetted brokerage carrier pool, ~990 agents, and scale to absorb compliance cost that smaller brokers cannot. The bull argument is that *Montgomery* is a consolidation catalyst that accrues to Landstar. The bear argument is that the cost of that defense now runs permanently through the claims line, and the tail risk is unquantifiable. **The market is currently paying for the bear case: 24x forward earnings in year one of a pricing upcycle, with the stock 27% below a high set before the ruling.**
