## 1. The Setup in One Paragraph

Seagate is reporting into the single most interesting setup of its cycle: fundamentals have never been better, and the stock just fell ~31% in a month. The September quarter itself is close to a formality — company guidance ($4.1B / $7.30) was set in July, Western Digital's Sept-quarter print on Oct 22 blew out ($4.38B vs. $4.14B; EPS $4.64 vs. $4.08), and STX has beaten for five straight quarters (avg. 4-qtr surprise +12.4%). The entire trade is in the **December guide and the pricing/margin trajectory**, against a tape that has decided to worry about Toshiba's capacity doubling and cycle-peak risk. The cautionary precedent is sitting right there: WDC guided December revenue to **$5.0–5.2B vs. $4.5B consensus** with a **60.5% gross margin** — a ~10%+ raise — and the stock still fell ~5% in the session and ~11% after hours. A beat-and-raise is necessary but demonstrably not sufficient.

## 4. Key Metrics to Watch — Ranked

1. **December-quarter revenue and EPS guidance.** The only number that matters. Printed consensus is $4.49B/$8.38, but WDC's guide implies the real bar is **$4.9–5.1B and EPS north of $9.00**. A guide at or below $4.6B will be read as confirmation that Seagate is pricing conservatively into a softening market, regardless of how good Q1 was.
2. **Non-GAAP gross margin, and the trajectory.** Guided ~57% for Q1. WDC guided **60.5%** for December. If Seagate prints ~57% and guides to only ~58–59%, the market will ask why the margin gap to WDC is widening — bears will answer "LTAs cap the upside."
3. **Pricing per terabyte / price-per-exabyte commentary.** WDC's blended y/y price-per-TB went from high-single-digits to high-teens, with Morgan Stanley citing spot transactions at **2–5x contracted prices**. Seagate's long-term agreements cap its participation in that spot strength. Any quantification of realized price increases — or evidence that LTAs are being repriced — is the cleanest read on whether the margin story has another leg.
4. **Contract coverage duration.** "Allocated into 2028" needs to become "2029" or "2030." WDC's CEO claimed visibility to 2030–2031. If management extends the booked horizon, the oversupply narrative loses its footing; if the language is unchanged, bears will call it a stall.
5. **Mozaic ramp.** Mozaic 4 (44TB) was targeted at 50% of HAMR exabytes by end of CY26, with Mozaic 5 qualification shipments in late CY27. HAMR % of nearline exabytes (40% exiting FY26) is the cost-curve metric — it's what lets Seagate hold margin if pricing eventually rolls over.
6. **Capex and capacity response.** 4–6% of revenue is the guide. Any move above that range would be read two ways — confidence in demand, or the start of the supply response that ends the cycle.
7. **TDK magnetic-heads bid.** Reported (Bloomberg) that Seagate outbid Toshiba for TDK's heads unit; Toshiba disputes the characterization and nothing is confirmed. Expect a question. A multi-billion-dollar deal is a balance-sheet and antitrust overhang against a company that has been deleveraging hard.

## 6. Catalyst Checklist

- [ ] **Dec revenue guide vs. ~$4.9B whisper** (not the $4.49B screen) — the decisive number. WDC reset the bar on Oct 22 and consensus hasn't fully caught up.
- [ ] **Dec gross margin guide vs. WDC's 60.5%** — a narrowing gap validates pricing power; a widening gap feeds the "LTAs cap the upside" bear case.
- [ ] **Booked coverage horizon: "2028" → "2029/2030"?** — the direct rebuttal to Toshiba oversupply fears, and free to deliver if true.
- [ ] **Explicit management response to Toshiba's capacity doubling** (~¥60B/$380M Philippines expansion, doubling nearline capacity by FY27, 30TB-class in 2027). Buy-side wants industry supply growth quantified in exabytes versus demand, framed against Morgan Stanley's ~400 EB 2027–28 shortfall estimate.
- [ ] **TDK heads-unit commentary** — size, financing, antitrust path, or a clean denial. Silence is probably fine; confirmation of a multi-billion-dollar cash deal is not.
- [ ] **Capital returns** — FCF (Q4 was $1.1B), buyback pace post the $1.2B debt retirement, and any dividend action. WDC raised its dividend alongside its print; a matching move would help.

**Read on the setup.** Implied vol is pricing a genuinely two-sided event, and for once that looks about right rather than cheap. The asymmetry is better than it was three weeks ago — a 41% drawdown and a mid-teens forward multiple on 135% FY27 EPS growth is not a crowded long. But the WDC template is the thing to respect: when a stock has run 135% YTD, an in-line-to-good guide is a sell, and only a guide that forces estimate revisions higher gets paid. If you want to own the fundamental story, the honest expression is long into the number *small*, adding on a disappointed-but-not-broken reaction, rather than sizing up for a beat that is already the base case.
