## 2. What to Watch — Ranked

**1. Gross margin vs. the 27.5% "baseline" (highest importance).**
On the Q2 call, CFO Barry Logan called 27.5% "the baseline that we expect looking forward," after Q2 GM compressed 180bp to 27.5% from an OEM-pricing-inflated 29.3%. **The Q3 comp is structurally 180bp easier than Q2's** — Q3 2025 GM was 27.5%, not 29.3%. But Q3 2025's 27.5% was itself +130bp Y/Y on A2L pricing. The debate: does GM hold ~27.5%, or normalize toward the ~26.2% pre-A2L level? Every 50bp ≈ **$0.18 of EPS** on a $2.2B quarter. This is the single largest swing factor, and it is where the Q2 blow-up came from.

**2. Organic revenue vs. the "mid-single-digit" July run-rate.**
Jackson Supply (closed June 1, ~$230M annual sales) contributes a full quarter in Q3 — roughly **+$57M, or ~2.8pts of inorganic growth**. So the $2.20B consensus embeds only **~3.5% organic**, *below* the "+4% to 5%" quarter-to-date organic pace management cited through July 28 and below their stated expectation of mid-single-digit Q3 growth. Revenue is modestly beatable **if** August–September held. Peer read-across says be careful: Lennox cut FY26 EPS to $23–24 in July on residential volumes **−12%** and resi revenue −7%.

**3. The Q4 math, not the Q3 print.**
FY26 consensus of $12.19 against 1H actual of $5.92 and Q3E of $4.11 leaves **~$2.16 implied for Q4 vs. $1.68 last year (+29%)**. That looks stretched. Granite Group (closed Oct 12, ~$500M annual sales, 82 Northeast locations) adds Q4 revenue but plumbing mix is typically lower-margin, and purchase accounting/integration costs land in Q4. **A clean Q3 can still produce negative revisions.** Listen for any 4Q framing on the call.

**4. Dividend coverage.** The dividend was raised 10% to **$13.20/yr** ($3.30/qtr) — that is **~108% of FY26E EPS of $12.19**, and the 4.3% yield (vs. WSO's historical 2–3%) says the market is discounting it. With Granite cash out the door and a new **$750M unsecured facility closed Oct 22**, any commentary on payout, leverage, or capital allocation is a live catalyst in both directions.

**5. SG&A and inventory.** SG&A was +2% ex-acquisitions in Q2 ("a more efficient company as business conditions have simplified"). Q3 2025 SG&A was 16.6% of sales; watch for leverage vs. the +5% growth of a year ago. On inventory: management flagged ~$100M (≈7 days) above normal with field stock down ~$200M, and guided to **lower inventory in 2H26**. The 6/30 balance of $1,890M vs. $1,386M at YE25 is a seasonal build — a clean Q3 drawdown plus strong operating cash flow (vs. a record $355M in Q3 2025) is the bull's supporting evidence.

Secondary: HVAC equipment mix (67% of Q3 2025 sales, was −7% Y/Y), residential vs. commercial split, e-commerce (37% of sales, +13%), non-U.S. markets (−14% in Q3 2025), and refrigerant — a $35M line in a $2B quarter, so a Y/Y headwind but not a thesis driver.

Framework: GM and SG&A flex around a $2.0–2.3B revenue base; operating income converted to EPS at Q3 2025's realized **0.688** ratio (interest, ~24% tax, Carrier JV noncontrolling interest) on **~41.0M** diluted shares.

| **Bull** (25%) | $2.26B (+9%; organic ~+6.5%) | 27.6% | ~$264M (11.7%) | **~$4.45** | Resi replacement re-accelerates through Sept; GM holds the 27.5% baseline; SG&A leverage at 16.3%; strong OCF + inventory drawdown; constructive Granite accretion framing | **+8% to +12%** |
| **Base** (50%) | $2.20B (+6.4%; organic ~+3.6%) | 27.1–27.3% | ~$240–245M (11.0%) | **~$4.00–4.12** | In-line revenue, GM ~20–40bp light, SG&A at 16.6%; no Q4 reassurance | **−2% to +4%** |
| **Bear** (25%) | $2.13B (+3%; organic ~flat) | 26.3% | ~$202M (−14%, 9.5%) | **~$3.40–3.50** | Aug/Sept volumes roll over per Lennox; OEM pricing normalization takes GM below "baseline"; SG&A deleverage on acquired cost base; Q4/FY27 numbers come down hard | **−8% to −14%** |

**Operationally, what distinguishes them:** the bull case requires *both* GM ≥27.5% *and* organic ≥5% — management has already told you the first is their expectation, so the burden is on volumes. The bear case needs only one leg to break; Q2 proved GM alone is sufficient (revenue was nearly in-line at $2.10B vs. $2.14B, yet the stock fell 12.9%).

**Management signals to listen for:** "baseline" reaffirmed on GM = bull support. Any reframing of 27.5% as "elevated," or renewed mention of OEM pricing actions/customer mix diluting ASPs, = bear. On volumes, a QTD October organic figure (they gave one in July) is the highest-signal datapoint on the call.

**Historical calibration:** Q2 FY26 (Jul 29) — EPS $4.00 vs. $4.35–4.41, stock **−12.9%** close-to-close, **−16.4% intraday**, with a further **−14.9% post-earnings drift**. Q1 FY26 (Apr 28) — beat ($1.87 vs. $1.70) and the stock *still* dipped. Q4 FY25 (Feb 17) — miss (−12%). Q3 FY25 (Oct 29, 2025) — EPS miss of 5.5%, but stock only **−1.2%**, because record GM and record $355M operating cash flow offset it. **Three EPS misses in the last four quarters.** The pattern: beats don't get paid, misses do get punished — except when cash flow and margin optics give the bulls a story.

## 4. Catalyst Checklist

1. **Gross margin vs. 27.5%** — the entire Q2 de-rating was a margin event. ≥27.5% reframes 2027; ≤26.8% confirms the normalization bear case. *Highest beta item.*
2. **EPS vs. the $4.04 whisper, not the $4.13 consensus** — a $4.05–4.10 print is a "technical miss" that may trade fine given positioning; below $3.95 and the GM debate reopens.
3. **Organic growth vs. the +4–5% July exit rate** — tests whether Lennox's residential volume collapse is a share-shift/OEM issue or a true end-market problem. WSO reports *before* Lennox (Oct 28) and Carrier/Trane, so this print is the sector's first read.
4. **Q4 and Granite framing** — with ~$2.16 of Q4 EPS embedded in consensus vs. $1.68 LY, the risk of negative revisions on an in-line quarter is material. Watch for integration costs, plumbing mix impact on GM, and any FY27 accretion commentary.
5. **Capital allocation / dividend** — $13.20 payout vs. $12.19 FY26E EPS, plus the new $750M facility and Granite consideration. Reassurance here is worth multiple points; evasion is a problem given a 4.3% yield is doing real work in the shareholder base.
