## 3. Key Metrics to Watch — Ranked

1. **NAV per share (most important).** Three consecutive declines. A flat-to-up print re-rates the stock off a 4% book discount; a fourth decline below ~$19.15 validates the bear case that credit marks, not just spreads, are the problem. The realized-vs-unrealized *mix* matters more than the headline: Q2's −$0.25/share unrealized vs. only −$0.01 realized is a very different quarter than the reverse.
2. **Core EPS vs. $0.48.** $0.48+ ends the uncovered-dividend headline; $0.47 extends it to three quarters; ≤$0.46 makes the Q4 dividend a live question despite spillover.
3. **Non-accruals at amortized cost vs. the 2.4% base.** Q2's rise came from four unrelated names. Management guided to industry-wide "normalization" toward their ~3% historical average — so a drift to 2.6–2.8% is *pre-announced* and should be partially discounted. New-name concentration or a jump in the fair-value figure (1.4%) is what hurts. Context: reported non-accruals at the ten largest BDCs were ~3.95% at cost in Q2, so ARCC remains well inside the peer set.
4. **Q4 2026 dividend declaration.** The binary. Base case is a $0.48 hold.
5. **Earning-asset yield and funding cost.** Yield on debt has compressed 30bp YoY to 10.3% against industry gross spreads now 4–5% (−100bp vs. 2025). Offsets: new senior loan spreads **+20bp** vs. Q4'25, upfront fees **+50bp**, a $476M securitization reset **−35bp**, and the inaugural **$1B commercial paper program** management sized at **50–100bp** of funding savings. Q3 is the first quarter that CP program shows up.
6. **Origination conversion.** Q2 deal reviews +25% sequentially; June was "one of our strongest months in two years." Did that convert? Q2 fundings of $2.2B against $2.9B of exits meant the portfolio *shrank* — a second quarter of net shrinkage caps forward NII regardless of yield.

**Macro tailwind the market may be underweighting:** the Fed *hiked* 25bp on Sept 16, 2026 to 3.75–4.00% (first hike since July 2023), with a December hike widely expected and futures putting an Oct 28 hold at ~91%. SOFR sits ~3.88% spot / ~3.75% 3-month average. With 94% of new Q2 commitments floating-rate, CEO Kort Schnabel explicitly flagged modest base-rate widening as "a benefit," and noted rate *volatility* matters more to transaction volume than rate *level*. This is a tailwind to Q4'26–Q1'27 NII, not to the quarter being reported — listen for whether management quantifies it.
