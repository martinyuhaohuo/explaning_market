## 2. Setting the Bar: Why This Quarter's Consensus Is Harder Than It Looks

The single most important observation for positioning: **$0.945 implies a +10% sequential EPS step-up from Q2's $0.86, and the arithmetic says it has to come from credit, not revenue.**

- **Margin tailwind is explicitly guided down.** Q2 NIM (FTE) hit 3.90%, +47bp YoY and +22bp sequentially. Management stated on the Q2 call that the 22bp sequential improvement "is unlikely to repeat," and disclosed that ~$241K of the benefit was non-recurring (bond calls, credit marks on acquired loans) — roughly 3–4bp of borrowed margin.
- **New-business yields are compressing.** Loan pipeline yields fell from ~7% to the "high-to-mid-6% range," while management flagged *increasing* competitive pressure on deposits (cost of deposits 2.06% vs. 2.17% YoY — the YoY tailwind is nearly exhausted).
- **Accretion is a known, finite prop.** Guided to ~**$700K/quarter of purchase-loan accretion** over the next six months — ~$0.05/share pretax, ~9–10bp of NIM. Quantifying the core (ex-accretion) margin is the main analytical task on the call.
- **Therefore the step-up must be provision.** Q2's $1.94M provision included ~$1.4M of initial net charge-offs (0.24% NCO ratio) tied to one credit. Normalizing provision to ~$1.0–1.1M is worth roughly $0.06–0.08/share. Consensus is, in effect, a bet that Q2's credit event does not recur.

**Also note the EPS-vs-earnings optics.** The Presence Bank acquisition lifted the diluted share count ~16% YoY (~9.36M → ~10.85M). Even with NII up ~32% YoY, EPS is roughly *flat* against the prior-year adjusted $0.94. Headlines comparing reported EPS growth will understate operating momentum; headlines comparing net income will overstate per-share progress. Judge this print on **PPNR per share and ROTE**, not EPS growth.

**Miss streak is live:** NWFL has missed consensus EPS two quarters running (Q1'26 $0.72 vs. $0.805; Q2'26 $0.86 vs. $0.87), both times on merger/credit noise rather than core revenue — Q2 revenue actually beat by ~13%.

## 3. Key Metrics to Watch — Ranked

**1. Credit: the $22M nonperforming commercial credit (highest importance).**
NPLs jumped to **1.23% of loans** from 0.34% a year earlier, with a single ~$22M bankruptcy accounting for most of the ~$23–24M of NPAs. Allowance is **1.13% of loans** against reserves-to-NPAs of just **90%**. Management called it "isolated rather than indicative of broader portfolio deterioration." Watch: (a) any resolution, recovery or further write-down on this credit; (b) whether NPLs migrate *beyond* it — a second name appearing would break the "isolated" narrative and re-rate the multiple; (c) whether reserve coverage is rebuilt toward 100%+ of NPAs; (d) criticized/classified and 30–89 day delinquency trends, the leading indicators.

**2. Core NIM ex-accretion and ex-one-timers.**
Reported NIM will likely look fine (~3.85–3.92%). The question is the *core* run-rate once you strip ~$700K accretion and Q2's $241K of non-recurring items. Also watch deposit beta on the way down and the mix contribution from **$493M of municipal deposits** (seasonally volatile, and a funding-cost swing factor into Q4).

**3. Loan growth and the payoff problem.**
Loans $2.263B, **+26.4% YoY** (overwhelmingly acquisition-driven). Q2 organic growth was held back by higher-than-expected payoffs despite good production. Growth came from CRE and indirect consumer (seasonally strong in Q2 — expect that tailwind to fade in Q3), while C&I saw slight paydowns. Watch loan/deposit ratio (90.0% in Q2) for funding headroom.

**4. Expense discipline / Presence Bank synergy run-rate.**
Integration including core conversion completed in Q2; merger expenses collapsed to $53K from ~$5M in Q1. Q3 is therefore the **first genuinely clean quarter** — the first true read on the post-deal expense base and the realized cost-save run-rate. Management says it is earning back deal dilution **two years ahead of schedule**; Q3 is where that gets tested with no accounting noise. Also watch commentary on the three-year AI efficiency program (likely narrative, not yet numbers).

**5. Capital returns and M&A posture.**
Dividend $0.32/quarter ($1.28 annualized, ~3.7–3.8% yield), ex-date Oct 15, payable Nov 2 — a Q3 raise would be a positive surprise. TBVPS $22.96 (vs. $21.17 YoY), adjusted ROTE 15.1%, adjusted ROA 1.29%. CEO James Donnelly said on the Q2 call that "urgency for additional M&A has eased." Any reversal of that — a new deal announcement — would likely be taken badly at 1.5x TBV with a credit question still open.

### What each scenario requires, and the management tell

- **Bull:** needs the credit resolved or reserved-through, *plus* evidence that core NIM ex-accretion is stable. **The tell:** management quantifying core NIM separately from accretion and stating it is flat-to-up; specific language on recovery value or collateral on the bankruptcy credit; a dividend increase.
- **Base:** the most likely outcome and the hardest to trade. **The tell:** reiteration of the "isolated credit" framing with NPLs unchanged, Q4 margin described as "stable to modestly lower," and loan growth "low-to-mid single digits."
- **Bear:** **The tell:** any new named credit, "elevated" or "lumpy" provisioning language, reserve build without a resolution, "intensifying" deposit competition, or a Q4 margin outlook below 3.80%.

## 5. Catalyst Checklist

1. **Provision & the $22M bankruptcy credit vs. an implied ~$1.0–1.2M provision.** The highest-leverage number in the release. Every $1M of incremental provision ≈ $0.07/share. Resolution with recovery is the bull case; a second deteriorating name is the bear case. Reserves/NPAs at 90% leaves no cushion.
2. **Core NIM ex-accretion vs. reported ~3.88%.** The buy-side wants core NIM disclosed separately from the ~$700K/quarter accretion, and a Q4 margin outlook. "Stable" is enough; anything implying sub-3.80% breaks the earnings power story.
3. **First clean post-integration expense base.** With merger costs at ~$0 for the first time, Q3 sets the run-rate that underpins the $3.88 FY2027 consensus. An efficiency ratio holding in the low-50s validates the deal math; drift toward 57%+ does not.
4. **Organic loan growth net of payoffs.** Q2's growth leaned on seasonally strong indirect consumer lending that fades in Q3, with C&I paying down and pipeline yields compressing ~50bp. Flat-to-down loans would mean the post-deal growth story is really just the acquisition.
5. **Capital-return and M&A narrative shift.** A dividend raise or buyback authorization would support the multiple. Conversely, walking back "urgency for M&A has eased" into a new deal would likely be penalized at 1.5x TBV with credit unresolved.
