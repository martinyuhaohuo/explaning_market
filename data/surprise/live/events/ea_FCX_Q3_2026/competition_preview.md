## 1. The setup in one paragraph

This is an unusual print: **FCX pre-announced the quarter's volumes, realized price, and a cost miss on October 2**, so the P&L is largely de-risked. Q3 copper production came in at ~830M lbs and gold ~230k oz (both in line), copper sales ~750M lbs (on plan), but gold sales fell to ~100k oz after ~60k oz of refined gold was deferred from Q3 into Q4 on PTFI shipment timing. That deferral cut by-product credits, pushing consolidated unit net cash costs ~5% above the July guide of $2.00/lb (so ~$2.10/lb). Meanwhile copper realized **above $6.50/lb**. Net: the beat/miss math is narrow, and **the stock will trade on guidance and narrative, not on the quarter** — specifically Q4 volume catch-up, the Grasberg ramp trajectory, the first read on 2027, and how management frames the U.S. tariff premium. The tell is that copper sits near all-time highs while FCX is ~19% off its own high and down ~10% over the past month — the equity has decoupled from the commodity, which cuts both ways into the print.

## 3. Key metrics to watch — ranked

**1. Consolidated unit net cash cost (guide: ~$2.10/lb, i.e., 5% above $2.00).** The single most important line. The pre-announcement blamed by-product credits, which is a *timing* explanation that reverses in Q4. If the actual print shows cost pressure beyond the gold deferral — labor, diesel, mining rates, Grasberg fixed-cost absorption — the "transitory" framing breaks and the FY2026 $1.90/lb target (ex-PTFI idle costs) goes with it. **Watch the Q4 and full-year unit cost guide more than the Q3 actual.**

**2. Q4 implied volume ramp.** FY2026 guidance is 3.1B lbs copper / 650k oz gold / 93M lbs moly. With Q2 at 710M lbs and Q3 at ~750M lbs, holding the full year requires a **Q4 in the ~900–950M lb range** (derived; assumes Q1 ≈ 700M lbs, which I could not verify directly — treat as approximate). Gold is starker: ~550k oz of the 650k guide must land outside Q3, implying a Q4 near 300k+ oz including the 60k deferral. **That is a steep, back-end-loaded ramp with little room for slippage.** Any trim to FY volumes is the most likely source of a negative surprise.

**3. Grasberg Block Cave ramp.** Q3 mill throughput averaged ~140,000 t/day, **67% of pre-incident rates**, with ~70,000 t/day from the Block Cave itself. Milestones to confirm: material handling upgrades complete by early 2027, Block 1S restart mid-2027, **80% of capacity by mid-2027, full capacity by year-end 2027**. Recall Q1 2026 — a strong beat was overwhelmed by revised Grasberg guidance. This asset drives the stock's multiple.

**4. Realized prices and the cash flow mark-to-market.** FY2026 operating cash flow guidance of ~$8.3B assumed H2 at **$6.00/lb copper, $4,000/oz gold, $30/lb moly**. Actual Q3 realizations ran above $6.50/lb copper, gold is well north of $4,000 (Q2 realized $4,520), and moly is estimated near $31/lb. **The OCF guide should move up.** Rough sensitivity: ~$0.10/lb on ~3.1B lbs annual sales ≈ ~$300M of revenue, so a meaningful raise is mechanical, not heroic — if it doesn't come, ask why.

**5. Indonesia regulatory and logistics.** The PTFI smelter restarted in late August 2026. Gold shipment timing, export licensing, and smelter ramp are now a recurring swing factor on reported results — this is the quarter's demonstrated fragility, and the buy-side will want evidence the 60k oz actually clears in Q4.

**6. Capital allocation and growth.** Net debt $2.1B (excluding $3.2B of downstream processing facility debt) against a $3.0–4.0B target — balance sheet capacity exists. Capex $4.3B for 2026 ($3.0B major projects); H1 capex was $2.1B. Dividend $0.15/qtr ($0.075 base + $0.075 variable); H1 buybacks were a token $203M. **Watch for: a Bagdad doubling FID (targeted by year-end 2026), leach progress toward the 300M lb/yr goal, El Abra permitting, a variable-dividend increase, and any first-pass 2027 capex figure.**

## 4. Scenario analysis

Because volumes and price are known, EPS dispersion is genuinely tight — roughly **$0.03–0.04 per share** for every $0.10/lb move in either realized copper price or unit cost on 750M lbs. The scenarios below are therefore weighted toward *guidance*, not the quarter.

| Scenario | Revenue | Adj. EPS | Key driver | Stock reaction |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Bull** (~25%) | $7.4–7.6B | **$0.86–0.92** | Unit costs at/below ~$2.08/lb; FY volumes reaffirmed with gold catch-up explicitly confirmed; OCF guide raised on spot decks; constructive early 2027 framing (copper sales well above 3.1B lbs); Bagdad FID and/or tariff-premium quantification; variable dividend bumped | **+5% to +9%** |
| **Base** (~50%) | $7.1–7.4B | **$0.78–0.84** | Modest beat on cost control and moly; FY2026 volumes held; OCF guide marked up on price; 2027 formally deferred to the December/January guidance release; Grasberg milestones reiterated unchanged | **−2% to +3%** |
| **Bear** (~25%) | $6.9–7.2B | **$0.68–0.74** | Unit costs above ~$2.20/lb with non-transitory drivers; FY2026 copper and/or gold volumes trimmed (Grasberg or U.S. weather/flooding carry-over); 2027 capex guided higher; further Indonesia shipment slippage | **−6% to −12%** |

**What each would look like on the call.** *Bull:* management quantifies the Q4 by-product credit reversal in dollars, reaffirms 80%-by-mid-2027 Grasberg with the material handling system on schedule, and talks concretely about capturing the U.S. refined-copper premium. *Base:* "approximately in line," costs described as timing-driven, 2027 "we'll provide guidance with Q4." *Bear:* hedged language on the Block Cave ("evaluating the sequence"), a second gold deferral, or cost inflation attributed to mining rates and labor rather than by-products.

**Historical calibration.** Despite four consecutive beats averaging +32.6%, **the stock has not been paid for them**: −2.3% the day after the Q2 beat (July 23) and roughly −0.7% on the Q1 report (April 23), when a 21% EPS beat was swamped by revised Grasberg guidance. (One vendor cites a −12.6% Q1 move; it conflicts with the other sources I found and I'd discount it.) **The read-through: beats on the quarter have not moved FCX; guidance revisions have.** Skew your attention and your risk accordingly.

## 5. Catalyst checklist

1. **Unit net cash cost vs. ~$2.10/lb — and the Q4 guide.** Matters because the entire bull case rests on the cost spike being a by-product-credit timing artifact. A Q4 guide back toward ~$1.90–2.00/lb validates it; anything above ~$2.10/lb says the cost base has reset upward at exactly the moment Grasberg volumes are absent to absorb it.
2. **FY2026 volume reaffirmation (3.1B lbs Cu / 650k oz Au).** The implied Q4 ramp is steep and back-end loaded. The buy-side expects a reaffirm; a trim is the highest-probability source of a drawdown and would mark the third Grasberg-related guidance disappointment in four quarters.
3. **Operating cash flow guide mark-to-market.** Spot copper, gold, and moly all sit above the deck underpinning the ~$8.3B FY OCF guide. A raise is the expected minimum; the magnitude is how the market will size 2027 free cash flow and the variable dividend.
4. **Section 232 / U.S. tariff positioning.** Commerce is weighing **15% on refined copper imports from January 2027, rising to 30% in 2028**. As the largest U.S. copper producer, FCX is the primary domestic beneficiary of a COMEX-over-LME premium. Any quantification of the earnings uplift — or a warning on the "stranded" ~718kt of COMEX inventory unwinding — is a narrative-mover.
5. **Growth-plan decision points.** Bagdad capacity-doubling FID targeted by year-end 2026; leach initiative toward 300M lbs/yr; El Abra regulatory progress in Chile. These are the levers behind the "America's Copper Champion" framing and the main source of a multiple re-rate independent of the copper price.

## 6. Trading setup

- **Options-implied move: ~4%** (Bloomberg-compiled data, published ahead of the Oct 27 print). FCX has exceeded the implied move in **3 of the past 8** reports; last quarter options implied 8.2% and the stock moved 4.1%.
- **Implied vs. scenarios:** a ~4% straddle looks **roughly fair to slightly cheap** given my bear-case range of −6% to −12%. The distribution is asymmetric — the quarter itself is de-risked, so the left tail is a guidance tail, not an EPS tail. Directionally, that favors owning downside protection over selling premium into the print, and it argues against buying the straddle on a pure volatility view.
- **Momentum/positioning:** FCX is down roughly 6% over the trailing week and ~10% over the trailing month, and sits ~19% below its $80.24 52-week high — even as LME copper set an all-time high of $14,940/t on September 9 and COMEX hit $6.9285/lb on September 22, with copper still near records (~$6.50/lb on Oct 23). YTD return figures vary by source (~26% to ~40%) and the 1-year gain is ~56%, outpacing the mining group's ~39%. The de-rating into a record copper tape suggests the market is already discounting Grasberg execution risk and the cost reset — which cushions the bear case somewhat and raises the payoff on a clean reaffirm.
- **Valuation:** ~32x trailing, **~17x forward** earnings (one source frames it as 21x forward) — the gap is entirely the 2027 Grasberg recovery. You are underwriting the ramp, not the current quarter.
- **Macro backdrop:** world mine production fell **1.1% to 11.34Mt in H1 2026** with capacity utilization down to 77.1% from 80.5%; the El Teniente mudslide and Congo export restrictions add supply risk. Structural demand from EVs, grid, renewables, and AI data centers remains the core bull argument. Tight supply is FCX's friend — but tariff-driven inventory relocation into U.S. warehouses means part of the price signal is positioning, not scarcity.
