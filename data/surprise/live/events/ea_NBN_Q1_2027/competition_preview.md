## 2. Key Metrics to Watch (ranked)

**1. Net interest margin and purchased-loan accretion — most important.**
Q4 FY26 NIM was 4.80% (down from 5.10% YoY, flat sequentially). Management guided NIM to "remain relatively steady" despite declining accretion from the purchased book, which still yields ~8%. This is the single biggest swing factor: NBN's entire valuation premium rests on a ~4.8% NIM and 23% ROE being durable, not a run-off artifact. Watch the gap between reported NIM and NIM ex-accretion. Anything below ~4.60% reframes the story.

**2. National Lending origination volume and purchase pricing.**
Q4 delivered a record $257.3M of NL originations — the *third consecutive* record — within $389.8M of total volume ($94.4M NL purchased at 92.3% of UPB, $33.5M SBA 7(a), $4.5M insured small-balance). Two things matter: whether the origination record extends to a fourth quarter, and **the purchase price on acquired loans**. Purchase pricing has been volatile (97.6% in Q3, 92.3% in Q4) — lower prices mean better forward accretion. Management flagged intense competition from credit funds for homogeneous multifamily pools, with NBN most competitive in diversified collateral.

**3. Credit quality, with specific attention to lender finance concentration.**
Q4 was clean: NPAs $34.8M (0.67% of assets, improving), past dues 0.54%, NCOs $1.8M, provision only $679K, ACL 1.30% of loans. But **lender finance is roughly two-thirds of NL balances**. In a 2026 tape where non-depository financial institution exposure is under active scrutiny, a single lender-finance problem credit would be punished out of proportion to its size. Watch the criticized/classified migration, not just the NPA headline.

**4. Capital runway and the raise question.**
Tier 1 leverage was 11.9% at June 30 with ~$1.5B of remaining loan capacity against a $5.23B balance sheet growing 22% YoY. At the current origination pace that capacity is roughly four to five quarters. Any language softening toward an equity raise is a near-term overhang on a stock at ~1.9x book ($70.58 BVPS, +22% YoY).

**5. Expenses and funding mix.**
Management guided quarterly expenses up ~$1M before reaching steady state (data warehouse, automation investment). Efficiency ratio was 35.98% — the best-in-class cost structure is part of the thesis. Separately, loans of $4.59B against deposits of $3.69B is a **~124% loan/deposit ratio**; NBN funds growth wholesale. Watch deposit cost trajectory against Fed policy — this is where falling rates help and where a funding squeeze would show up first.

## 3. Scenario Analysis

| Scenario | EPS | Revenue | Key Driver | Stock Reaction |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Bull** (25%) | $3.85–4.15 | $70M+ | NIM holds ≥4.85%; 4th straight record NL originations >$275M; purchase pricing in low 90s; provision <$1M; ROE ~22% | **+6% to +10%**, new highs above $142 |
| **Base** (50%) | $3.45–3.70 | $66–69M | NIM 4.70–4.80%; total volume $350–400M; credit stable; expense ramp on plan; ROE ~19–20% | **−2% to +4%** |
| **Bear** (25%) | <$3.15 | <$63M | NIM compresses below 4.60% on accretion run-off; volume slips below $300M on competition; NPAs >1.0% or a lender-finance charge-off; capital raise signaled | **−8% to −15%** |

**What each requires operationally.** The *bull* case needs the purchased portfolio to keep yielding ~8% while new originations at ~7.25% and >50% LTV replace it without diluting margin — plus evidence NBN is winning diversified-collateral pools that the credit funds won't bid. The *base* case is simply Q4's machine running one more quarter with the guided $1M expense step. The *bear* case does not require a credit event; **accretion run-off alone can produce it**, which is why NIM-ex-accretion is the number to find first.

**What management commentary signals which.** Listen on the Tuesday call for: (a) Wayne quantifying the purchased-book yield — "approximately 8%" repeated is base case, any number starting with a 7 is bearish; (b) the forward pipeline characterization versus last quarter's "intense competition" framing; (c) whether the expense guide stays at ~$1M or expands; (d) any unprompted discussion of capital.

**Historical calibration.** On July 27, 2026, NBN beat by $0.65 (19% above consensus) *and missed* the revenue estimate — it traded down 1.1% pre-market, then closed **+2.99%** ($129.24 → $133.11). That is the template: the revenue line generates a knee-jerk, the EPS and margin detail set the close. Note the muted upside response to a 19% beat — this stock has already re-rated (+31.6% over the past year, market cap $1.07B → $1.13B), and the easy multiple expansion from 8x to 10.3x is behind it.

## 4. Catalyst Checklist

1. **EPS vs. the $3.60–3.75 whisper** (not the $3.41 published consensus) — with only two analysts, the published number is a weak anchor and the buy-side has calibrated to a four-quarter average beat of +$0.32.
2. **NIM ex-purchased-loan accretion** — the durability test for a 23% ROE. Management promised "relatively steady"; this is the quarter where lower accretion was flagged to start biting.
3. **Fourth consecutive record in NL originations** (bar: $257.3M) plus purchase price as % of UPB — tells you whether NBN is still getting paid for taking the loans the credit funds won't.
4. **Lender-finance credit disclosure** — two-thirds of NL balances in a sector under scrutiny. Clean quarter = non-event; any migration = outsized downside.
5. **Capital commentary** — ~$1.5B runway at a 22% growth rate. Raise language at 1.9x book is the main structural overhang.

- **Asymmetry:** The realized ~3% move is narrower than the bear case's −8% to −15%. The distribution is left-skewed here — NIM compression or a lender-finance credit mark produces a move larger than history suggests, while a beat gets a muted reception because the re-rating has already happened. If you're expressing a view, own the downside tail rather than paying for the upside.
