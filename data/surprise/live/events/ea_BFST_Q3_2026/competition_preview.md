## 2. Key Metrics to Watch — Ranked

**1. Nonperforming loan resolution (highest-torque item).** NPLs were $84.1M, or **1.26% of loans** — elevated versus peers, and the single thing keeping BFST at 1.35x TBV instead of 1.6x. Management guided **10–20% NPL resolution in Q3**, OREO down 10–15%, and a **~$50M year-end NPL target**. That implies Q3 NPLs of roughly **$67–76M**. Anything above ~$80M, or any *new* credit migration, breaks the "credit is healing" narrative that drove the stock's +19% YTD run. Watch ACL/loans (1.02%) for a defensive build.

**2. Core NIM vs. 3.68%.** Management guided "modest margin improvement," with ~**4bp** of mechanical help as the $100.3M low-yield acquired-loan sale redeploys. I model GAAP NIM ~3.78% / core ~3.72%. Loan yields are 6.48% against 2.17% funding cost.

**3. Loan growth vs. "high single-digit annualized."** Loans HFI *fell* $24.8M in Q2 (the sale masked solid origination). Period-end should land near **$6.79B** (+8% annualized). Houston is the stated engine but is only **6.2%** of loans today — small base, so the growth has to show up to be believed. Deposits also fell $229M in Q2; a second quarter of deposit shrinkage would force funding-cost questions.

**4. Core efficiency ratio / the 1.25% core ROAA target.** Core ROAA was 1.05% in Q2; management reaffirmed 1.25% as the goal while calling it a "stretch." Core efficiency 63.94% needs to break below ~62% for that math to work. Q3 is the first clean quarter of Progressive cost saves — this is the proof point.

**5. Fee income durability.** Swap fees were lumpy in Q2; management said Q3 wins had "already materialized." APC adds a full quarter of insurance/benefits revenue. SSW contributes $5.6B AUM.

## 3. The Macro Variance Nobody Has Repriced

This is the most important thing in this preview. **On the Q2 call (July 23), management's explicit baseline was "no further Fed rate cuts in 2026," and they framed Q3–Q4 margin upside around repricing brokered and organic CDs *downward*.**

**The Fed hiked 25bp on September 16, 2026, to 3.75%–4.00%** — the first hike since 2023, a 12–0 vote, with 16 of 19 FOMC members expecting at least one more this year. **And the next FOMC decision lands October 27–28 — the day *after* BFST reports.**

Two consequences:

- **The CD-repricing-lower thesis is dead.** That was a named pillar of H2 margin expansion. It is gone, and the Street's models may not have scrubbed it.
- **But BFST is mildly asset-sensitive, so the hike is a net positive — just a small one.** 52.4% of loans float (65.3% float or reprice within 12 months); 78.1% of deposits are interest-bearing. At management's own modeled **45–55% deposit beta**, a +25bp move is roughly +$8.8M annualized on floating loans versus ~+$7.1M on interest-bearing deposits — call it **+2bp of NIM**, and only ~two weeks of it falls inside Q3.

**Net read: the hike is a marginal Q3 positive and a modest Q4 positive, but it replaces a *larger* expected benefit.** The risk is that Q4 NIM guidance comes in flatter than the "modest improvement" cadence the Street extrapolated. Management will also likely be deliberately vague on rate assumptions with FOMC 24 hours out — don't mistake that hedging for weakness.

**Sector read-through is encouraging.** Q3'26 regional bank prints show margins expanding *with* deposit costs behaving: Regions NIM 3.68% (+2bp QoQ) with IB deposit costs flat at 1.70% and adj. EPS $0.73 vs. $0.66; BankUnited NIM 3.16% (+16bp YoY) with deposit costs down to 1.99%; Metropolitan Bank NIM 4.36% vs. 4.08%. Funding competition has not reignited. That materially de-risks my NIM assumption.

**What tips it bear:** credit, not margin. A $8.9B bank at 1.26% NPLs with ~50% of the book in CRE (income-producing 22.7%, owner-occupied 17.3%, construction & land 10.4%) gets no benefit of the doubt on an upside credit surprise. Baton Rouge 12.5% / Dallas 11.8% / Monroe 9.1% CRE concentration.

## 5. Catalyst Checklist

1. **NPLs vs. $84.1M** — needs to print $67–76M to validate the $50M year-end target. *The* swing factor on the multiple.
2. **Core NIM vs. 3.68%** — is the 4bp loan-sale redeployment benefit showing, and what replaces the dead CD-repricing tailwind?
3. **Core NIE vs. the ~$58M guide** — first clean quarter of Progressive conversion savings; gates the 1.25% ROAA story.
4. **Loan growth vs. high-single-digit annualized** — plus whether deposits stabilize after the $229M Q2 decline.
5. **Q4 NIM/NIE guidance under a hiking Fed** — management previously guided ~$57M Q4 NIE. Any walk-back of "modest margin improvement," or of the 1.25% core ROAA target, is the tell.
6. **Capital allocation** — see below; this one is underappreciated.

## 6. Trading Setup

- **Price $31.82**, 52-week range **$23.08–$32.53** — the stock is **within 2.2% of its 52-week high**.
- **YTD +19.1% vs. KRE +10.9%** — ~830bp of outperformance already banked.
- **1.35x TBV** ($23.61 TBV/share) and **10.1x forward EPS**; consensus PT ~$34.8 implies only ~9–10% upside. The sell-side is largely spent.
- **Options:** BFST options are thin and I could not source a reliable earnings-implied move. Zacks flagged unusually high IV in the Oct 16 $22.50 put series, but that's a single illiquid strike and I would not read a directional signal into it. **Use the ~3.8% median historical 1-day move as the working expectation, and don't size off screen-quoted implied vol in a name this illiquid** (32.6M shares out, $1.04B cap).

**The capital-allocation point.** On the Q2 call the CFO said buybacks were attractive "as long as it stays above $120" — that is **120% of tangible book**, not $120/share, i.e. roughly **$28.3** at current TBV. BFST bought 176,849 shares in Q2 at a **$27.22** weighted average. **At $31.82 the stock is ~135% of TBV — well clear of management's own value threshold.** Practical implication: the buyback that cushioned prior drawdowns is almost certainly switched off. There is no company bid under this print. That asymmetry is why I'd treat the bear case as having more room to run than the headline probabilities suggest.

### Positioning

**Fundamentals favor a beat; the setup does not favor the stock.** My $0.76 is above consensus and the sector tape supports the margin assumption — but a 1–2c beat at 52-week highs, 1.35x TBV, with the buyback dormant and two of the last three beats sold, is a poor risk/reward for new longs into the print. The quarter is likely fine. The *reaction* is the coin flip.

Preferred expression: **own it through the print only if your thesis is credit normalization**, since NPL resolution is the one variable that re-rates the multiple rather than nudging the quarter. Otherwise wait for the Oct 28 FOMC to clear and buy a disappointment toward ~$28–29, where management's own capital-allocation math says the stock is cheap and the buyback switches back on.
