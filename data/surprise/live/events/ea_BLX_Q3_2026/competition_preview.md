### ⚠️ The EPS trap: the AT1 coupon is lumpy and lands in Q3

Bladex issued a **$200M perpetual AT1 at 7.50%** in September 2025. The coupon is deducted from profit attributable to common shareholders **in the quarter it is distributed**, not accrued evenly:

- **1Q26:** net profit $56.4M → EPS $1.31 ⇒ ~$7.2M deducted
- **2Q26:** net profit $66.5M → EPS $1.77 ⇒ $66.5M ÷ 37.54M shares exactly ⇒ **zero deducted**

A semi-annual payment schedule (March/September) means **Q3 almost certainly carries a ~$7.5M deduction ≈ $0.20/share**. On ~$67M of net profit, headline EPS lands near **$1.55–1.60**, not $1.75. If a vendor prints a "miss" against a smoothed ~$1.68 estimate, check net profit and the AT1 line before reacting. Reasonable confidence, not certainty, on the Q3 timing — first thing to verify in the release.

## 2. Key metrics to watch (ranked)

**1. NIM — the whole debate (2Q26: 2.24%, –10bps QoQ, –12bps YoY).** Management conceded in July that "margin pressure was stronger than we initially expected," driven by abundant regional liquidity and competition on short-term lending spreads, not by rates. FY guide is ~2.30%; 1H came in at 2.29%, so **Q3 needs ≥2.30% to keep the guide intact** — unlikely. Watch whether NIM stabilizes near 2.24% (acceptable) or breaks below 2.20% (thesis damage). Offsetting: the Fed **hiked 25bp to 3.75–4.00% on 16-Sep-2026** (first hike since 2023, 12-0 vote, Chair Warsh), with the SEP implying one more by year-end. That lands too late to help Q3 NII materially but is a Q4/2027 tailwind for a floating-rate asset book — expect bulls to pivot to that framing on the call.

**2. Fee income — can it hold near the record? (2Q26: $23.3M net fees, +78% QoQ; non-interest income $25.6M ≈ 26% of revenue vs. a ~15% historical average).** Management explicitly warned quarterly fees are "uneven due to deal-timing." The swing factor: Bladex **structured a senior loan of up to $1.0B for the Republic of Panama on 15-Sep-2026** — its largest-ever transaction. Arranger economics on a sovereign of that size could plausibly be worth several million dollars of structuring fees. If it books in Q3, fees set another record; if it slipped to Q4 or was priced thin, fees mean-revert toward $15–18M and revenue misses.

**3. Provisions — the under-modeled headwind.** CFO van Hoorde guided coverage of impaired credits **toward 1.5–1.6x by year-end, from 1.2x**. On $75.1M of Stage 3 credits, that implies a **$25–30M allowance build across 2H26**, or ~$12–15M per quarter versus a ~$5.6M quarterly run-rate in 2025 (9M25 provisions $16.7M). That alone is ~$0.20/share of optical EPS drag. Watch Stage 3 (0.5% of portfolio), NPLs (0.7% of loans), and whether the coverage target is reaffirmed, pulled forward, or quietly dropped.

**4. Panama exposure, balance sheet and Tier 1 (2Q26: 16.6%, guide 15–16%).** Bladex has not disclosed how much of the $1.0B it retained. Held in full, a ~100% risk-weighted sovereign adds ~$1.0B of RWA against a ~$10.5B base — roughly **–140bps of Tier 1, to ~15.2%**, the bottom of guidance. Relevant because Panama is split-rated: **Moody's Baa3 (negative), S&P BBB– (stable), Fitch BB+ (sub-investment grade since March 2024)**. Commercial portfolio was $13.0B at 2Q26 against a 13–15% FY growth guide implying ~$13.1–13.3B by year-end — so the portfolio may print **above guidance** (~$13.6–14.0B) purely on this deal. Distribution evidence is the bull tell; retention plus Tier 1 at 15.x% is the bear tell.

**5. Deposits and funding mix (2Q26: $7.89B record, +22% YoY, 64% of funding).** Bladex was **upgraded by S&P to BBB+ on 22-Jun-2026** and placed **MXN 5.0B of 3-year FRNs on 8-Sep-2026** (after MXN 4.265B in April). Cheaper wholesale funding is the main remaining lever to defend NIM, since management says operational-deposit cost benefits are a 2029–2030 story.

**6. Efficiency (2Q26: 24.1%; 1H 25.2%; guide 27–28%).** The guide implies a meaningful 2H cost step-up. A Q3 ratio in the mid-20s is a quality beat; a jump to 28%+ signals strategic-plan investment spend arriving.

## 4. Catalyst checklist

1. **NIM vs. ~2.24% (the whisper is the floor, not the guide)** — the sell side has already written down the FY ~2.30% guide; what matters is sequential stabilization. Sub-2.20% breaks the "NII grows through margin pressure" story.
2. **Non-interest income vs. ~$20M, and Panama fee disclosure** — the single largest line-item uncertainty in the model. Fees at ~26% of revenue is the re-rating argument; one-quarter-wonder status is the bear case.
3. **Provision expense vs. ~$6–9M and the 1.5–1.6x coverage path** — a guided, non-credit-driven allowance build that most models don't carry; the most likely source of an optical EPS miss on clean credit.
4. **Tier 1 vs. the 15–16% guide + Panama hold size** — tests capital discipline, the dividend trajectory, and the $65–73 price targets that assume mid-teens ROE without a raise.
5. **FY26 adj. ROE guidance (14–15%) with 1H already at 15.3%** — the guide implies a 2H of ~13–14.7%. Reaffirmation is effectively conservatism; failure to lift toward ~15% after three quarters reads as a soft warning.
6. **Dividend ($0.6875/qtr)** — a raise historically comes with Q4 results, so flat is expected; an early increase would be a genuine surprise signal.
