**Report date:** Monday, October 26, 2026, 4:15 pm ET (20:15 UTC), after market close
**Conference call:** Tuesday, October 27 (CEO Andrew Walters, CFO, President Aundrea Williams) — note the ~16-hour gap between print and call
**Price:** $58.19 (Oct 26 intraday) | **Market cap:** ~$2.44B | **52-wk range:** $43.75–$67.09

## Bottom line

The reported EPS line is going to look bad and it mostly doesn't matter. Consensus implies adjusted EPS down 5–15% YoY on revenue up 4–8% — but that gap is almost entirely the $700M March equity raise, which took diluted shares from 35.6M in Q3'25 to ~43M. Underlying net income grew 17% in 1H26. The actual risk in this print is the FY2026 guidance math, which is severely back-end loaded, and the first quantification of Quadvest dilution.

### The whisper is about Q4, not Q3

This is the single most important piece of arithmetic in the preview:

- 1H26 adjusted diluted EPS: **$1.23**
- FY2026 standalone guidance: **$3.08–$3.18** (reaffirmed July 27)
- If Q3 prints $1.20 → 9M = $2.43 → **implied Q4 of $0.65–$0.75**
- Q4 FY2025 actual adjusted EPS: **$0.46** (FY25 $2.99 less 9M25 $2.53)

So guidance requires Q4 adjusted EPS up **41–63% YoY** — in the seasonally weakest quarter, in the first quarter that carries Quadvest's stepped-up depreciation and $100–200M of new acquisition debt. Even a flat-YoY Q3 of $1.27 only brings the implied Q4 down to $0.58–$0.68. Either Q3 beats materially, Q4 is genuinely loaded with rate-case timing, or the guide gets trimmed. That resolution is the print.

Corroborating signal: the Street's FY2027 EPS estimate sits around **$2.84** (finviz forward P/E 20.5x at $58.21) — *below* the FY2026 guide and below FY2025's $2.99 actual. The sell-side is modeling a 2027 earnings decline against management's 6–8% CAGR target anchored off $2.99. One of those two is wrong, and management may be asked to reconcile it on the call.

## Key metrics to watch — ranked

1. **FY2026 guidance language** — reaffirm $3.08–$3.18, narrow to the low half, or recast. Watch whether management shifts the basis from "standalone" to Quadvest-inclusive; a basis change is an easy way to lower the bar without saying "cut."
2. **Quantified Quadvest dilution.** Closed Oct 1, so Q3 (period ended Sept 30) is clean standalone and Q4 is the first impact quarter. Management has previously framed stepped-up fair-value depreciation as 10–20% of standalone plan dilution until 2028 rates. The buy-side wants a dollars-per-share number and a bridge to the early-2027 consolidated Texas GRC.
3. **Water production cost inflation vs. recovery.** Q2 saw a **$12.1M** increase in production costs on purchased-water and groundwater extraction pricing. Valley Water charges have compounded at 10–11% for a decade and now absorb $0.42 of every customer dollar. This is the structural margin threat; regulatory lag determines whether it reaches the P&L.
4. **Rate case calendar.** Connecticut GRC: $28.8M ask on ~$145M of investment, rates effective Feb 2027, management "open to settlement" and "cautiously optimistic" post-rebuttal. Maine: first consolidated GRC, $9.5M ask. California: $176M Williams Station PFAS ion exchange filing (note a source conflict on the decision date — the Q2 call implied year-end 2026, the 10-Q says Q4 2027; worth clarifying), plus the 2028–2030 GRC filing due January 2027.
5. **Share count and equity needs.** Management has guided to staying out of the equity market through at least YE2027 ex-acquisitions, funding base capex with the $400M forward equity component. Any softening of that commitment is the worst possible headline for this stock given dilution is already the whole bear case.
6. **Texas connection growth.** 59,800 active connections at June 30 (+10% in 1H, +5,400), ~99,000 in the contracted development pipeline. Texas goes from 8% to a targeted 26% of customers by 2029. Watch Q3 connection adds and any pipeline revision.
7. **Capex pacing.** $206.9M deployed through June against a $483M FY2026 budget (43%) and a $2.7B 2026–2030 plan. Under-spend reads as rate base growth slipping.

**Operationally, what each requires:** Bull needs the revenue line *and* the cost line to cooperate — Q2 already showed revenue can miss (−3.2%) even with >90% of budgeted 2026 rate increases pre-approved, because usage and regulatory mechanism adjustments still swing. Bear doesn't require an operational failure at all; it only requires management to acknowledge the Q4 arithmetic.

**Historical calibration:** HTO is a 0.34-beta name with muted earnings-day moves — Q2 FY2026 (EPS +3.2% beat, revenue −3.2% miss) produced roughly +1.2% to +3.6% depending on the measurement window. EPS has beaten in ~62–75% of recent quarters. But the setup is different now: the stock is −6.5% over one month and −9.4% over the quarter with estimates cut 11%, so the asymmetry has shifted. A washed-out, de-risked setup usually dampens downside — except when the disappointment is guidance rather than the quarter, which is exactly the risk here.

## Catalyst checklist

1. **Adjusted EPS vs. $1.18–$1.20** (not vs. the $1.08 tail) — and more importantly, the implied 9M run-rate against the $3.08–$3.18 guide. A $1.20 print that reaffirms guidance without explaining Q4 is a hollow reaffirm.
2. **Underlying net income growth** — needs to hold near the +15–17% of 1H26 to validate that the EPS "decline" is purely dilution. Sub-10% net income growth breaks the bull thesis entirely.
3. **Quadvest dilution in dollars per share, plus FY2027 framing** — the gap between the Street's ~$2.84 and the 6–8% CAGR off $2.99 has to close somewhere on this call.
4. **Connecticut GRC settlement signal** — a settlement framework would de-risk the single largest pending revenue item ($28.8M) ahead of February 2027 rates.
5. **Equity/balance sheet reaffirmation** — explicit restatement of no common equity through YE2027 ex-M&A. Financial strength is the weakest part of the quant profile here.

## Caveats on the data

- Consensus is drawn from only 3–7 analysts and the published figures disagree materially ($1.08 to $1.21 EPS; $250M to $263M revenue). I have weighted toward the most recently updated sources.
- Q4 FY2025 adjusted EPS of $0.46 is my derivation (FY25 $2.99 anchor less 9M25 $2.53 reported), not a directly reported figure.
- One aggregator describes HTO as "unprofitable and cash-flow-negative." The company is clearly profitable ($107M TTM net income); negative free cash flow is real but is the normal condition of a utility deploying $483M of capex against $828M of revenue. Don't read it as a distress signal.
- Sources conflict on the Q2 earnings-day move (+1.2% vs. +3.6%) and on the CPUC PFAS decision date (year-end 2026 vs. Q4 2027).
