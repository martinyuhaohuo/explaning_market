## 2. The GAAP EPS print will look like a blowout miss. Ignore it.

On Sept 15–17, 2026 Piedmont priced **$230M of 2.875% exchangeable senior notes due Feb 2031** (79.0514 shares per $1,000 = **$12.65 exchange price**, ~31% above spot) and used proceeds plus revolver and cash to retire **all $287.3M of its 9.250% senior notes due 2028** — including a make-whole premium. It simultaneously repurchased **5,434,782 shares (~4.3% of the count) for ~$50M** from note purchasers.

The loss on early extinguishment is excluded from Core FFO but lands squarely in Q3 GAAP EPS. **Headline EPS could print materially worse than –$0.05 with zero read-through to the thesis.** Anchor on Core FFO, same-store cash NOI, and the guidance revision.

The refi economics are the under-appreciated leg of this print and were **not** in July guidance:

| | Annualized |
|---|---|
| 9.25% notes retired ($287.3M) | –$26.6M interest |
| 2.875% exchangeables ($230M) | +$6.6M |
| Incremental revolver (~$107M @ ~5.5%) | +$5.9M |
| **Net interest savings** | **~$14M ≈ $0.11/sh** |
| 2026 benefit (Sept 17 → YE, ~3.5 mo) | ~$4M ≈ **$0.03/sh**, almost all in Q4 |

Only ~2 weeks of benefit falls in Q3, so **the refi barely moves Q3 Core FFO — it moves the Q4 guide and the 2027 bridge.** Combined with a 4.3% lower share count, there is room to take FY26 to $1.53–$1.56 and still be conservative. Weighted average cost of debt should step down from 5.50% toward ~5.0–5.1%.

## 3. Key Metrics to Watch (ranked)

**1. FY26 Core FFO guidance revision — the single most important line.** Guidance has been raised two consecutive quarters ($1.49–$1.54 → $1.50–$1.55). A raise to $1.53–$1.56 just banks the refi. Leaving $1.50–$1.55 untouched implies ~$0.03 of operating erosion is offsetting the savings — a quiet negative.

**2. The leased-vs-economic occupancy gap — this *is* the Piedmont story.** 88.9% in-service leased but economic occupancy only just above 80%. Signed-not-commenced backlog was **$39M of annualized cash rent = ~570bp of future occupancy**. That gap is why the dividend was cut: ~$60M/yr redirected to tenant build-outs and free rent. Watch SNC dollars and commencement timing; management has guided occupancy to the "low 90s by 2027."

**3. Leasing volume and cash spreads.** Q2: 459ksf, +14.1% cash / +32.4% accrual, record net effective rent of $25.56/sf. Management flagged **>700ksf in the legal stage for Q3** and ~2M sf of outstanding proposals, with FY tracking the high end of 1.7–2.0M sf. The Q3'25 comp is a tough 724ksf. Sub-500ksf *with* a shrinking pipeline is the bear trigger; sub-500ksf with the pipeline intact is timing noise.

**4. Cash vs accrual same-store NOI divergence.** Cash SSNOI: +11.1% Q1, +9.0% Q2 (+10% 1H). Accrual: +1.9%, +2.8%. The cash/accrual spread is free-rent burn-off, not organic growth. FY guide of 5–8% on *both* bases implies 2H cash deceleration. **The real tell is accrual accelerating toward mid-single digits** — that's durable earnings power, and it's what lifts 2027.

**5. Leverage and the dividend clock.** Net debt/Core EBITDA 7.2x at Q2; management targeted **<7.0x by YE26**, ~6.5x in 2027–28, ~6x longer term. Dividend suspended since April 23, 2025, with reinstatement "no earlier than 2027." Any firming of timing or size re-rates the stock to a different buyer base — the biggest non-linear catalyst in the name.

**6. Capital allocation consistency.** Royal Lane (Dallas) land parcel was guided to close in Q3 for ~$12M net — did it? Acquisition pipeline is Dallas / Northern Virginia, 70–80% leased, high-single-digit going-in yields stabilizing to low double digits. Management said in July that buybacks were "not a priority," then bought $50M of stock in September. Expect that question on the call.

**What signals each on the call:** Bull — management quantifies the refi benefit and extends it into a 2027 Core FFO framework. Base — refi savings get described as "offsetting incremental free rent." Bear — occupancy commencement dates get vaguer and 2027 dividend language softens to "we continue to evaluate."

**Historical calibration:** 2026 reactions have been consistently positive and modest — Q1'26 +1.3% to +2.4%, Q2'26 **+4.4%** after-hours to $10.04 despite a GAAP EPS miss ($0.09 loss vs $0.03 expected). The market has repeatedly looked through GAAP noise to leasing and guidance. PDM has beaten only 3 of the last 8 quarters on the *GAAP* line — precisely why that line is not the variable that matters.

## 5. Catalyst Checklist

1. **FY26 Core FFO guide vs $1.50–$1.55** — does the ~$0.03 of refi savings plus 4.3% lower share count show up, or get absorbed? The clearest signal in the release.
2. **Signed-not-commenced backlog vs $39M / 570bp** — growing backlog = visible 2027 revenue; shrinking without an occupancy step-up = commencement slippage.
3. **Q3 leasing volume vs the >700ksf legal-stage pipeline** disclosed in July — conversion rate is the credibility test on the "2M sf FY" claim.
4. **Accrual same-store NOI** — needs to accelerate off +2.8%. Cash growth is a free-rent artifact; accrual is the real thing.
5. **Net debt/Core EBITDA vs the <7.0x YE target**, plus any change to dividend reinstatement language. This is the re-rating lever.

Office REITs remain the cheapest major REIT category at ~7.4x FFO. Targets are drifting up but ratings remain Neutral — nobody is defending an upgrade, which caps post-print sell-side amplification in the bull case.

**Bottom line:** the asymmetry is in the guidance line, not the quarter. Q3 Core FFO is near-fully determined by the 1H run-rate and should land $0.38–$0.39; the September refi is worth ~$0.11/share annualized but contributes almost nothing to Q3. A raise that banks the refi plus any firming of 2027 dividend language is the bull path. An unchanged guide after a $14M annual interest reduction is the signal that free rent and TI are eating the benefit — and that is the version of this print worth being short into.
