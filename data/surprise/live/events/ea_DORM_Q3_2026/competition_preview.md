> **The one-line setup:** Q2 looked spectacular ($3.08 adj. EPS vs. $1.89) almost entirely because of a one-time IEEPA tariff refund worth ~$1.18/share. Strip that out and Q2 gross margin was ~38%, not 46.1%. Q3 is the first clean quarter — it tells you whether the ~40% structural gross margin management promised for year-end is real, and whether the sharply reduced revenue guide (3–5% from 7–9%) is now conservative or still too high. The stock has already given back the entire Q2 pop (−11.9% post-earnings drift), so expectations are no longer elevated.

## 2. The Guidance Math (where the real risk sits)

FY2026 guidance after the Q2 cut: **net sales +3% to +5%**, **adj. diluted EPS $8.50–$8.80**, GAAP EPS $7.93–$8.23, tax rate 23.5%. FY2025 base: net sales $2,130.3M, adj. EPS $8.87.

| | Low end | High end |
|---|---|---|
| FY26 implied net sales | $2,194M | $2,237M |
| Less 1H'26 actual | $1,073.4M | $1,073.4M |
| **= 2H implied** | **$1,121M (+3.6% y/y)** | **$1,163M (+7.5% y/y)** |
| Less Q3 consensus $565M | $556M | $598M |
| **= Q4 implied** | **+3.4% y/y** | **+11.2% y/y** |

**Takeaway:** the top end of the revenue guide requires a double-digit Q4 — implausible given Q4'25 was $537.9M and pricing is being *reduced*. Either Q3 revenue needs to come in ahead of $565M, or the FY revenue range gets trimmed to the low end. A quiet narrowing to "3–4%" is my base case and is largely discounted.

**EPS is better calibrated.** FY $8.50–$8.80 less 1H adj. $4.64 leaves **$3.86–$4.16 for 2H**. Consensus Q3 of $1.97–$2.00 leaves Q4 at ~$1.86–$2.19 vs. $2.17 in Q4'25 — reasonable. My back-solve of 2H adj. operating margin from guidance is **14.4–14.9%**, bracketing the consensus-implied 14.7% for Q3. Consensus is sitting right on the guide, which means **the EPS bar is honest and the surprise is more likely to come from margin mix than from the headline**.

**The IEEPA accounting, cleanly:** Q2's refund added ~$44.4M to gross profit (+8.2 pts of margin, ~$1.18/share). Only ~**$0.30** of that was incremental to FY26 guidance — the recovery of costs originally booked in Q4 FY2025. The other ~$0.88 recovered costs incurred in 1H'26 and is therefore a wash within the year. So 1H adj. EPS of $4.64 is really ~$3.46 underlying. Anyone anchoring on "Dorman beats by 73%" is anchoring on an accounting event.

## 3. Key Metrics to Watch — Ranked

**1. Gross margin vs. ~38.5–39% consensus-implied, and the walk to 40% (highest importance).**
CFO Rayfield guided gross margin to **normalize around 40% by year-end**, explicitly framed as the structural base for 2027. Q1'26 printed 36.0%; Q2 was ~38% ex-refund. If Q3 lands at 39%+ with a credible path to 40% in Q4, the 2027 earnings base is defensible and the stock re-rates. A 37-handle without explanation breaks the 2027 bridge and the whole "$8.50+ is the floor" argument.

**2. Price vs. volume disclosure in Light Duty.**
Management is deliberately handing tariff savings back ("if tariff costs come down, we pass those back to our customers" — CEO Olsen). The bull case requires **volume** growth from new business wins and product launches to more than offset price givebacks. Light Duty was $430.3M in Q3'25 (+9%) and flat in Q2'26 against a tough comp. Mid-single-digit Light Duty growth in Q3 with positive volume = thesis intact. Flat-to-down = the 3–5% FY guide is at risk from the bottom, not just the top.

**3. Heavy Duty operating margin inflection.**
The most leveraged line in the model: 4.5% operating margin in Q3'25, 0.8% in Q1'26, 4.2% in Q2'26 on +7% sales. Management expects 2H growth *above* company average. Heavy Duty is a ~$230M revenue business running at ~4% margin against a Light Duty segment at ~24% — every 100 bps here is real but small; the signal value is about whether the Class 8 freight cycle has turned. Watch for any language on order rates and customer destocking.

**4. FY2026 guidance action (revenue range narrowing vs. EPS maintained).**
Best outcome: trim revenue to 3–4%, **raise or hold** adj. EPS $8.50–$8.80. That reinforces the "margin story, not volume story" framing the market has already accepted. Cutting EPS below $8.50 is the single most damaging outcome because it invalidates the 40%-gross-margin-by-year-end commitment.

**5. Free cash flow and capital deployment.**
FY2025 FCF was only $75.7M on $204M of net income — poor conversion, driven by tariff-related working capital. 1H'26 operating cash flow was $196.4M vs. $59.8M (flattered by the refund). Clean Q3 cash conversion matters for the M&A narrative: $931M liquidity, 0.69x net leverage, $363M remaining on the buyback authorization (runs to 2027), $98M repurchased in 1H'26 at ~$118–120 average. Olsen said sellers have "reset with more rational valuations." **A deal announcement alongside the print is a live possibility** and would be read as a vote of confidence at $124.

**6. Specialty Vehicle.**
Smallest and most discretionary ($50.4M in Q3'25, flat; −1% in Q2'26; guided *below* mid-single-digit for 2H). Margin here is volatile (26.1% in Q2'26 vs. 12.8% in Q3'25) and can swing consolidated results by a few cents. Low expectations; limited downside.

**What would need to happen, and what management would say:**

- **Bull:** Light Duty up mid-single digits with *volume* called out as the driver; gross margin at or through 40%; explicit confirmation that 40% is the 2027 base; possibly an M&A announcement. Listen for "ahead of plan on margin recovery."
- **Base:** Revenue roughly in line, margin in the high 38s–39s, management reiterating "on track to exit the year around 40%." The stock does little because this is exactly what the guide implies and what $124 prices.
- **Bear:** Hedged language on the 40% exit rate — "approaching," "trending toward," "by early 2027" instead of "by year-end." Any admission that competitive price matching (rather than tariff pass-back) is driving the price reductions would be the worst tell, because it makes the givebacks permanent rather than cost-linked.

## 5. Catalyst Checklist

1. **Gross margin ≥ 39%** — the single number that validates or breaks the ~40% structural base underpinning 2027 estimates. Below 38% and the forward P/E of ~15x is on the wrong denominator.
2. **FY2026 adj. EPS band held at $8.50–$8.80** — a cut here, after August's revenue cut, establishes a pattern of guide-downs and the multiple compresses regardless of the quarter itself.
3. **Light Duty volume vs. price split** — decides whether 3–5% FY revenue growth is achievable from the bottom. Wells Fargo cut its target to $150 from $160 on Oct 23 while staying Overweight, which reads as an estimate trim, not a thesis change; several other targets have been trimmed in recent weeks.
4. **Heavy Duty margin** — any move toward high-single-digit operating margin is the cheapest available upside optionality in the model and is not in consensus.
5. **M&A / buyback intensity** — $363M of authorization and 0.69x leverage at a stock 24% off its 52-week high. Aggressive Q3 repurchase disclosure or a bolt-on acquisition reframes the story from "margin defense" to "capital deployment."
