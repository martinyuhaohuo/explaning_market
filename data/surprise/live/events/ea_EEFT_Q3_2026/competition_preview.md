If Q3 prints the consensus $3.69, the **FY guide implies Q4 of $2.48–$2.96 vs. $2.39 LY (+4% to +24%)**. The top half of that is a stretch. Translation: *a Q3 in line plus a reaffirmed 10–15% is, functionally, a guide to the low end.* Management already pre-framed this on the Q2 call, saying Q2/Q3 would be a "smaller share of annual earnings than historical quarters" as digital mix shifts seasonality.

**The arithmetic of a $3.69:** diluted shares are roughly flat YoY (≈1.9M issued for CoreCard in Oct 2025 against $125–150M of buyback, with 400–500k more shares to retire in H2), and interest expense steps up ~$6M for the back half on the EUR700M bond. So +2% EPS requires **operating income up ~3–5% and adjusted EBITDA roughly flat-to-up**. Q2 delivered operating income **−14%** and adjusted EBITDA **−6%**. That is the crux of this print.

## 3. Key Metrics to Watch (ranked)

1. **Cross-Border Payments operating income.** Q2: revenue −4%, op income **−34%**, adj. EBITDA −31% — this single segment is the whole bear case. Watch for the rate of decline to compress toward −10/−15%. Q3 2025 base: $59.3M op income on $452.4M revenue (13.1% margin); Q2 2026 ran 9.9%.
2. **Consolidated adjusted EBITDA vs. ~$245M (flat YoY).** The cleanest single read on whether the Q2 margin break was a one-quarter event or the new run-rate.
3. **Payments Infrastructure margin, not revenue.** Q2 was revenue +11% but op income only +2% — growth is being bought with merchant-acquiring and CoreCard mix plus cost inflation. Revenue growth here will look fine; *decremental margin is the tell.* Also: ATM count has flattened (57,814 installed, +1% YoY in Q2 vs +4% in Q3 2025).
4. **Peak ATM/travel season outcome.** Management guided cautiously on Q2's call: U.S.-to-Europe bookings 5–8% below the peak 2025 window, European travelers "more selective," World Cup redirecting European travel to the U.S., with a "more resilient second part" of the season expected. Macro backdrop is supportive on volume (European international arrivals +5% YTD 2026; Greece +38%, Italy +21%) but the question is *spend per transaction*, not footfall.
5. **Mexico corridor inflection.** Weller said U.S.–Mexico was down 16–17% and "starting to change direction," with "a little bit of improvement" entering Q3. Independent data is mixed-to-negative: **Banxico remittances to Mexico fell 3.6% YoY in August 2026** ($5.452B) — the first annual drop since January, and −10.8% vs. August 2024 — even though the first eight months of 2026 were +2.2%. The 1% U.S. excise tax on cash-funded remittances (effective Jan 1, 2026) structurally disadvantages the cash-heavy Mexico corridor where Ria is most exposed.
6. **epay's transaction/revenue divergence.** Q2: revenue +5% on transactions **−11%** (low-value mix shift in APAC). Fine while digital content and gaming carry mix; a revenue-side crack would be new. Watch real-money-gaming certifications (Marker Trax, Koin) targeted for year-end.
7. **Digital accelerators.** 26% of revenue, +31% in Q2, +35% YTD vs. the 23% Investor Day framework. The bull case needs this to stay above framework; management has already flagged the CoreCard comp helps.
8. **Quality of earnings.** The GAAP-to-adjusted gap widened to $1.11 in Q2 ($1.71 GAAP vs $2.82 adjusted, 39% of adjusted) from $0.87 in Q3 2025. A further widening invites multiple compression even on an adjusted beat.

## 4. Scenario Analysis

| Scenario | Revenue | Adj. EPS | Key Driver | Stock Reaction |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Bull** (25%) | $1.20–1.22B | **$3.85–4.05** | Cross-Border revenue flat-to-positive and op income decline narrows to <15%; adj. EBITDA +4–6%; travel season beat; FY guide narrowed to **upper half** of 10–15% | **+10% to +16%** |
| **Base** (45%) | $1.16–1.19B | **$3.55–3.75** | Payments Infrastructure +7% with flat margins; epay +4%; Cross-Border −4% with op income −20%; adj. EBITDA flat; **FY reaffirmed at 10–15%, commentary points to low end** | **−2% to +5%** |
| **Bear** (30%) | $1.13–1.16B | **$3.20–3.45** | Cross-Border op income down >30% again; ATM season disappoints on spend-per-transaction; epay revenue follows transactions lower; **FY guide cut to below 10% or "low end of 10–15%" made explicit** | **−10% to −18%** |

**What each needs operationally / what management would say**

- **Bull:** Ria digital transactions (7.9M, +33% in Q2) scaling fast enough to offset cash-corridor erosion *in dollars, not just units*; Mexico explicitly "returned to growth in September"; CoreCard deal count above the "five, six deals" disclosed in July; an incremental buyback authorization at 6x earnings. Listen for a *numeric* Q4 bridge — management volunteering the Q4 math is the strongest bull tell.
- **Base:** Reaffirmation without a Q4 bridge; "stabilizing," "trending as expected"; digital accelerators re-anchored to the 23% framework. This is a survivable print the market likely shrugs at or mildly rewards given positioning.
- **Bear:** Any variant of "we now expect to finish below the low end," a second consecutive quarter of negative adjusted EBITDA growth, or a Cross-Border impairment/restructuring charge. Also bearish: heavy framing of the 1% excise tax as structural rather than transitional.

**Historical calibration:** EEFT has beaten consensus EPS in only **3 of the last 8 quarters**. Q2 2026 (Jul 30) — miss, **−8.4% close, −10.1% intraday low** from $83.67. Q4 2025 (Feb 12, 2026) — EPS miss, stock dipped. Q1 2026 (Apr 29) — beat on both, **+3%**. Q3 2025 (Oct 23) — EPS beat on a revenue miss, which the market tolerated. The asymmetry in the reaction history is stark: **beats get +3%, misses get −8% to −10%.**

## 5. Catalyst Checklist

1. **Cross-Border Payments operating income vs. $59.3M LY** — needs to show the −34% Q2 decline was the trough. This is the single variable with the most explanatory power for the stock.
2. **FY2026 adjusted EPS guidance language** — reaffirm 10–15% vs. cut vs. explicit "low end." Guidance survival matters more than the Q3 EPS line, because a reaffirmation implicitly promises a Q4 of $2.48+ against a $2.39 comp.
3. **Consolidated adjusted EBITDA ≥ ~$245M** — the flat-YoY line that separates "Q2 was a blip" from "the margin story has changed." Paired with Payments Infrastructure decremental margin.
4. **Peak-season ATM commentary** — did the Q2-flagged 5–8% booking shortfall and World Cup redirect actually show up in Q3 transaction economics, and what's the read-through to Q4/FY2027?
5. **Capital allocation at a 6x multiple** — the $125–150M annual buyback range was already exhausted in H1, with only 400–500k shares planned for H2 and *no* incremental repurchase baked into guidance. An upsized authorization would be a credible management signal at these levels; silence will be read as leverage constraint (total debt $2,654.0M at Q2, up from $2,021.8M at YE2025, against $2,220.7M of cash including ATM cash).

The re-rating requires Cross-Border op income to visibly inflect, and nothing in the August Banxico data argues it already has.
