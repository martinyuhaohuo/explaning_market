### 1. The setup in one paragraph

TRU enters the print with a **policy overhang, a deteriorating mortgage tape, and a consensus pinned to the top of its own guidance** — an unusually asymmetric combination. The stock crashed to ~$65 in early October on Bloomberg's report that FHFA Director Bill Pulte will move Fannie/Freddie from tri-merge to **bi-merge** credit reports, then rebounded ~20% into the print (~$78–79, vs. 52-week range $60.96–$89.12). Meanwhile Equifax's October 20 print showed the U.S. mortgage market **deteriorated sharply in the back half of Q3** (30-year fixed +~75bps since July), costing EFX ~$20M in-quarter and an $80M cut to FY mortgage revenue. TRU guided Q3 assuming only *low-double-digit* H2 inquiry declines with the 10-year at 4.7%. That assumption looks stale.

### 3. Key metrics to watch (ranked)

1. **Non-mortgage organic growth (guide: ~+6%, at/slightly above Q2 trajectory).** This is the whole bull case now. If mortgage is structurally impaired, the multiple rests on Financial Services ex-mortgage, fraud/identity (TruValidate), and analytics. Q2 was U.S. Financial Services +18%, Emerging Verticals +9%.
2. **Mortgage revenue and inquiry decline.** Guide assumed mortgage +28% reported (+6% ex-FICO royalty) on mid-to-high-single-digit FY inquiry declines. EFX's ex-FICO mortgage growth of just **+1%** vs. plan is the read-across. Watch whether TRU's inquiry decline came in worse than low-double-digits in September.
3. **Management's quantified bi-merge exposure.** TRU has publicly framed **~$100M+** of revenue (≈2% of FY revenue) tied to GSE tri-merge after stripping FICO pass-through, and argued real-world utilization settles above a naive two-thirds. William Blair models even a full one-third loss as a **low-to-mid-single-digit % EBITDA hit**. A crisp, defensible number on the call matters more than the quarter.
4. **Consumer Interactive.** Declined 3% in Q2 (indirect growth offset by direct pullback). A third consecutive decline, or a step-down, removes a stabilizer.
5. **FY2026 guidance action.** EFX held FY EPS ($8.50) despite cutting mortgage revenue, using cost offsets. Whether TRU can do the same — hold $4.75–4.83 while trimming revenue — is the single biggest swing factor for the stock.
6. **Margin bridge.** The 35.2–35.4% guide embeds (80bps) FICO royalty pass-through and (60bps) acquisition integration against +20–40bps underlying expansion. Q4 was supposed to be the margin-acceleration quarter as low-margin mortgage mix shrinks — ironically, a weak mortgage market helps here.
7. **FCF / leverage / buyback.** 1H FCF ~$325M, leverage 2.6x vs. <2.5x target, $150M repurchased YTD with H2 "at least comparable." At ~15.6x forward earnings, buyback acceleration would be a credibility signal.
8. **OneTru migration.** 60% of U.S. batch and 30% of online migrated at Q2; completion targeted by year-end. Slippage would be a soft negative on the 2027 cost story.

### 5. Catalyst checklist

1. **Adj. EPS vs. the ~$1.23 whisper (not the $1.21 published consensus)** — the guide ceiling is the real bar; printing $1.19–$1.21 reads as a stealth miss.
2. **FY2026 adj. EPS: held at $4.75–$4.83, or cut?** EFX held its number despite a mortgage revenue cut; TRU failing to do the same breaks the "diversified, self-helping compounder" narrative.
3. **A quantified, bounded bi-merge impact with mitigation roadmap.** Analysts need a number to model. The absence of one is itself a negative — it leaves GS's $68 target framework (vs. the $89.29 street mean) as the anchor.
4. **Non-mortgage organic growth ≥6%** — confirms the ex-mortgage engine (fraud, identity, India +8%, Canada +10%, Mexico ahead of plan) is intact.
5. **CFO succession update.** Todd Cello departs after 29 years (CFO through 12/31/26, advisor to 3/1/27). Naming a credible successor removes a small but real discount; silence into a guidance cut compounds it.
