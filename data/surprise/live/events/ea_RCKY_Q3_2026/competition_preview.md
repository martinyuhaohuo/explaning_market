## 1. The setup in one paragraph

Rocky Brands goes into this print with genuine operating momentum (Q2 revenue +12%, XTRATUF now the largest brand, inventory the cleanest since the Honeywell-brands acquisition) but a **broken scoreboard**. IEEPA tariff refunds have made published consensus nearly useless as a beat/miss benchmark — Q2 adjusted EPS of $1.90 landed $1.35–$1.55 above a $0.35–$0.55 consensus. The $1.50 Q3 consensus implies ~12.9% operating margin, roughly 290bp above my base case, which suggests the sell side is either embedding a larger refund benefit than management flagged (~$2M) or has simply not recalibrated. **The tradable variable is not the headline EPS — it is the ex-tariff gross margin line and the Q4 revenue trajectory.**

## 4. Key metrics to watch (ranked)

1. **Gross margin excluding tariff items.** The whole bull case rests on Q2's 38.7% adjusted being the trough. Low-40s = thesis intact. Sub-40% = pricing isn't offsetting component/freight inflation, and the ~$3.26 ex-tariff EPS guide is at risk.
2. **Q4 revenue trajectory / FY ~8.5% reaffirmation.** Q4 consensus $149.8M requires lapping a +30.8% retail comp from Q4'25. Wholesale faces an easy comp (−2.1%), so the mix of the beat matters more than the total.
3. **Wholesale segment margin.** Down 430bp in Q2 to 36.3% on sourcing-optimization costs and shelf-space incentives. Is that one-time investment or structural give-back?
4. **XTRATUF.** Guided to ~30% annual growth with "the largest set of pre-book orders in the brand's history" for H2. This is the single largest swing factor in the revenue line and the main multiple-support argument.
5. **Remaining IEEPA receivable + incremental claims.** $16.8M outstanding at 6/30/26 (post-period $8.2M collected by Aug 4). Watch cash conversion and whether management quantifies any *additional* refund claims on finally-liquidated entries.
6. **Debt and leverage.** $122.4M at 6/30/26 (revolver $101.3M, term loan $22.6M), down 7.6% Y/Y. Refund cash was earmarked partly for debt paydown, partly for DC expansion and Dominican Republic raw-material inventory. Sub-$110M would be a clean positive.
7. **2027 tariff/pricing plan.** New Section 301 duties don't hit the P&L until very late 2026 / early 2027. Management deferred 2027 pricing decisions pending another expected 301 round.

**What each would require operationally**

- *Bull:* Retail/DTC holds mid-to-high-teens growth with 46%+ segment margin, wholesale returns to 38–40% margin as sourcing-optimization costs anniversary, and management frames FY27 as pricing-neutral on tariffs. Commentary tell: explicit "low-40s achieved" plus a quantified FY27 price increase already accepted by key accounts.
- *Base:* Revenue in line, margins progressing but freight surcharges and digital-ad spend keep opex near 31%+. Commentary tell: "on track," no change to the ~$3.26 ex-tariff EPS guide, 2027 tariff answer remains "we're waiting to see."
- *Bear:* Wholesale growth decelerates below 3% as independents and key accounts manage inventory into a softer consumer, and the Q2 customer-bankruptcy write-off ($1.1M) proves to be the first of several. Commentary tell: hedging on the ~8.5% FY growth number, or introducing a FY27 gross-margin headwind without an offset.

## 6. Catalyst checklist

1. **Ex-tariff gross margin vs. the "low-40%" H2 guide** — the single cleanest read on whether pricing is beating cost inflation. *Note: it was not fully explicit on the Q2 call whether "low-40s" was stated gross or net of refunds; press-release framing of this distinction is itself a catalyst.*
2. **Reaffirmation of ~8.5% FY26 revenue growth and ~$3.26 ex-tariff EPS** — the buy side needs both, not just the reported ~$5.00 number, which is tariff-inflated and non-repeatable.
3. **Wholesale segment margin recovery** — 430bp of Q2 erosion labeled "investment." Proof or walk-back.
4. **IEEPA refund cash collection and use** — debt paydown pace vs. reinvestment; any incremental claim quantification post-*Learning Resources* / *Freestyle*.
5. **First real 2027 tariff-and-pricing framework** — the bridge from a tariff-boosted 2026 to a clean 2027 base. Absence of a framework is itself a negative, given the stock's optically cheap ~12x headline P/E masks ~14x on ex-tariff earnings.

## 8. Assumptions and caveats

- Consensus is thinly covered and demonstrably miscalibrated to tariff accounting; the $1.50 figure is from a single aggregator dated Oct 20, 2026. No buy-side whisper number was locatable for this name.
- My Q3 build assumes a ~$2M IEEPA refund benefit in gross margin, 22% tax, 7.55M diluted shares, and ~$1.9M interest expense. If the refund benefit is larger, reported EPS rises without any operating improvement.
- FY2026 revenue math: $481.98M FY25 × 1.085 ≈ $523M; H1'26 actual $242.8M implies ~$280M H2, versus $279.6M of consensus — i.e., consensus revenue is effectively the guidance, leaving little cushion.
- Tariff policy remains the dominant exogenous variable in both directions: refund recovery is a 2026 cash/P&L tailwind, and the Section 301 successor regime is a 2027 cost headwind that has not yet been priced into guidance.
