## 1. The Setup in One Paragraph

Nucor guided Q3 to **$5.55–$5.65** on September 17 — roughly 9% below the then-prevailing ~$6.17–$6.20 consensus — and the stock fell 3.7% after hours and ~6% the next session (from a $265.14 close to ~$248.53 intraday). The stock has since fully recovered to new highs on surging HRC spot prices. That leaves an awkward setup: **published consensus ($5.71–$5.83) now sits *above* company guidance**, which only happens when the buy side is underwriting a beat-your-own-guide outcome. Nucor delivered exactly that last quarter — guided $4.70–$4.80 on June 17 and printed $5.04, ~5% above the high end. The real question on Monday is not Q3 at all; it is **whether Q4 commentary validates spot HRC near $1,290/st as a 2027 earnings base, or frames it as a tariff-driven supply squeeze that fades.**

## 3. Key Metrics to Watch — Ranked

**1. Steel Mills average selling price per ton and metal spread** *(highest importance)*
Zacks models ~$1,157/ton, +11.5% y/y. Guidance cited "higher average selling prices across all major product categories with stable volumes, partially offset by higher costs of products sold." Scrap was soft-to-sideways through Q3 while HRC rose — spread should expand. Any ASP miss here is the single most damaging outcome because it undercuts the Q4/2027 price-durability thesis, not just the quarter.

**2. Q4 2026 directional segment guidance and 2027 framing**
Spot HRC is ~$1,290/st (SMU late-October average), with Nucor's own CSP lifted to $1,255/st on Oct 19 (from $1,240 on Oct 12) and West Coast JV California Steel Industries at $1,320/st. Those hikes land in **Q4, not Q3** — contract lag means Q4 realized pricing should step up again. The market needs management to say so.

**3. Steel Products earnings and backlog**
Guided up on *both* volume and price — the only segment guided higher on both. Watch joist/deck, towers & structures (Indiana, Utah), and the data-center/grid demand narrative. Structural consumption was running ~+15% YTD at Q2.

**4. Raw Materials magnitude of decline**
Guided down on "lower pricing and shipments," with Q2 commentary flagging lower scrap pricing *and higher iron ore costs* (DRI). This is a known negative; the question is size. Note October scrap turned up (+$20/gt on shredded/P&S/HMS, +$10/gt busheling), which helps DJJ in Q4 but raises Q4 mill input costs.

**5. Corporate/eliminations and clean-EPS quality**
Two Q2 tailwinds do **not** repeat: a ~$130M cost-of-goods refund for prior-period raw material procurement, and a ~$61M (~$0.20/sh) non-cash Helion valuation gain. Guidance explicitly embeds higher corporate expense. Scrutinize any headline beat for one-timers — a "beat" built on another investment mark will not be paid for.

**6. Volumes / shipment growth tracking**
FY26 shipment growth guided 5–10%, with Q2 commentary pointing to "closer to the higher end."

**7. Capital allocation and capex**
Q3 buybacks already disclosed: ~2.03M shares at $247.04 average; ~$1.36B returned YTD. Policy is ≥40% of net earnings annually. FY26 capex $2.5B (~60% growth). West Virginia sheet mill: melt shop commissioning began July, commercial ramp early 2027, ~50% utilization by YE2027. A 2027 capex preview is a plausible surprise item in either direction.

## 5. Catalyst Checklist

1. **Q3 EPS vs. ~$5.85 (not $5.71).** The Zacks number is stale relative to the guide-down-then-rally sequence. A $5.71 print will screen as a "beat" and likely still trade flat-to-down.
2. **Steel Mills realized ASP/ton and the implied Q4 exit rate.** This is the line item that supports or breaks the ~$20 FY2027 EPS consensus. KeyBanc frames the stock at ~7.0–7.5x 2027 EV/EBITDA — a multiple only defensible if $1,250+/st HRC persists.
3. **Q4 segment-level direction, especially whether Products is guided higher again.** The buy side wants Q4 EPS framed at or above Q3 — which would be the first time in years Nucor guides a seasonally weak quarter flat-to-up.
4. **Import share commentary.** Imports have fallen from ~25% share at the start of 2025 to ~14%. Management's read on whether Section 232 keeps that structural is the core 2027 variable. Any suggestion of import re-entry or lead-time normalization is the cleanest bear trigger.
5. **Capital returns pace + 2027 capex.** Q3 buybacks at $247.04 average were below the current price — a disclosed acceleration supports the floor; a 2027 capex step-up above $2.5B pressures the FCF story.
