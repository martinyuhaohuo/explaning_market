## 2. Key Metrics to Watch — Ranked

### 1. Average OEC on Rent and On-Rent Yield *(highest importance)*
This is the earnings engine and the cleanest read on the T&D cycle.

| | Q3'25 | Q2'26 | Q3'26 Watch Level |
|---|---|---|---|
| Avg. OEC on rent | $1,262.5M | $1,365.7M (+13.1% Y/Y) | **>$1.40B** |
| On-rent yield | 38.2% | 39.4% (+80bps) | **≥39.5%** |
| Fleet utilization | 79.3% | 81.6% | **≥80%** |
| Ending OEC | $1,621.7M | $1,679.3M (record) | **>$1.70B** |

Management explicitly said rental entered Q3 with OEC on rent and utilization *above* prior-year levels. If yield comes in flat-to-down sequentially, the "transmission mix is structurally higher-yielding" argument — a core bull pillar — takes real damage.

### 2. FY26 Guidance Treatment
Raised at Q2 to **$2.10–2.20B revenue / $437.5–455M adj. EBITDA**. Three outcomes, in descending order of frequency: reaffirm, narrow to the upper half (bullish), or trim to the low end (bearish). Given the Q4 back-end load, a *narrowing toward the low end* would be read as a de facto cut.

### 3. STEM Sales Backlog
$322.5M at Q2-end (down $89M sequentially on record deliveries), but management said it had already grown to **>$340M in early Q3**, with June quoting activity +26% Y/Y and "strong order growth so far in Q3." Anything below ~$340M contradicts guidance given 11 weeks ago and would be the quarter's most damaging disclosure.

### 4. Net Leverage and Levered Free Cash Flow
Net leverage: 4.53x (Q3'25) → 4.31x (YE'25) → 4.02x (Q1'26) → **3.85x (Q2'26)**. Target: "meaningfully below 4.0x" by YE26, 3.0x in 2027. Watch for **≤3.70x**. The FY commitment to >$50M levered FCF is the governor here — cash was just **$10.3M** at Q2-end against $1,673M total debt, so FCF delivery is concentrated in H2 and is not optional.

### 5. Pull-Forward Quantification
CFO Eperjesy flagged "tens of millions" of third-party/used equipment sales and RPO buyouts moved from H2 into Q2. The quarter hinges on whether that was $20M or $60M. A revenue miss that management attributes cleanly to already-disclosed timing is survivable; one that reveals the pull-forward was larger than flagged is not.

- **Base:** The quarter looks like management described it 11 weeks ago. Revenue sequentially down from $563.4M, EBITDA sequentially down from $116.8M, both up Y/Y, guide intact. Language: "tracking in line," "reaffirming."
- **Bear:** Watch for hedging on the Q4 ramp — "weighted toward the back half of Q4," "customer delivery schedules," or any softening on the >$50M levered FCF commitment. A guide "narrowed to $2.10–2.15B" is a cut regardless of how it's phrased.

**Historical calibration — this matters:**

- **StockStory** flags that CTOS "has missed Wall Street's revenue estimates multiple times over the last two years," though the last two quarters beat.

The asymmetry is unfavorable: beats get faded, misses get punished.

## 4. Catalyst Checklist

1. **Adj. EBITDA vs. ~$105M derived consensus** — the only P&L line the buy-side underwrites. Below $100M breaks the H2 math behind the FY guide.
2. **FY26 guidance language** — reaffirm vs. narrow vs. trim. With ~$600M of implied Q4 revenue, any wobble on the Q4 ramp is the highest-leverage sentence on the call.
3. **On-rent yield ≥39.5% with avg OEC >$1.40B** — validates or invalidates the "transmission mix lifts yield structurally" thesis. Flat yield at rising OEC = volume without price.
4. **STEM backlog ≥$340M** — management pre-announced this level. Missing a self-set bar is read as a demand inflection, not a timing issue.
5. **Net leverage ≤3.70x plus explicit FY levered FCF reaffirmation** — the deleveraging path to 3.0x in 2027 is the equity re-rating story. $10.3M of cash leaves no margin for a soft H2 conversion.
6. **First 2027 framing** — EPA 2027 NOx standards ($4,500–7,000/unit non-conformance penalties) create a chassis pre-buy dynamic that could pull 2027 demand into 2026. Any quantification of a pre-buy tailwind, or of the margin cost of mitigating it, is new information.

## 6. Modeling Traps — Read Before You Plug

1. **Segment reporting changed in 2026.** Prior-year ERS / TES / APS was replaced by **SER** (Specialty Equipment Rentals) and **STEM** (Specialty Truck Equipment & Manufacturing). Many data feeds have not restated history — segment Y/Y comparisons pulled from third-party sources are unreliable.
2. **Segment revenue is reported gross of eliminations.** Q2'26 SER $221.0M + STEM $437.8M = $658.8M against consolidated revenue of $563.4M. FY guidance of SER $850–875M + STEM $1,630–1,700M sums to $2.48–2.58B against a consolidated guide of $2.10–2.20B — roughly **$380M of intersegment eliminations**, not a guidance inconsistency. Use external-customer revenue (Q2: SER $218.8M, STEM $344.6M) for build-ups.
3. **Rental fleet capex is stepping down**, not up: FY26 net rental fleet investment of $170–200M vs. >$250M in 2025, on a fleet averaging just over 3 years old. This is the mechanism behind the FCF and deleveraging guide — if capex guidance moves *up* again, the >$50M levered FCF commitment is at risk even on a good operating quarter.
4. **Mid-single-digit net OEC growth** is the FY frame. Reconcile any Q3 ending-OEC figure against that, not against the 13% avg-OEC-on-rent growth rate, which is a utilization effect as much as a fleet-size effect.

### Bottom Line

Management pre-wired this quarter three months ago: sequentially lower than Q2, up Y/Y, with the Q2 pull-forward explicitly flagged. That makes Q3 a **low-information quarter on the P&L and a high-information quarter on guidance and fleet yield**. The bear case does not require a demand problem — it only requires the Q4 ramp to look harder than the guide implies. Given a strong URI read-across raising the bar, no short base, and a track record of faded beats, I'd watch rather than position into the print, with the Tuesday-morning call as the actual tradeable event.
