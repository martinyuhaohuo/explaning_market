## 1. The setup in one paragraph

HRI is the first truly *clean* comp quarter post-H&E (closed June 2, 2025), so the +20–33% headline growth of the last three prints collapses to mid-single-digit organic. The stock is down ~21% from its post-Q2 level (~$163 on July 28) and ~32% off February highs, even as **United Rentals just posted a record Q3 and raised full-year guidance** (Oct 21) — a strong read-through HRI equity has not been given credit for. The distinguishing features are HRI-specific: 3.95x net leverage, greater local/SMB exposure inherited from H&E, and a margin line still carrying integration drag plus a guided ~1pt full-year fuel/transportation headwind. With equity only ~35% of enterprise value, each $100M of EBITDA is worth ~13% of the market cap — which is why this name routinely moves 7–14% on earnings.

**My core call:** revenue risk is skewed *up* versus the published number; **EPS risk is skewed down**; and the stock trades on FY guidance and 2027 framing, not the print (true in each of the last three quarters).

## 3. The modeling tension — and where I disagree with consensus

**Revenue: guidance implies more than consensus does.** H1'26 ERR was $2,053M ($981M + $1,072M). FY guidance therefore implies H2 ERR of **$2,322–2,422M** vs. $2,161M in H2'25 (Q3 $1,122M + Q4 $1,039M) — i.e. **+7.5% to +12% YoY**. Applying last year's H2 seasonal split (Q3 = 51.9% of H2) gives **Q3'26 ERR of ~$1.21B at the guidance low end and ~$1.26B at the high end** — materially above the ~$1.16–1.17B that a $1.35B total-revenue consensus implies. Either consensus is modeling a sharp drop in equipment-sales/other revenue (plausible: HRI has been selling down the acquired H&E fleet, incl. $70M used sales in Q3'25), or the rental line is set up to beat.

**EPS: the bar is higher than it looks.** Backing into $2.92 on ~33.5M shares gives adj. net income ~$98M vs ~$73M in Q3'25 — ~+$33M pretax. With D&A rising on a larger fleet (+$15M or so) and interest roughly flat, that requires **adj. EBITDA near $595–600M, or ~43.5% margin — up ~120bps YoY**. That demands full cost-synergy capture *and* relief on the fuel/transport inflation management explicitly guided as a ~1pt full-year headwind. Achievable, not comfortable.

**Net:** a print of ~$1.38B revenue / ~$585M EBITDA / ~$2.75 EPS would read as "revenue beat, EPS miss" — a configuration this market has punished.

## 4. Key metrics to watch (ranked)

1. **FY2026 ERR and adj. EBITDA guidance** — the single determinant of the reaction. Three straight quarters traded on the guide, not the quarter (Q4'25: EPS beat $2.07 vs $1.87, stock **-14.1%**; Q2'26: EPS beat 95%, stock **-7.3%** intraday-basis). Watch whether the $4.375–4.475B ERR range is narrowed up, held, or trimmed.
2. **Adj. EBITDA margin and DOE% of ERR** — Q3'25 margin 42.3% with DOE at 41.6% of ERR; Q2'26 DOE spiked to 45.8% (from 43.6%) on acquisition costs, transportation and immature greenfields. Q3 is the quarter the $125M cost-synergy program — which management is pulling forward to **year-end 2026**, ahead of the original 3-year plan — should become visible. Anything below ~42.5% margin undercuts the whole synergy narrative.
3. **Dollar utilization** — 39.9% in Q3'25; Q2'26 improved 100bps YoY to 39.3%. A print of 40.3%+ confirms the fleet is being absorbed; sub-40% says HRI is buying revenue with capex.
4. **Local/SMB vs. national accounts** — the H&E-specific risk. HRI has flagged softness in local markets "where H&E had been overweighted" for four quarters. URI called local markets merely "stable" (a couple points of growth) with mega projects carrying the load. Any sign HRI's local book is inflecting with rate cuts is the real upside surprise.
5. **Leverage and capex discipline** — 3.95x at Q2 (vs 3.76x LY, 2.53x in Q1'25). Gross capex was raised $450M at the midpoint in July. The buy-side wants growth capex *and* a path below 3.5x; delivering both requires the H2 FCF ramp ($250–350M FY vs $202M in H1).

Language to fear: "moderating demand in certain local markets," "pushing out," "lower fixed cost absorption." On 5.8x EV/EBITDA, a $75M FY EBITDA cut is ~$435M of EV — roughly 10% of the equity.

## 6. Catalyst checklist

1. **FY26 ERR guidance vs. $4.375–4.475B** — a narrow-up signals Q4 ERR ~$1.15B+ and de-risks 2027 consensus. A trim is the bear trigger regardless of the Q3 print.
2. **Adj. EBITDA margin vs. ~43.5% implied by consensus EPS** — the actual bar. Expect management to quantify realized cost synergies against the $125M target and the ~1pt fuel/transport headwind.
3. **Dollar utilization vs. 39.9% LY** — the cleanest single read on whether the 30%-larger fleet is earning its keep.
4. **Local/SMB commentary and any rate-cut-driven inflection** — the H&E thesis stands or falls here; URI flagged expected local acceleration post-rate-cuts but wouldn't time it.
5. **Capital allocation** — FCF trajectory toward $250–350M, deleveraging path from 3.95x, and whether buybacks are even on the table (dividend is ~$2.80/sh, ~$94M, comfortably covered). Any early 2027 capex framing is a swing factor.
