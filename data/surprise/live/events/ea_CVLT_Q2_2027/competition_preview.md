## 2. What to Watch — Ranked

**1. Net new subscription ARR (the only number that matters).** Q1 added $39.6M, down from $48.5M in Q4 FY26 and ~$50M in prior quarters. Management framed FY27 as **~$190M net new**, with "a modest step-up in fiscal Q2" and the majority H2-weighted. That sets the Q2 bar at **~$42–46M** (subscription ARR ~$1,096–1,100M). Below $40M and the implied H2 requirement jumps to ~$55M+/quarter — a guidance cut in waiting.

**2. FY27 subscription ARR guide ($1.20–1.21B).** Reiterate = relief. Trim to low end = the Jan 2026 playbook repeats. Raise = the bull case.

**3. Billings.** Missed consensus materially in *both* Q3 FY26 and Q1 FY27. It is the metric that broke the stock in January. A third consecutive miss will be read as demand, not optics.

**4. SaaS ARR and SaaS margin.** $424.3M (+38%) in Q1; needs >$500M by March. SaaS revenue crossed $100M/quarter and SaaS gross margin crossed 70%. Watch for mix-shift commentary — Q3 FY26's blowup was explicitly blamed on SaaS's lower ASPs diluting net new ARR dollars.

**5. Hardware/supply constraints and Q3 guide.** Q1 flagged "mid-single-digit sequential pressure on term length" from hybrid-environment hardware availability, plus lighter-than-normal perpetual-to-subscription conversions and a few million in FX drag on ARR. Is this one quarter or structural? Q3 subscription revenue guide of ~$285–290M keeps FY intact.

**6. NDR (114% in Q1) and the Azure / Microsoft 365 ramp.** Management explicitly *excluded* meaningful contribution from the native Azure program; the Oct 15 M365 security launch (450M+ paid commercial seats) is free optionality. Any quantification = new narrative.

**What each requires operationally.** *Bull:* term-license net new ARR turns up sequentially (it was flat in Q1), identity/security holds >⅓ of net new ARR, and the on-prem hardware timing drag reverses as deferred deals close. *Base:* SaaS carries the quarter while term stays flat — the Q1 pattern, one quarter further along. *Bear:* SaaS keeps diluting net new ARR dollars while term stalls, and management resets the back half.

**Management tells.** Listen for whether the H2 net new ARR ramp is described in *committed pipeline* terms versus "expected seasonality." Re-characterizing the hardware issue as "demand timing" rather than "supply" is the bear tell. Any new disclosure framework around ARR would read as defensive given the pending litigation.

## 4. Catalyst Checklist

1. **Net new subscription ARR vs. ~$45M** — the dollar figure, not the growth rate. Determines whether the $190M FY27 bridge survives.
2. **FY27 subscription ARR guide** — reiterate $1.20–1.21B or trim. A trim is a −20% event given the stock's recovery.
3. **Billings vs. consensus** — third strike risk; this metric, not revenue, drives the sell-side models.
4. **Q3 subscription revenue guide (~$285–290M needed)** plus SaaS ARR tracking to >$500M.
5. **Hardware/supply narrative** — bounded to H1 or extending; and whether deferred on-prem deals closed in Q2.
6. **Azure native integration / M365 monetization** — excluded from guidance, so any quantification is incremental.

## 5. Trading Setup

- **Recent action:** +12–14% over the past month, **+33.9% YTD**, but **−6.5% over the past year** — the stock has round-tripped to roughly where it traded before the October 2025 print. It is **+39% off the July 28 post-print close of $120.02** and ~5% below the 52-week high of $175.03.
- **Positioning risk:** the recovery has happened *into* the print, not after it. The stock enters at ~31x forward earnings having already discounted a clean quarter.
- **Sell-side is behind, not ahead:** 11 Buy / 8 Hold / 0 Sell with an average target of ~$163–165 — **below spot**. Recent raises are catch-up moves to levels at or near the current price: Jefferies **Hold $170** (from $130, Oct 26), Cantor **Neutral $171** (from $132, Oct 26), Baird **$180** (from $160, Oct 19), Mizuho **Outperform $185** (from $175, Oct 15). Limited "upgrade fuel" on a beat; ample downgrade fuel on an ARR miss.
- **Implied move: not verified.** MarketChameleon's straddle data is paywalled and CVLT did not appear in TipRanks' Oct 26–30 implied-move table. One quote showed ~49% IV with a 16-DTE expected move of only ~2.9%, which is inconsistent with an earnings-inclusive straddle and looks stale. **Based on realized history and that IV level, I'd expect the straddle to price an 11–14% move — confirm before sizing.** If the market is pricing materially *less* than ~11%, options are cheap relative to this name's four-quarter realized distribution (−16%, −31%, +11%, −20%).
- **Skew:** my scenario-weighted expected move is modestly negative (≈ −3%) with a fat left tail. The asymmetry favors defined-risk downside expressions (put spreads) over outright long exposure into the print.
- **Overhang:** a securities class action was filed in June 2026 over the January 2026 ARR-growth disclosures. It raises the reputational cost to management of another ARR reset — which cuts both ways: more incentive to have de-risked the guide in July, less tolerance from investors if they didn't.

**Bottom line:** EPS and revenue will almost certainly be fine; they have been for eight straight quarters and it hasn't mattered. The entire print rests on net new subscription ARR of ~$45M and the credibility of the H2-weighted bridge to $1.20–1.21B. The stock has recovered the full July drawdown ahead of a quarter where the last three of four reports produced double-digit declines on beats, with sell-side targets already below spot. Risk/reward into the print is unattractive long; the opportunity is in owning optionality or waiting for the post-print dislocation this name reliably produces.
