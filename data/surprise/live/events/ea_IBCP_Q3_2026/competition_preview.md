## 1. The setup in one paragraph

Q3 is IBCP's **first quarter consolidating HCB Financial / Highpoint Community Bank** (~$590M assets, $532M deposits, $354M loans), which closed **July 1, 2026**. Critically, the two banks are running as **separate subsidiaries until the November 9, 2026 system conversion**, so Q3 carries **HCB's full expense base with essentially none of the 40% cost saves** (management targets those in early 2027) plus one-time deal and conversion costs. On top of that, the share count rose ~7% (1,470,574 shares issued). The result is a quarter where **operating trends should look better than the headline**, and where the GAAP print is likely to be messy. This is a "read the expense line and the purchase-accounting footnote before you trade it" quarter.

## 3. My estimate build (base case)

Starting point: Q2 2026 standalone — NII $47.9M, NIM 3.71% (+6bps q/q, +13bps y/y), fee income $15.3M (including ~$3.4M of non-core: a $1.8M MSR fair-value gain and a $1.6M Visa equity gain), noninterest expense $37.8M (including $0.4M litigation and **$369K merger expense** — note: a transcript-derived "$4.4M" merger-cost figure circulating online conflicts with the 8-K line item; I use the filed $369K), provision $2.72M, tax rate 17.2%, 20.81M average diluted shares.

**The single largest uncertainty is the core expense run-rate.** Management guided to "$36–37M" quarterly core NIE on the Q2 call, but legacy Q2 core was already ~$37.0M — so it is genuinely ambiguous whether that guide was legacy-only or HCB-inclusive. If HCB-inclusive, my base EPS is ~$0.10 too low and the quarter is a clean beat. If legacy-only (my assumption), $0.95 is a fair bar. **Resolving this ambiguity is the most valuable thing on the call.**

**Second-largest: whether the day-2 CECL provision lands in Q3.** Under CECL, the allowance on acquired *non-PCD* loans is established through the income statement in the acquisition quarter — which for a July 1 close is Q3. IBCP never disclosed an estimate. If it is recognized and the Street's $0.95 is a GAAP figure, the headline will read as a large miss on what is operationally an in-line quarter. **This is the main source of cosmetic-miss risk.**

## 4. Key metrics to watch — ranked

1. **Core noninterest expense, HCB-consolidated, and the Q4/2027 expense guide.** Anything at or below ~$40M core is a positive surprise; $42M+ pushes the 40%-cost-save credibility question into 2027 and compresses the FY27 $3.88 bar.
2. **NIM, split between core and purchase-accounting accretion.** Guided core expansion is 2–4bps/quarter. A reported 3.80%+ that is mostly accretion is lower quality than 3.72% that is mostly core. Ask for the accretion dollar amount.
3. **Credit — the $32.8M NPL bucket.** NPLs jumped to 0.74% of loans in Q2 (from 0.20% a year earlier), with roughly **two-thirds in a single commercial development project** that management described as a slow legal process. Allowance was 1.49% of loans with ~200% NPL coverage. Resolution, partial charge-off, or further migration is the biggest single-name risk in the print.
4. **Tangible book value per share.** Three forces collide: ~4% announced deal dilution at closing (3.4-year earnback), strong organic accretion (TBV/share was $24.24, +14.8% annualized in Q2), and **negative AOCI marks from the September rate hike**. Post-deal CET1 was projected at ~11.5%.
5. **Loan growth and the Q4 bridge.** Management flagged Q3 as seasonally soft and reaffirmed "low-double-digit commercial growth for 2026" — which requires a strong Q4. HCB's low 60% loan/asset ratio is real redeployment optionality for 2027.
6. **Deposit costs under a hiking Fed.** Cost of funds was 1.53% and *falling* in Q2. The FOMC **raised rates 25bps to 3.75–4.00% on September 16, 2026** — the first hike since 2023 — with 16 of 18 participants expecting one more by year-end. Whether management reaffirms the 2–4bps/quarter NIM guide in a hiking regime matters more than the Q3 NIM itself.

## 5. Scenario analysis

Operating EPS (excluding merger/conversion charges and any day-2 CECL provision), 22.2M diluted shares, 17.5% tax rate:

| Scenario | Revenue | Core NIE | Provision | Op. EPS | NIM | What has to happen | Stock reaction |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Bull** | $70.8M | $39.8M | $2.5M | **~$1.05** | 3.85%+ | Meaningful purchase-accounting accretion, MSR fair-value gain from higher rates, HCB expenses lighter than feared, NPL resolution progress, buyback restarted | **+4% to +7%** |
| **Base** | $68.2M | $40.5M | $3.0M | **~$0.92–0.96** | 3.75% | In-line revenue; HCB carries full cost base pre-conversion; credit stable; conversion reaffirmed for Nov 9 | **−1% to +2%** |
| **Bear** | $66.0M | $41.5M | $4.5–5.5M | **~$0.72–0.78** | 3.65% | HCB dilutes NIM with little accretion offset, deposit costs re-accelerate post-hike, expense creep, and/or the development-project NPL migrates to charge-off | **−5% to −9%** |

**Management commentary that signals each:** *Bull* — a specific accretion dollar figure plus a core NIE guide with a "3" handle for Q1 2027 and confirmation that cost saves are tracking ahead of the 40% target. *Base* — "conversion on track for November 9, savings realized in early 2027," no change to NIM guidance. *Bear* — any hedging on the conversion date, a widened expense range, or "we are monitoring" language on the development credit.

**Historical calibration:** IBCP does not pay much for beats. Q2 2026 beat by $0.06 on EPS and by $3.2M on revenue and the stock finished roughly flat (−0.4%, $35.88 vs. a $36.01 prior close). Q3 2025 came in in-line and traded down ~0.7% pre-market. The asymmetry runs the other way — credit or expense surprises get punished harder than operating beats get rewarded. My bear-case range is deliberately wider than the bull.

## 6. Catalyst checklist

1. **Core NIE vs. my ~$40.5M / the ambiguous "$36–37M" guide** — determines whether $0.95 was ever the right bar, and sets the FY27 $3.88 trajectory.
2. **NIM print and the accretion split vs. 3.71% in Q2** — plus a *reaffirmation or revision* of 2–4bps/quarter expansion now that the Fed is hiking again.
3. **The $32.8M NPL / single development project** — buy-side wants either a resolution timeline or a quantified charge-off, not another "it's a legal process."
4. **GAAP-to-operating bridge: day-2 CECL provision + merger/conversion costs + TBV/share** — the reconciliation table is where the quarter is actually won or lost.
5. **Capital return.** Zero shares repurchased in H1 2026 against a **1.1M-share authorization that expires in December 2026**. With CET1 ~11.5%, a restart (or a new 2027 authorization) is a clean positive signal; letting it lapse silently reads as capital being reserved for more M&A.
6. **Any commentary on further Michigan M&A** — IBCP has now done HCB (2026) after Traverse City State Bank; a management team signaling another deal before the HCB saves are banked would likely be taken poorly at 1.5x TBV.

## 8. Positioning view

The risk/reward is **modestly constructive into the print but better after it.** Fundamentals are sound — NIM expanding, 9.8% annualized loan growth in Q2, 1.37% ROA, 14.5% ROE, and a 3% yield at under 10x forward — and the stock is cheap versus peers. But Q3 is structurally the ugliest quarter of the HCB deal cycle: full acquired cost base, zero cost saves, one-time conversion spend, a probable day-2 provision, and 7% more shares. If you don't already own it, the better entry is likely **after** the GAAP headline is digested, particularly if a day-2 provision drives a mechanical "miss." If you do own it, the thing that would change my mind is **expense guidance**, not the Q3 EPS number.
