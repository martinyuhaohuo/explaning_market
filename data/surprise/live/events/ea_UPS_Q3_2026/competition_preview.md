# UPS (NYSE: UPS) — Q3 FY2026 Earnings Preview

**Report date:** October 26, 2026 · **Event:** Tuesday, October 27, 2026, press release ~6:00 AM ET (10:00 UTC), call 8:30 AM ET — **pre-market** · **Price:** ~$100 (Oct 23 close $101.06)

---

## 1. The Setup in One Paragraph

UPS enters this print having beaten EPS in 7 of the last 8 quarters (avg. surprise ~12.4%) — and having fallen on each of the last three: **-3.1% (Q4'25), -4.8% (Q1'26), -6.6% (Q2'26)**, the last one on a beat *and* a guidance raise. The market is no longer paying for quarterly beats; it is underwriting (a) whether post-Amazon margin gains are structural rather than fuel-surcharge-and-mix driven, and (b) whether the FY26 guide's extreme Q4 dependence is credible. Consensus Q3 EPS of **$1.62 is down 6.9% YoY** — but on our segment work, essentially *all* of that decline is an anomalous Supply Chain Solutions comp, while the core U.S. Domestic business should post ~60bps of YoY margin expansion. **How management frames that distinction is the swing factor on the day.**

## 3. Segment Bridge — Where the YoY EPS Decline Actually Comes From

| Segment (adj.) | Q3'25A Rev | Q3'25A Op $ | Margin | Q3'26E Rev | Q3'26E Op $ | Margin | Δ Op $ |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| U.S. Domestic | $14,220M | $905M | 6.4% | ~$14,400M | ~$1,008M | 7.0% | **+$103M** |
| International | $4,673M | $691M | 14.8% | ~$4,950M | ~$668M | 13.5% | **−$23M** |
| Supply Chain Solutions | $2,522M | $536M | 21.3% | ~$2,750M | ~$289M | 10.5% | **−$247M** |
| **Consolidated** | **$21.42B** | **$2,132M** | **10.0%** | **~$22.1B** | **~$1,965M** | **~8.9%** | **−$167M** |

**Takeaway:** the SCS base effect alone (−$247M, roughly **−$0.22 of EPS**) more than accounts for the entire −$0.12 YoY EPS decline. Strip it out and UPS is growing operating profit. The Q3'25 SCS adjusted margin of 21.3% ($536M on $2,522M) is a severe outlier versus Q1'26 (8.1%), Q2'26 (10.2%) and Q4'25 (10.3%) — **verify the composition of that base in the 10-Q before holding management accountable to it.** If sell-side notes frame the headline miss-versus-last-year as deterioration, that is a mispricing to lean against.

**Sensitivity to carry into the print:** ~100bps of U.S. Domestic operating margin ≈ **$143M operating profit ≈ $0.13 of EPS** (851M shares, 23% tax). U.S. Domestic margin is the single highest-leverage line in the release.

## 4. Key Metrics to Watch — Ranked

1. **U.S. Domestic adjusted operating margin vs. ~7.0% guide** — the whole thesis. ≥7.5% validates structural improvement; ≤6.5% says the Q2 print was fuel/mix noise. Watch the *quality* of the beat: revenue-per-piece driven by fuel surcharge is discounted by the market; driven by SMB/healthcare mix is paid for.
2. **FY26 guidance treatment ($91.2B revenue / $8.65B adj. op profit / $7.22 adj. EPS)** — 1H actuals were $44.0B and **$2.83** of EPS. With Q3 at consensus, **Q4 must deliver ~$24.9B revenue and ~$2.77 EPS, +16% YoY** against Q4'25's $2.38. That is the most demanding quarter in the guide. Any trim here is the bear trigger; a confident, *specific* Q4 bridge is the bull trigger.
3. **Free cash flow and dividend coverage** — FY FCF guide **$5.5B vs. $5.4B of dividends** (1.02x). 1H FCF was only **$1,573M**, so 2H must produce ~$3.9B. Payout ratio is being quoted at 94% (GAAP trailing) to 122% depending on basis; 91% on the $7.22 adjusted guide. At a **6.56% yield**, the market is pricing non-trivial cut risk. Explicit dividend reaffirmation language matters as much as the EPS line.
4. **SMB mix and healthcare revenue** — SMB was 34.5% of U.S. volume in Q2 (+250bps YoY, ADV +4.3%); healthcare revenue >$3B for two straight quarters. These are the "better, not bigger" proof points. Digital Access Program ($1.4B in Q2, B2B DAP volume +34%) is the cleanest read on pricing power with small shippers.
5. **Cost take-out run-rate** — ~$3B of 2026 glide-down/reconfiguration benefit targeted; 45 buildings closed and ~30,000 operational positions removed through mid-year, $4.5B of Amazon-related expense eliminated. Watch for an updated building-closure count and whether the 2026 benefit figure is raised.
6. **International margin and trade** — guided 13–14% vs. 14.8% LY. China-to-U.S. returned to growth in May; Canada-to-U.S. remains tariff-impaired. Management flagged de minimis elimination benefits landing in September — Q3 is the first quarter to show that.
7. **Peak season commentary** — UPS raised holiday surcharges effective Oct 25, with air fees up ~22–23% (shoulder $1.10→$1.35; peak-of-peak $2.05→$2.50). Management expects ~24% sequential volume lift Q3→Q4. Pricing described as "rational."

## 5. Scenario Analysis

| Scenario | Revenue | Adj. EPS | U.S. Dom. margin | Key driver | Stock reaction |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Bull** (25%) | $22.6–23.1B | $1.72–1.80 | 7.5–8.0% | De minimis + surcharge benefit lands early; cost take-out ahead of plan; FY EPS nudged to ≥$7.25; FCF reaffirmed with explicit dividend support | **+6% to +10%** → $106–110 |
| **Base** (45%) | $22.2–22.4B | $1.62–1.68 | 6.9–7.2% | In-line quarter; FY $7.22 reaffirmed but still Q4-loaded; no new dividend language | **−2% to +3%** → $98–103 |
| **Bear** (30%) | $21.8–22.0B | $1.52–1.58 | <6.5% | Volume softer than the 24% seasonal lift implies; fuel/Middle East cost pressure; FY cut to $6.90–7.05; FCF guide trimmed toward/below the dividend | **−8% to −13%** → $87–92 |

**What each requires operationally**

- **Bull:** U.S. ADV decline narrows materially from Q2's −3.3% *with* revenue-per-piece holding high-single-digit growth — i.e. price and volume improving together, not trading off. Management quantifies a 2027 margin path.
- **Base:** The Q2 pattern repeats — margin up YoY, driven partly by mix/fuel, with 2027 left vague. Historically this has still produced a *down* day.
- **Bear:** SCS margin lands at or below 10%, International below 13%, and the Q4 bridge is left unspecified. Any softening of dividend commitment turns this into the left tail.

**Historical calibration:** the left tail is fatter than the straddle implies. UPS has exceeded its options-implied move in 4 of the last 8 prints, and on Jan 30, 2025 a 5.8% implied move preceded a **−13.3%** realized move. All three 2026 prints were negative despite beats. Skew is to the downside.

## 6. Catalyst Checklist

1. **U.S. Domestic adj. op margin vs. 7.0%** — ±100bps = ±$0.13 EPS and, more importantly, decides whether the structural-margin story survives.
2. **FY26 adj. EPS $7.22 — reaffirmed, raised, or cut** — with ~$2.77 required in Q4 (+16% YoY), this is the highest-variance item in the release. Demand a quantified Q4 bridge on the call.
3. **Dividend/FCF language** — $5.5B FCF vs. $5.4B dividend, with ~$3.9B of FCF needed in 2H. At a 6.56% yield, the equity is partly a credit-like instrument; coverage commentary moves it more than EPS.
4. **Quality-of-revenue disclosure** — fuel surcharge vs. mix contribution to revenue-per-piece; SMB share of volume; healthcare revenue. This is what separates a paid-for beat from an ignored one.
5. **First forward reference to 2027** — buy-side is underwriting further margin expansion next year. Any framing of 2027 U.S. Domestic margin (or refusal to frame it) is the narrative catalyst. Secondary watch items: 2028 Teamsters negotiation posture and Amazon's in-house fulfillment expansion.

**Read on the options market:** a 6.1% straddle against a 4.8% realized average looks *modestly rich* on a symmetric basis — but the realized distribution is left-skewed and the January 2025 precedent (−13.3%) shows the tail is live. Selling premium into this print is poorly compensated for the downside gap risk. If positioning for direction, **put spreads financed by upside calls** better match the distribution than a long straddle. Flat-to-long into the print is only justified if you underwrite the SCS-comp mispricing in Section 3 *and* believe the dividend is safe.

**Our lean:** the fundamental setup is better than the headline YoY EPS decline suggests (U.S. Domestic is genuinely improving; the decline is a base effect). But three consecutive sell-offs on beats, a 6.4% 90-day negative revision trend, a Q4-loaded guide, and 1.02x dividend coverage mean the burden of proof sits with management, not the bears. **Base case: an in-line quarter trades flat-to-lower.** The asymmetry only turns favorable if management quantifies 2027 margins and explicitly backstops the dividend.
