## Recent context
- **Q2 2026 (reported July 28):** Net sales $375M, down from the prior year. Net loss $21M. Adjusted EBITDA from continuing operations was -$8M, versus +$40M a year earlier. Shipments rose 8% year over year, but market pricing fell 9%. Lewiston planned outage timing and about $5M of Iran-related transport costs added pressure.
- **Q3 guidance:** Adjusted EBITDA of $20M–$30M.
- **FY2026 guidance:** Revenue $1.4B–$1.5B, maintained.
- **Pricing action:** Two $60/ton SBS price increases. Expected to add $10M–$20M in H2 2026, with $50M–$60M annualized EBITDA once fully implemented.
- **Restructuring:** Cypress Bend, Arkansas cuts about 20% of roles, for $8M–$12M of annual savings.
- **Balance sheet:** Net debt down $59M in Q2.

## Key metrics to watch (ranked)
1. **Adjusted EBITDA vs. the $20–30M guide.** The guide is the key number. The top half of the range needs the SBS price increases to stick.
2. **SBS pricing and realized price.** Whether the $60/ton increases are holding, and the size of H2 realization. This is the main margin lever.
3. **Shipments vs. market pricing.** Q2 volume rose 8% while pricing fell 9%. Watch whether volume growth continues.
4. **Q4 and FY2026 revenue guidance.** The $1.4–1.5B range needs to hold. Any cut would be a clear negative.
5. **Cost items:** transport and energy costs, the Iran-related freight exposure, and early Cypress Bend savings.
6. **Net debt and liquidity.** Continued debt reduction supports the equity story.

**What would signal each case:**
- **Bull:** Management cites specific price-increase realization, volume strength continuing, and cost savings running ahead of plan.
- **Base:** Reiterated guidance with no new detail on price realization.
- **Bear:** Language about "softer than expected" pricing, a lower Q4 outlook, or pushed-out savings.

**Historical reaction context:** Reactions have been mixed. Q3 2022 fell about 17% despite an EPS beat, because of a weak EBITDA outlook. Q2 2022 rose about 22% despite a miss, on strong sales and price increases. Guidance and pricing commentary have mattered more than the headline EPS beat or miss.
