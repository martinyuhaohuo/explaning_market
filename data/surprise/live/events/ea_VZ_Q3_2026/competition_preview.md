# Verizon Communications (VZ) — Q3 FY2026 Earnings Preview

**Report date:** October 23, 2026 · **Earnings:** Monday, October 26, 2026, **before market open** — press release and financial tables 7:00 a.m. ET, webcast 8:30 a.m. ET · **Price:** $44.38 (Oct 23) · **Market cap:** ~$184B · **P/E:** ~11.6x · **Dividend yield:** ~6.4% ($2.83/sh)

---

## 1. The setup in one paragraph

Verizon enters this print with the best fundamental momentum in a decade and the worst narrative backdrop in a decade. Operationally, CEO Dan Schulman's subsidy-light "Simplicity" repositioning has produced three straight quarters of improving subscriber trends (Q1 2026 was the first positive Q1 postpaid phone net adds since 2013; Q2 delivered +184k against ~106k consensus), two guidance raises, and record Q2 adjusted EBITDA. Then on October 8–9, SpaceX agreed to buy Grain Management's nationwide 800 MHz portfolio (up to 14 MHz paired low-band) for ~$8B, giving Starlink Mobile the indoor-coverage layer it lacked. Telecom derated violently — VZ fell roughly 8–9% in the session (reported magnitudes vary by source; T-Mobile and AT&T fell more). **So this is no longer an EPS print. It is a durability print:** management must show churn, convergence, and the Frontier fiber moat are structural before Starlink launches in early 2028, and must do it without conceding higher capex.

## 3. Key metrics to watch — ranked

1. **Consumer postpaid phone churn.** Q2 was 0.84% (down 6 bps YoY) versus 0.91% in Q3 2025. This is now *the* valuation variable: it is the single number that either substantiates or refutes the "we have a moat before Starlink" argument. Sequential improvement or holding 84 bps through iPhone season = thesis intact. Drift back toward 0.90% = the bear case writes itself.
2. **Postpaid phone net adds (total, and Consumer vs. Business split).** Consumer is the swing factor — it lost 7k in Q3 2025. Business carried the prior-year quarter (+51k phone). A headline beat driven only by Business is low-quality.
3. **Mobility & broadband service revenue growth.** Guided +2.5–3.0%; management has pointed toward a **3–4%** exit trajectory. Printing a 3-handle is the clearest evidence that subsidy-free pricing is monetizing rather than just retaining.
4. **ARPA/ARPU.** Management has signaled ARPU turns positive in Q4. AT&T just posted postpaid phone ARPU +4% versus ~1.6% consensus — that resets the comparison bar. Verizon needs to show its simplified plans aren't a disguised price cut.
5. **Broadband net adds and fiber passings.** Q2 was 348k; the company targets >32M fiber passings by year-end (30M at Frontier close on Jan 20, 2026, plus ≥2M new in 2026) against a 40–50M medium-term goal. Convergence (fiber + wireless bundles) is the stated churn defense.
6. **Free cash flow and capex discipline.** FCF guide $21.9–22.1B against a ~$11.8B dividend. Capex plan is ~$16.5B. **Any hint of a capex step-up in response to SpaceX is the most dangerous thing management can say** — AT&T's stock fell ~5% on a beat-and-raise precisely because of capex and deleveraging timing.
7. **$9B cost program progress.** Q2 showed cost-to-acquire down 15% YoY and cost-to-retain down 17%. This is what funds the EPS guide.
8. **AI Connect commentary.** A $1B+ Google dark-fiber deal is signed, with "more multi-billion deals expected" and meaningful revenue starting **2027**. Zero Q3 revenue impact — it is pure narrative optionality, and the only credible offset to the Starlink story.

## 4. Scenario analysis

| Scenario | Revenue | Adj. EPS | Postpaid phone net adds / churn | Key driver | Stock reaction |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|

| **Bear (~25%)** | ≤$34.6B | ≤$1.26 | **<140k** / churn ≥0.88% | iPhone-season promo intensity forces subsidy re-entry, Consumer phone adds flat-to-negative, ARPA growth stalls, FY subscriber guide quietly trimmed to "750k–1M" (dropping "upper half"), or capex guided higher to answer Starlink | **−6% to −10%** (toward $40–41.5, testing the 52-week low zone of $38.39) |

**How to read management on the call:**
- *Bull tell:* specific, quantified convergence metrics (bundled-customer churn differential, Frontier-market ARPA), plus an unprompted reaffirmation that 2027 capex is unchanged.
- *Bear tell:* reversion to language about "competitive promotional environment," "investing in the customer base," or "flexibility on capex." Any of those three phrases is a sell signal on the print.
- *Narrative tell:* how Schulman frames SpaceX. Dismissiveness will not clear; the market wants a dated, resourced answer (spectrum depth, D2D partnerships, bundled fiber) and evidence that the 2026 churn gains were structural rather than promotional.

## 5. Catalyst checklist

1. **Consumer postpaid phone churn versus 0.84%** — the proxy for whether the Starlink derating is justified. Highest-beta single number in the release.
2. **Postpaid phone net adds versus ~150k published / ~190k guidance-implied** — beating the Street but missing the guidance math is a trap outcome that screens as a beat and trades as a miss.
3. **FY2026 EPS/FCF guidance action** — a third raise, however small, is the cleanest signal Verizon can send. A reaffirm is acceptable; silence on the subscriber range is not.
4. **Capex language for 2027** — the asymmetric risk. Protecting the $21.9–22.1B FCF guide (and therefore the ~6.4% dividend) matters more to this shareholder base than any subscriber number.
5. **Strategic/narrative response to SpaceX** — AI Connect pipeline quantification, convergence disclosure, spectrum-position defense, or inorganic/partnership news. A buyback upsize would read as management saying the stock is dislocated.

**Observations on the setup.** The ~4% implied move is *cheap* relative to the dispersion in my scenarios (+8% to −10%) and relative to realized reactions in the last three quarters, two of which exceeded 4% and one of which was nearly 12%. Verizon has historically been a low-vol dividend proxy; it is currently trading as a disrupted growth story, and option pricing has not fully re-rated to that regime. Long-volatility structures into the print screen better than directional exposure.

Skew is genuinely two-sided. The downside is that at ~11.6x and a 6.4% yield, a lot of bad news is priced — the stock already absorbed an 8–9% single-day hit and sits 14% off its high, so an in-line print into depressed positioning can squeeze. The upside risk to that view is that the dividend-oriented holder base is the most likely marginal seller if 2027 capex rises, and that cohort does not wait for the second data point.

**Read-across from AT&T (reported Oct 21, 2026) — the most important calibration available.** AT&T beat on revenue ($32.3B, +5.3%) and adjusted EPS ($0.65), raised FY EPS guidance to ~$2.40, and held the ≥$18B FCF target — and still fell 4.6% pre-market and more than 5% intraday, because postpaid phone net adds (379k) slightly missed ~387k and because of capex/deleveraging concerns. (It subsequently recovered.) **Conclusion: in this tape, an EPS beat earns nothing. Subscribers, churn, and capex set the reaction function.** Note also that AT&T's ARPU +4% raises the bar for Verizon's monetization narrative. T-Mobile does not report until October 28 (after the close), so Verizon prints without the full competitive picture — and any Verizon subscriber beat carries residual risk of being revealed as share taken from, or ceded to, T-Mobile two days later.

## 7. Caveats on this preview

- Consensus figures are vendor-sourced and dated **Oct 19–23, 2026**; they drift into the print. The revenue spread ($34.77B vs. $34.87B) and the implied-move spread (3.5% vs. 4.76%) reflect genuine source disagreement, reproduced here rather than averaged away.
- There is no published, reliable sell-side consensus for Q3 postpaid phone net adds. The ~150k–200k range and the ~190k guidance-implied figure are **my derivations** from the FY guide and seasonality, not a surveyed number — treat them as a framework, not a datapoint.
- Reported magnitudes of the October 8–9 telecom selloff differ across sources (VZ −8.75% per one account; another put AT&T at −7.28% and T-Mobile at −6.89% versus −9.81% and −13.27% elsewhere). The direction and severity are not in doubt; the decimals are.
- The SpaceX transaction is **not closed** — targeted for H1 2027, with Starlink Mobile service launch in early 2028, and subject to regulatory review. Nothing in Q3 2026 results will be affected by it. The repricing is entirely about terminal value.
- Scenario probabilities are my judgment, not a market-derived distribution.
