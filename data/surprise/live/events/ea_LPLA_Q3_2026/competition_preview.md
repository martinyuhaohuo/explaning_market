## 2. Key Metrics to Watch (ranked)

**1. Client cash: ending balances, ICA yield, and the post-hike repricing path.** Client cash is ~30% of gross profit and the single largest swing factor in the stock. Q2 ended at $56.9B; July fell to $54.3B on advisory fee billing, August was flat at $54.4B. Q2 cash sweep revenue was $456.9M. The new variable: **the Fed hiked 25bp to 3.75–4.00% on Sept 16** — its first hike since 2023, under Chair Warsh, with the dot plot pointing to one more by December. That is a reversal of the "rate-cut double whammy" fear that compressed LPLA's multiple earlier this year. Listen for whether the +10bp yield guide gets *raised* and for any reframing of 2027 cash revenue sensitivity. This is the highest-leverage item on the call.

**2. Commonwealth conversion — retention vs. the 90% target.** Onboarding is slated for **Q4 2026**, i.e. the quarter now underway. Q2 retention was running "mid-80s" against a 90% asset-retention target; run-rate EBITDA was raised to ~$435M. Third-party tracking has counted 654 advisor departures (~22.5% of headcount) since the deal, with departures peaking in October, and both firms have begun staff cuts. A confirmed on-time conversion with retention at/near 90% de-risks the 2027 EBITDA bridge; slippage or a retention walk-down is the most likely source of a sharp negative reaction.

**3. Organic NNA and the September month.** LPL does not publish a quarter-end month separately, so September lands with the print. July $7.4B (~3% annualized), August $13.5B (6.4%) → $20.9B through two months; Q3 total likely ~$30B ± $4B (Q2 was $23B / 4%). Watch the **mix**: August advisory NNA was $12.5B (up Y/Y) but brokerage NNA was just $0.9B vs $6.1B a year ago. Advisory is now 60.8% of assets vs 57.8% a year ago — good for revenue quality, but decelerating brokerage flows are a real growth question.

**4. Recruited assets and the pipeline.** Q2 recruited assets were $24.9B (+35% Y/Y), strongest in ~2 years ex-large institutional wins; TTM $89B; pipeline at a record. Management said it had "cleared the decks" to restart institutional conversations — a new large institutional win announced on this call would be a genuine positive surprise not in numbers. Note the recruiting leadership transition (Kenneth Hullings takes over in November).

**5. Expense outlook — 2026 core G&A and any first 2027 framing.** Full-year 2026 core G&A guide is **$2,140–2,165M** (lowered at Q2). The January 2026 print fell 3.5% *despite* a beat purely on 2026 expense guidance — the market has already demonstrated it will punish an expense guide here. Any early 2027 core G&A or investment-spend commentary is the asymmetric risk on this call.

**6. Capital return.** $309M repurchased in Q2 (accelerated from a $125M plan on price weakness), ~$300M planned for Q3, against a fresh **$2.5B** authorization. With the stock ~21% off its 52-week high, an upsized Q4 buyback would read as a management-conviction signal.

## 4. Catalyst Checklist

1. **Client cash revenue vs. ~$462M / ending balances vs. $54B** — ~30% of gross profit; the Sept Fed hike plus cash-based ICA tiering means the yield trajectory, not the balance, is what re-rates the stock. *Whisper: buy-side wants the +10bp guide raised.*
2. **Commonwealth: Q4 conversion date confirmed + asset retention vs. the 90% target** — the 2027 EBITDA bridge ($435M run-rate) hinges on this; "mid-80s" repeated would be a miss against expectations.
3. **Q3 organic NNA vs. ~$30B / September month** — July's 3% annualized was soft; the buy-side needs the quarter to land at 4%+ with advisory mix intact and brokerage NNA not negative.
4. **Core G&A print vs. $540–560M, 2026 guide reaffirmation, and any 2027 framing** — the direct cause of the January selloff; the highest-probability source of a guide-driven de-rating.
5. **Recruited assets vs. ~$25B + pipeline commentary / institutional win** — the one place a genuine upside surprise can come from, with the pipeline at a record and institutional conversations restarted.

## 5. Trading Setup

- **Recent performance:** $316.70, **–6.8% over 12 months** and ~21% below the $400.16 52-week high, but **+3.7% MTD** in October — the stock is bouncing into the print off a washed-out base, with 23 analysts at a $411–422 mean target.
- **Valuation:** ~13–14x forward EPS vs ~24.8x trailing, on TTM revenue up ~40% to $19.2B. The de-rating is about *terminal* cash-spread and fee-pressure risk, not current numbers.
- **Positioning skew:** estimates cut 5% into the print + 8-of-8 beat history = a low bar on the EPS line, so the risk/reward on the *print* is favorable, while the risk sits almost entirely in **guidance** (expenses, cash yield, Commonwealth). That argues for owning the beat but respecting a negative-guide tail.
- **Implied move:** I could not source a live ATM straddle quote for LPLA — Market Chameleon's earnings-move dataset is paywalled and this week's TipRanks implied-move screen does not include LPLA. **Based on realized history, the last four one-day post-earnings moves averaged ~3.0% absolute (range 1.3%–3.9%), which typically corresponds to an implied move of roughly ±5%.** Treat that as an estimate, not a quoted figure; verify against the Oct 30 expiry straddle before sizing an options trade. My scenario dispersion (–12% to +10%) is wider than the realized history on the downside, because the Commonwealth conversion window and a possible first 2027 expense frame both land in this call.
- **Macro wrinkle:** the FOMC decision lands **the next afternoon (Oct 28, 2:00 PM ET)**, with ~90% priced for a hold at 3.75–4.00% and ~75% of participants expecting at least one hike by year-end. A hawkish Warsh press conference is a *tailwind* for LPLA's cash spread and could extend or reverse the earnings-day move within 24 hours. Do not assess the print's reaction in isolation.
