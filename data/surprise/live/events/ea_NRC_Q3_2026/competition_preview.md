## 2. What Management Said Last Quarter (Q2 FY2026, reported July 27)

The Q2 print is the key setup document. Results: revenue **$35.39M (+4% Y/Y, +2% Q/Q)**, TRCV **$151.9M (+11% Y/Y)**, adj. EBITDA **$9.4M (27% margin, down 3pts Y/Y)**, adj. EPS **$0.31**, but a **GAAP net loss of $(3.3)M / $(0.15)** and an operating loss of $(3.2)M. Free cash flow was essentially nil at **$0.1M**.

Explicit forward commentary from the call:

- **Q3 revenue up sequentially; Q3 adj. EBITDA margin ~27%**, pressured by the annual customer conference held in September.
- **Q4 margin expansion** as revenue scales and operating costs normalize.
- **Effective tax rate ~40% in both Q3 and Q4** (non-deductible executive bonuses / accelerated vesting under §162(m)); normalized effective rate high-20s, cash taxes low-to-mid 20s.
- **"Landmark" customer** (largest deal in company history, announced Q1) went live with revenue recognition starting in Q2; **next phase completes by year-end 2026**.
- **Pipeline +60% Y/Y, sales activity +35% Y/Y**, but **average deal size down** amid buyer hesitation following the Qualtrics–Press Ganey combination (closed May 2026).
- **The Governance Institute: strongest bookings in seven years**; Experience TRCV +mid-teens, Governance Institute TRCV +~10%.
- **~71% of customers buy only one product** — the cross-sell thesis.
- Capital allocation: $0.16/qtr dividend maintained (~2.9% yield), ~397K shares repurchased under a $60M authorization.

**Balance sheet (6/30/26):** cash $3.3M, **total debt $90.0M**, **shareholders' equity just $6.5M** (down from $14.0M at YE25), total assets $136.2M. Equity has been drained by buybacks and dividends against thin GAAP earnings — this is a leveraged small cap, and it constrains how aggressively management can keep buying stock.

## 3. Key Metrics to Watch — Ranked

1. **TRCV, and specifically whether it sets a new record.** This is the actual KPI the equity trades on. Q1'26 printed an all-time-high **$152.1M**; Q2'26 came in at **$151.9M** — *fractionally lower sequentially*, even as the Y/Y optic was a strong +11% against an easy base. A second consecutive quarter without a sequential record would undercut the entire re-rating story. Bogey: **>$152.1M, and ≥+10% Y/Y (i.e., ≥$155.9M)**.
2. **Adj. EBITDA margin vs. the ~27% guide.** Q3'25 was 31%. Hitting 27% means ~400bps of Y/Y compression and roughly **−11% Y/Y adj. EBITDA dollars on +3% revenue growth**. Margin, not revenue, is the bear case. Anything ≤26% breaks the "investments are temporary" narrative.
3. **Q4 margin-expansion reaffirmation.** The entire base case rests on this. Management must repeat it with specifics (cost normalization, landmark-customer contribution). Silence or softening is the single most dangerous outcome on the call.
4. **GAAP optics and the ~40% tax rate.** GAAP EPS will be mechanically depressed and may print near zero or negative again. My modeled GAAP EPS is roughly **$0.08–$0.15** (adj. EBITDA ~$9.7M less ~$2M D&A, ~$1.5M interest on $90M debt, taxed at ~40%, on 22.0M shares), versus adjusted EPS of ~$0.20–$0.22 at a normalized high-20s rate. Watch whether stock comp normalizes from Q2's elevated **$7.4M** and whether the **$3.2M** of management-transition costs fully drop out.
5. **Pipeline conversion and average deal size.** Pipeline +60% and activity +35% are only valuable if they convert. The tell is **average deal size recovering** — the Q2 explanation (buyers pausing on Qualtrics–Press Ganey) is a fine one-quarter excuse and a poor two-quarter one.
6. **Free cash flow.** $0.1M in Q2 against a $0.16 dividend (~$3.5M/qtr) and active buybacks, funded on a revolver, with $3.3M of cash. Watch the revolver balance.

**What each requires operationally:**

- **Bull:** the landmark customer's second phase lands early and the Governance Institute's seven-year-best bookings start converting to recognized revenue, while September conference costs come in under plan. Management language to listen for: a specific Q4 margin number, a named TRCV record, and evidence that *average deal size* has turned up.
- **Base:** essentially management's own script. Note that in-line may not be good enough — see the setup below.
- **Bear:** the signal is sequential. Flat-to-down revenue vs. $35.39M, or a third quarter of ~$152M TRCV, tells you the +60% pipeline is not converting and that the 2-point accounting headwind (~$1.5M annualized, from the reputation-monitoring revenue change) is masking real-growth deceleration. Hedging on Q4 margins would confirm it.

**Historical calibration:** NRC has twice this year delivered a revenue beat and/or Y/Y adjusted-EPS growth and still traded *down* — **−8.3% after Q2'26** (vs. S&P +1.5% over the window) on GAAP-loss and margin-compression optics, and **−1.2% after-hours on Q1'26** from ~$16.90 despite a record TRCV and the largest deal in company history. The market is punishing margin compression and ignoring TRCV headlines. Do not assume a top-line beat is tradeable.

## 5. Catalyst Checklist

1. **TRCV > $152.1M (prior record), ≥+10% Y/Y** — the only growth metric with narrative power; a second flat sequential quarter is thesis-breaking.
2. **Adj. EBITDA margin ≥27%** (vs. 31% in Q3'25) — the guided floor; a miss here is what sold the stock in July.
3. **Explicit reaffirmation of Q4 margin expansion** — the buy-side needs this to underwrite FY27 estimates; it is the highest-leverage sentence on the call.
4. **Landmark customer phase-2 on track for year-end** plus the first quantification of its revenue/margin contribution.
5. **Average deal size inflecting up** — converts the Qualtrics–Press Ganey disruption from a story into share gains.
6. **Capital allocation under a stretched balance sheet** — dividend declaration, buyback pace against $6.5M equity, $90M debt, $3.3M cash and ~breakeven FCF.

**On the options-implied move:** I did not find a usable one, and I would not quote one. At ~116K shares/day and a ~$495M cap, NRC's listed options carry negligible open interest and wide spreads; any "implied move" derived from them reflects a stale mid-market mark rather than a tradeable market view. **Use realized moves instead: a ±8–10% one-day distribution is the right prior**, consistent with the −8.3% Q2 reaction and the stock's 2026 realized volatility.

**The setup is the risk.** The stock has roughly doubled off its 52-week low and sits ~5% from its high, having rallied ~32% from ~$16.90 at the Q1 print in late April. Simply Wall St attributed the move largely to **multiple expansion (+58.5% P/E change) rather than earnings**, over a stretch when the shares were up only 1.8% from May 31 to September 25 — i.e., the re-rating front-ran the fundamentals. Against a two-analyst consensus of +3.4% revenue growth and *negative* 9% adjusted-EPS growth, an in-line print into a near-high valuation skews the reaction distribution to the downside. Asymmetry favors owning this after a disappointment rather than into the event.

### Bottom line
Consensus asks for +3.4% revenue growth and a 9% adjusted-EPS *decline*, with management's own guide implying ~400bps of Y/Y margin compression and a punitive ~40% tax rate. Those are achievable bars. The problem is that the stock has already re-rated ~2x off its lows on multiple expansion, so Q3 is a *quality-of-growth* test, not a beat-or-miss one: TRCV must set a new record and Q4 margin expansion must be reaffirmed explicitly. Absent both, July's pattern — good TRCV headline, −8.3% tape — is the most likely repeat.
