# Kforce Inc. (KFRC) — Q3 FY2026 Earnings Preview

**Report date:** October 26, 2026 · **Event:** Q3 2026 results, release ~4:04 p.m. ET today (20:04 UTC), conference call 5:00 p.m. ET · **Price:** ~$58.50 (Oct 25) · **Market cap:** ~$1.0B

---

## 1. Setup in One Paragraph

Kforce enters this print as a *re-rated* cyclical, not a cheap one. The stock is up ~87% YTD, +13.5% over the last month, jumped 6.6% on Oct 23 (to $57.21) on sector sympathy after Robert Half's Oct 22 EPS beat, and sits ~5% below its 52-week high of $61.68 versus a 50-day MA of $53.37 and 200-day of $48.88. The two prints that made the stock — Q3'25 (+30.9%) and Q1'26 (+43.4%) — were *inflection* prints off a depressed base. Q2'26 (+0.6%) was the first print where the recovery was already priced. This one is further along that curve: consensus EPS of $0.76 sits at the **top** of management's $0.71–$0.79 guide, and consensus revenue of $354.4M sits **above** the $353.0M guidance midpoint. The asymmetry has flipped from the last two quarters — upside now requires a Q4 guide that beats, not merely a Q3 beat.

## 3. Key Metrics to Watch — Ranked

**1. Technology segment YoY growth (most important).** Technology is ~93% of revenue. Q3'25 base was $307.1M. Consensus total of $354.4M, assuming FA lands ~$26.5M, implies **Technology ~$328M, or +6.8% YoY — an acceleration from Q2'26's ~+5.0%**. Management explicitly promised accelerating YoY growth and cited job orders and new assignment starts up ~18% YoY in Q2 ("best in four years"), with eight of the top ten industries growing sequentially. If Technology prints below +6%, the acceleration narrative stalls regardless of the headline.

**2. Q4 guidance and the sequential daily-revenue rate.** Q4 has fewer billing days (62 in Q4'25 vs. 64 guided for Q3), so headline Q4 revenue will look flat-to-down sequentially by construction. Normalize to revenue *per billing day*: Q3 guidance midpoint = $5.52M/day vs. Q2'26's ~$5.46M. The buy side needs Q4 guided daily revenue up again, plus EPS at/above $0.60.

**3. Flex gross margin (26.7–26.9%).** Q2's 28.5% total GM was flattered by an unexpectedly strong direct-hire quarter ($7.5M) that management said will fade seasonally in Q3 ("not a critical place for significant investment"). Flex GM is the clean read on bill/pay spread discipline — Q2 was 26.9%, up 110 bps YoY. Holding ≥26.9% is a beat; sub-26.7% signals pricing erosion and would overshadow a revenue beat.

**4. SG&A leverage toward 22.2–22.4%.** Q2 SG&A rose 50 bps YoY to 22.7% on performance-based comp normalizing off historic lows. Management's Q3 guide embeds ~30–50 bps of reversal. Here's the tension worth pressing: **consensus EPS of $0.76 effectively requires the high end (5.7%) of the operating-margin guide on upper-end revenue.** Using Q2's realized EBIT-to-net conversion (~65%), a 5.5% margin on $354M maps to roughly $0.74, not $0.76. Consensus is not conservatively positioned.

**5. Diluted share count — an underappreciated mechanical headwind.** Guidance steps shares *up* to 17.2M from 17.0M despite $14.7M of H1 buybacks (527k shares). With the stock near $58 versus ~$32 in April, in-the-money equity awards add dilution under the treasury-stock method. Models that assume buybacks shrink the count may be a penny or two light.

**6. Cash conversion.** H1'26 operating cash flow was **negative $6.7M** (FCF −$13.9M) versus +$18.6M in H1'25, driven by receivables build as revenue recovered — the normal cost of growth, but the revolver is drawn $107.1M of $200M ($91.8M available) while the company returned $28.2M in H1 ($14.7M buyback, $13.5M dividends / $0.80 per share). A second quarter of negative FCF with the dividend at $1.60/yr annualized (~2.7% yield) becomes a legitimate question on the call.

**7. AI / Consulting Solutions commentary.** Management says Consulting Solutions carries 400–600 bps higher gross margin than traditional staffing and that it is hiring AI specialists from major consulting firms. There is still **no quantified Consulting Solutions or AI revenue line in the 10-Q**. Any first-time sizing or mix disclosure is a potential multiple-expansion catalyst — this is what ASGN has used to offset IT staffing declines (IT staffing −12% vs. consulting +19% in a recent quarter).

**Sector backdrop:** the ASA Staffing Index ran ~4.8–5.6% above prior year through mid-2026 (ten straight months of YoY gains); Robert Half's Oct 22 print showed talent solutions +2%, its first growth since 2022, with U.S. talent solutions +3%. The cycle is turning, but slowly and unevenly — ManpowerGroup's in-line quarter still left the stock down 20% over a month. Kforce's implied +6.8% Technology growth is well ahead of the industry, so the bar is company-specific, not macro-rescued.

## 4. Scenario Analysis

| Scenario | Revenue | EPS | Q4 Guide | Key Driver | Stock Reaction |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Bull** (25%) | $357–361M (+7.3–8.5%) | $0.80–0.84 | Rev $356–366M, EPS $0.63–0.71 | Tech +8%+; flex GM ≥26.9%; job orders/starts still +15%+; SG&A at 22.2% | **+8% to +15%** — breakout through $61.68 to new highs |
| **Base** (50%) | $353–356M (+6.1–7.0%) | $0.74–0.78 | Rev ~$348–358M, EPS $0.57–0.65 (brackets consensus) | In-line Tech ~+6.5%; flex GM mid-range; direct hire down seasonally as flagged | **−3% to +4%** — "good but priced"; a Q2'26 repeat |
| **Bear** (25%) | $349–352M (low end) | $0.70–0.73 | Rev ≤$348M or EPS mid ≤$0.56 | Tech <+6%; flex GM <26.7%; order growth decelerating; another quarter of negative FCF | **−10% to −18%** — unwinds the month's 13.5% rally, back toward the 50-day ($53) or 200-day ($49) |

**What would need to happen operationally**
- *Bull:* September and October daily revenue up sequentially, not just quarter-average; Technology order book converting to starts at Q2's pace; pricing holding so flex GM is flat-to-up despite the direct-hire drag; SG&A at the low end as revenue scales over a fixed ~1,600-associate cost base.
- *Base:* The quarter lands as guided — low-single-digit sequential growth, flex GM easing ~10 bps seasonally exactly as management framed it — and Q4 is guided around the billing-day math with no demand acceleration claimed.
- *Bear:* Consultants-on-assignment (>8,000 in Q2) flat or down, direct hire falling more than seasonally, and bill/pay spread compression as clients push back on rate — plus a Q4 guide that leans on "macro uncertainty."

**What management commentary signals each:** *Bull* — "job orders and starts continued to grow at double-digit rates in September and October," plus a quantified Consulting Solutions mix. *Base* — repetition of "low-single-digit sequential growth" and "continued stability in bill-pay spreads" without new leading-indicator data. *Bear* — hedging on October trends (recall Kelly's mid-call correction on July trends last quarter), or framing Q4 around billing-day math rather than underlying demand.

**Historical context for the reactions:** the +30.9% (Q3'25) and +43.4% (Q1'26) moves both came off a sub-$33 stock pricing in a permanent staffing depression, where a beat plus any guidance improvement re-rated the whole earnings power. Q2'26 (+0.6%) is the better template now: a $0.03 EPS beat, a revenue beat of ~$1.2M, and a Q3 guide above consensus produced essentially no move because the stock had already tripled off the low. Expect this print to behave like Q2'26 unless the Q4 guide genuinely clears $0.60.

## 5. Catalyst Checklist

1. **Technology revenue ≥ ~$328M (+6.8% YoY)** — the single number that validates or breaks the acceleration thesis.
2. **Q4 EPS guide vs. $0.60 / revenue vs. ~$353M** — with the stock at ~23x FY26E (~$2.55) and ~22x FY27E (~$2.66), the Q4 guide is what justifies the multiple. A guide *below* consensus is the highest-probability source of a double-digit drawdown.
3. **Flex gross margin inside/above 26.7–26.9%** — proves the margin story is pricing and mix, not a one-quarter direct-hire windfall.
4. **Operating margin ≥5.5%, SG&A ≤22.4%** — required for consensus EPS; anything at the low end of the margin guide likely produces an EPS "miss" versus the $0.76 whisper even with a revenue beat.
5. **Operating cash flow inflection + dividend/leverage commentary** — reversal of the H1 working-capital drag, with revolver draw not materially above $107M.
6. **Any first quantification of Consulting Solutions / AI revenue** — the one disclosure that could re-rate the stock rather than just reset the quarter.
