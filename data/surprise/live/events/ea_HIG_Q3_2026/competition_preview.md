## 1. The Setup in One Paragraph

Consensus calls for Q3 core EPS of **$3.15, down ~17% YoY** — but that headline is misleading. Q3 2025 was an all-time record quarter ($3.78, +43% YoY) built on a *$70mm* catastrophe load (vs. $247mm in Q3 2024) and **+$95mm of favorable** prior-year development. The consensus decline is almost entirely cat/reserve normalization against a comp that cannot repeat, not franchise deterioration. Meanwhile the actual Q3 2026 cat backdrop was benign — Gallagher Re puts 9M26 global insured cat losses at $92B, the lowest nine-month total since 2019 and 29% below the 10-year average, with six straight quarters lacking a single $10B+ event. **The setup is asymmetric to the upside on the print; the risk is concentrated in casualty reserves, not in cats.**

## 3. The YoY Bridge — Where the $0.63 Goes

This is the single most useful thing to internalize before the print. *Approximate, assuming ~$3.4B BI earned premium and ~276mm diluted shares (derived from Q2 26: $945mm core / $3.42).*

| Driver | Est. EPS impact |
|---|---|
| BI loss & LAE ratio +4.2 pts (cat + PYD normalization) | **−$0.41** |
| EB total loss ratio +2.7 pts (group disability incidence) | **−$0.13** |
| PI underlying CR −2.1 pts (earned pricing > loss trend) | +$0.05 |
| PI earned premium −5.5% | −$0.02 |
| Net investment income +~5% | +$0.10 |
| Lower share count (buyback) | +$0.08 |
| **Net** | **≈ −$0.33 of the −$0.63** |

The residual sits in BI non-cat property and mix. The takeaway: **~85% of the consensus YoY decline is two line items — BI cat/reserve normalization and the Employee Benefits disability loss ratio.** Neither is a pricing or franchise problem. Both are modelable.

**The key embedded assumption to test:** consensus BI reported CR of 92.6 vs. underlying 89.5 implies **+3.1 points of cat and prior-year development** (~$100–110mm pre-tax). In Q3 2025, reported 88.8 was *0.6 pts below* underlying — i.e., net favorable. That ~3.7-point swing is the crux of the quarter. Given the benign Q3 cat environment, consensus looks conservatively plugged *unless* management books another casualty charge.

## 4. Key Metrics to Watch — Ranked

**1. General liability & commercial auto prior-year development (highest importance).**
Q2 2026 carried a **$116mm adverse GL charge plus $26mm in commercial auto** — and the stock fell 2.9% *despite* a $0.21 EPS beat. This is unambiguously the swing factor for the stock, not EPS. Management framed Q2 as elevated large-loss activity and severity, "not a shift in the reserve review process." A clean quarter re-rates the multiple; a third consecutive charge breaks the "modest relative to the reserve base" narrative management has been running.

**2. Business Insurance catastrophe load.**
Benign backdrop should deliver a sub-consensus cat number. Watch one ambiguity: Gallagher Re data cites **~$43B of US severe convective storm losses** — the framing in secondary coverage is unclear as to whether that is Q3 or year-to-date (a single-quarter $43B SCS figure would be a record, so YTD is far more likely). SCS is where HIG's middle-market book actually has exposure, so this merits verification against the disclosure. **Hurricane Isaias — Cat 2, Florida Panhandle, Oct 9 — landed in Q4 and is not in this print** (low-to-mid single-digit billions industry-wide).

**3. Personal Insurance: does the premium decline inflect?**
Auto PIF −10% YoY and written premium −7% in Q2 (auto −10%, home flat). Margins are excellent and improving (consensus underlying CR 87.9%), but the segment is shrinking. The offset: agency channel +7% YoY, with the contemporary product rolling to **30 states by early 2027**. Swift was blunt that "the direct channel is going to continue to come under pressure." Watch agency new business and whether the auto PIF decline decelerates. Margin-for-volume is fine; margin *and* accelerating shrinkage is not.

**4. Employee Benefits group disability loss ratio.**
74.8% in Q2, up 6.3 pts, on behavioral-health claim incidence and paid family/medical leave utilization in newly launched states. Consensus embeds a 72.8% total loss ratio. Management says results remain "within expectations" and capable of 15%+ tangible ROEs. A third quarter of deterioration turns this from noise into a structural question about pricing adequacy.

**5. Business Insurance middle/large competition and retention.**
Management conceded "increasing competition in the middle and large space," particularly GL and workers' comp, and let retention decline on discipline. The Q2 guide was for **middle & large underlying CR roughly a point better than the 93.3 YTD level in 2H**, conditional on non-cat property normalizing. That is a checkable, specific guidance item.

**6. Net investment income / LP returns.**
Q2 NII was $800mm, +22% YoY, with LP returns at 7.6% annualized in 1H and guidance for 2H "generally consistent." Consensus P&C NII $633.9mm (+4.8%) looks beatable if LPs hold.

## 5. Scenario Analysis

| Scenario | Prob. | Core EPS | Revenue | Key Driver | Stock |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Bull** | 30% | **$3.55–3.75** | $7.4–7.5B | Cat load well under the ~3.1 pts embedded in BI; **zero or favorable** casualty PYD; PI underlying CR low-to-mid 80s; LP returns above 7.6% | **+4% to +7%** |
| **Base** | 45% | **$3.20–3.35** | ~$7.35–7.4B | Modest beat on benign cats, partially offset by continued GL/commercial auto strengthening of $50–100mm; EB loss ratio flat to slightly worse | **−1% to +2%** |
| **Bear** | 25% | **$2.70–2.95** | ~$7.3B | Third consecutive GL/commercial auto charge >$150mm; group disability loss ratio >75%; PI auto PIF decline steepens; middle/large retention deteriorates further | **−5% to −9%** |

**What makes Bull happen:** The cat math does most of the work. Management says PYD is immaterial, reaffirms the middle & large CR improvement, and the Hartford Funds/Wellington close stays on track for Q1 2027. Buyback pace at $475mm/quarter plus a benign cat quarter supports a TTM core ROE pushing toward 19%.

**What makes Bear happen:** Any new casualty charge. The Q2 2026 reaction is the template — HIG beat by $0.21 and fell 2.9%. In this tape, reserve credibility outranks EPS. A bear print would be a *beat* on core EPS with a charge that the market reads as the second data point in a trend.

**Historical calibration:** HIG has beaten in **7 of the last 8 quarters**, and in 3 of the last 4 with an average surprise of **+13.1%**. But the reaction function has decoupled from the beat: Q2 2025 +4.3% (on a +20% surprise), Q4 2025 +3.5% (EPS $4.06 vs. $3.20), Q2 2026 **−2.9%** (on a +6.5% surprise). The modal outcome is a beat; the modal *reaction* depends entirely on reserves.
