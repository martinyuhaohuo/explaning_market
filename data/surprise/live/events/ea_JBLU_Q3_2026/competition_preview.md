## Bottom line

This is not an earnings print. It is a credit event with an earnings release attached.

The operating story is actually *improving* — JetBlue raised Q3 RASM guidance by 4pts at the midpoint in September on genuinely strong demand. But a record jet-fuel shock has collided with the weakest balance sheet in the US industry ($8.5B debt, zero hedges), and on Oct 25 the *Wall Street Journal* reported creditors holding the $2B TrueBlue 2031 bonds and a $742M first-lien loan have retained PJT Partners and Akin Gump. Those bonds trade at 42 cents. The stock is down ~45% this month and sits at an all-time low.

**The number that matters tomorrow is not EPS. It is Sept-30 liquidity and the status of the TrueBlue 1.75x debt-service-coverage test, which went live in September 2026.** A failure doesn't trigger default — it redirects loyalty cash to debt repayment, which is the mechanism that would turn a bad year into a restructuring.

## Key metrics to watch — ranked

1. **Sept-30 liquidity and TrueBlue covenant status (critical).** $750M minimum quarter-end liquidity, tested quarterly, plus a 1.75x debt-service-coverage test effective September 2026. June 30 liquidity was ~$2.2B unrestricted cash and investments plus an undrawn $600M revolver, so the $750M floor is not the near-term binding constraint — the coverage ratio is. Any hedged or non-committal language here is the bear trigger.
2. **Q4 2026 fuel guidance (critical).** Q3 was guided at $3.96/gal. October spot is $4.63–$4.90/gal, with Argus at a record $206/bbl and analysts floating $225/bbl by year-end. Q4 is seasonally weaker *and* structurally more expensive. My rough math: Q4 needs ~+24% RASM just to hold the operating line flat on fuel alone. Expect a Q4 guide implying a wider loss than Q3.
3. **Fuel recapture rate.** Q2 recaptured ~50% vs. a 30–40% target — a genuine positive. Q3 recapture above 60% would materially change the 2027 bridge.
4. **CASM ex-fuel.** Raised to +6–8% from +2–4.5% on capacity cuts and Northeast ATC/weather disruption. Watch whether this is transitory deleverage or structural.
5. **JetForward and the 2027 profitability commitment.** $470M cumulative incremental EBIT through June 2026; $310M targeted for 2026 but only $25M realized in 1H. Reaffirming "sustained operating profitability in 2027" matters more for the credit than for the equity. Withdrawal of the $850–950M by-2027 target would be read as capitulation.
6. **Financing actions.** ~67% of PP&E and intangibles are already pledged. Watch for any new facility against the claimed ~$6B unencumbered asset base, sale-leasebacks, or an exchange offer.

## Catalyst checklist

1. **Sept-30 liquidity $ figure + TrueBlue 1.75x DSCR commentary** — determines whether loyalty cash gets trapped. Single highest-variance item.
2. **Q4 fuel assumption vs. $4.63–4.90 spot** — sets the 4Q cash-burn trajectory. UBS models ~$660M 2H26 burn to ~$1.6B year-end liquidity; a worse fuel guide breaks that.
3. **Q4 RASM guide vs. the ~+24% needed to offset fuel** — anything below ~+18% confirms recapture is topping out. Susquehanna explicitly flagged Q4 unit revenue as the key risk.
4. **Demand elasticity language** — the Sept 8-K claimed "no meaningful signs of elasticity." Industry evidence is now mixed: US airfares +22.4% YoY in September, Thanksgiving fares +~30%, and analysts report softness among "mass affluent" consumers. A change in tone here re-rates the whole sector.
5. **2027 operating profitability reaffirmation** — the load-bearing assumption in every bull case and every recovery value for the bonds.

## Risks to this view

- **Q3 fuel should be reliable.** The $3.96 guide was set Sept 10 with three weeks left in the quarter, so the October spike to $4.90 lands in Q4, not Q3. Don't over-extrapolate the record headline into tomorrow's P&L.
- **The maturity wall is not imminent.** $236M remaining in 2026, $478M in 2027, $582M in 2028, then $1.835B in 2029. Creditors organizing is a negotiating posture, not a deadline — JetBlue has real runway to execute an out-of-court exchange, which is arguably the modal credit outcome.
- **Demand has repeatedly beaten the bears in 2026.** Every quarter this year, elasticity fears have proven premature. A third consecutive RASM beat plus recapture above 60% would force a rethink of the cash-burn path.
- **Conversely:** a strong operating print will not save the equity if the covenant answer is evasive. Separate the two in real time.

**Positioning:** into the print I'd be flat the equity and own optionality. The operating trend genuinely deserves credit, but consensus is set at the good end of a September guide, and the asymmetry around a $2.28 stock with a trapped-cash covenant test live for the first time this quarter is unfavorable. The trade is after the liquidity number, not before it.
