## Prior Quarter (Q2 2026, reported July 28)

From the company's 8-K earnings release:
- Revenue $1.900B, down 1.6% year over year
- Diluted EPS $0.91 (GAAP), up from $0.90. Benzinga's $0.61 figure appears to be a mismatch and should be disregarded.
- Net income $72.8M
- Gross margin about 19.0%, down from about 19.7% a year earlier (my calculation)
- Aftermarket revenue $645.7M, up 1.5%, about 64% of gross profit
- Absorption ratio 130.8%, down from 135.5%
- U.S. Class 8 unit sales flat at 3,172, market share 5.8%

**Guidance:** No numeric guidance was given. Management said the second half of 2026 will be "considerably stronger" than the first half for Class 8 sales. It also said Q1 likely marked the industry trough. Medium-duty is expected to return to about 2025 levels. ACT Research forecasts 2026 U.S. Class 8 retail sales up 7.6%. That is a third-party figure, not company guidance.

**Capital return:** A three-for-two stock split and a 10.5% dividend increase to $0.14 per share (post-split) were announced with Q2. Confirm whether the Q3 EPS consensus is split-adjusted, since older figures may not be.

## Key Metrics to Watch (ranked)

1. **Class 8 unit sales and the second-half ramp.** The Q2 call set up a stronger H2. Watch whether Q3 unit volume and backlog support that.
2. **Gross margin.** It fell year over year in Q2. Watch whether new-truck and used-truck pricing offset the decline.
3. **Aftermarket growth (parts, service, collision).** It was up only 1.5% in Q2. Growth is the main driver of gross profit and earnings stability.
4. **Absorption ratio.** It was 130.8% in Q2, down from 135.5%. It shows whether fixed-cost coverage is holding.
5. **Q4 guidance tone.** There is no numeric guide, so the qualitative H2 language and the Q4 consensus ($0.68 EPS) will set the reaction.
