## 1. The Setup in One Paragraph

This is a **cash-flow print, not an EPS print**. Q3 deliveries are already public (161 jets, reported Oct 13), so the revenue line is largely de-risked and the swing factors are (a) BCA operating margin on known volume, (b) whether Q3 FCF stays positive after absorbing a $700M DOJ payment, and (c) whether management holds the FY26 $1–3B FCF bar that requires a very large Q4 delivery ramp. The optical YoY comparison is meaningless — Q3 2025 carried a $4.9B 777X reach-forward charge (−$7.47 EPS). Positioning is crowded long (10-day call/put 3.45, 89th percentile) into a stock that has already given back 15% since July, which cuts both ways.

## 4. Key Metrics to Watch (ranked)

**1. Q3 free cash flow vs. the "low hundreds of millions" guide — the single most important number.**
Q3 absorbs a $700M DOJ payment. Printing positive FCF *through* that outflow is the proof point for the turnaround narrative; printing negative resets the FY26 $1–3B range as unreachable. Q2 was +$631M, and management conceded that was flattered by favorable receipt timing — so the underlying run-rate is thinner than it looked.

**2. FY26 FCF guidance language: "on track for $1–3B" vs. "low end."**
Here is the arithmetic problem, and it's the crux of the print:

| Program | FY target | YTD (9M) | Q4 implied need | Q3 run-rate |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 737 | 500 | 367 | **133 (44/mo)** | 41/mo |
| 787 | 90–100 | 62 | **28–38 (9–13/mo)** | 7.3/mo |

737 at 44/month is achievable if rate 47 is genuinely stable, but it requires the best quarter of the recovery. **787 is the harder problem** — the line is stabilized at 8/month, so even the low end of the 90-unit target needs ~9.3/month, and 100 is not reachable. Expect the 787 target to be cut to ~88–92 or dropped. If both numbers hold *and* FCF is positive, that's the bull case; a 787 trim with FCF intact is the base case.

**3. BCA operating margin (Q2: −2.7%).**
This is the main EPS swing factor. On $11.67B of revenue, **each 100bp of BCA margin ≈ $117M ≈ $0.15 of EPS.** My segment build (BCA −2.7%, BDS $237M, BGS $980M, less typical unallocated/eliminations and ~$470M net interest) lands near breakeven pretax — which is how you get to a ~−$0.20 consensus. BCA reaching 0% would add ~$0.35; slipping to −5% would cost ~$0.35. Watch whether higher volume is actually absorbing fixed cost or whether abnormal production costs and the SPEEA wage step are eating it.

**4. BDS margin and charge risk (FY guide ~2.5%).**
Q2 was −2.2% GAAP on a **$280M VC-25B charge** (3.5% clean). VC-25B, KC-46, MQ-25, Starliner and T-7A are all serial charge candidates. A clean BDS quarter at 3%+ is a quiet but real positive; another nine-figure charge undoes the EPS line.

**5. 777X certification progress — tail risk of another charge.**
At Q2, 55% of certification flight testing was complete, with approval of the final phases expected in 2H26 and first 777-9 delivery still in 2027. The program has already absorbed $4.9B in 2025 charges plus an engine-durability fix still being certified with the supplier and FAA. Any language that slips 2027 is a multi-billion-dollar reach-forward loss risk.

**6. 737-10 commercialization.**
The FAA certified the 737-10 on **Oct 20, 2026** — the stock's most recent genuine catalyst, unlocking a 1,400–1,500 unit order book. Watch for confirmation of first delivery timing (2027) and whether the −7 follows.

## 6. Catalyst Checklist

1. **Q3 FCF vs. "low hundreds of millions" (post-$700M DOJ)** — the entire turnaround thesis rests on cash inflecting; this is the number the buy-side trades.
2. **FY26 $1–3B FCF: reaffirmed, narrowed to low end, or cut** — a trim is partly expected, so the reaction hinges on *why* (timing vs. cost).
3. **787 FY delivery target (90–100)** — mathematically stretched at 62 YTD and 8/month. A cut here is the most likely negative headline; a reaffirmation is a genuine upside surprise.
4. **BCA margin trajectory** — needs visible progress toward breakeven; ~$0.15 of EPS per 100bp.
5. **New charges (777X / VC-25B / KC-46)** — the recurring tail risk that has repeatedly broken the narrative.
6. **Rate 52 timing on the Everett North line** and 737-10 first-delivery confirmation — the 2027 EPS/FCF bridge.

**Already resolved, don't trade it twice:** the SPEEA strike risk cleared on **Oct 1, 2026** when professional (67.6%) and technical (53.5%) units ratified four-year contracts, after rejecting the first offer in August. This removed a threat to 777-9 and MAX 10 certification, but added cost — 10% wages effective Oct 2 plus 4% in March — a modest Q4 margin headwind, not Q3.
