**Report date:** October 26, 2026 (pre-event) · **Event:** Tuesday, October 27, 2026, ~6:00 a.m. ET release / 8:30 a.m. ET call (10:00 UTC) · **Stock:** $67.90 (Oct 26, –3.2% on the day)

## 1. The Setup in One Paragraph

The quarter itself is close to a non-event; the 2027 bridge is the trade. Centene has already banked $5.88 of adjusted EPS in 1H26 ($3.37 in Q1 + $2.51 in Q2) against a full-year guide of "greater than $4.80" reaffirmed as recently as September 16 at the Deutsche Bank Healthcare Summit. Consensus of ~$0.10 for Q3 and an implied **Q4 loss of roughly $1.00–$1.10** means the sell-side already models a sharply negative 2H. With the stock up ~71% YTD and sitting within ~6% of its 52-week high after a run from $31.63, the bar has moved from "can they beat?" to "can they underwrite 2027?" Management flagged that ~$0.50 of Q2's EPS came from non-recurring prior-year items, and three structural 2027 headwinds — Medicaid work requirements starting in January, the post-subsidy ACA risk pool, and a severe Star Ratings downgrade now under litigation — all land in the same guide.

## 4. Key Metrics to Watch — Ranked

**1. Medicaid HBR vs. 93.5% FY target (highest signal).** Q2 ran 93.9%. Molina printed a 93.0% Medicaid MCR on Oct 22 and lifted its FY26 rate assumption to 4.5% (from 4.0–4.5%), while raising its cost-trend assumption to 5.5% from 5.0% — rates offsetting trend, not beating it. A Centene Medicaid HBR at or below ~93.5% validates the margin-restoration thesis; above 94.5% breaks it. Watch specifically whether states are embedding **explicit acuity adjustments** ahead of the January work-requirement start.

**2. FY26 guidance revision.** Mechanically, in-line Q3 puts 9M EPS near $5.98 against a ">$4.80" guide. Anything short of a raise to the ">$5.00–$5.25" zone will be read as management pre-funding a weak Q4 or a weak 2027.

**3. Any 2027 framework.** The single biggest swing factor. Consensus sits near $5.30. Management must bridge over ~$0.50 of non-recurring 2026 items, Medicaid attrition, and the Stars hit. Molina has already anchored peers by guiding preliminary 2027 premium revenue to ≥$45B with building blocks >$9.50/share and –6% Medicaid attrition.

**4. Marketplace margin and morbidity.** The 4.5–5.0% margin guide was Q2's biggest upside surprise. With enhanced premium tax credits expired at end-2025, national individual-market enrollment fell 18% (25.2M → 20.5M June-to-June) and Centene's own book fell from 5.9M to 3.5M. Ambetter enters 55 new counties for 2027 while exiting Delaware and New Hampshire. The question is whether the remaining pool's acuity is priced — median industry premium increases of ~15% for 2027 suggest the market thinks the risk pool keeps deteriorating.

**5. Medicare — Stars, the lawsuit, and the MA reset.** Centene **filed suit against CMS in Georgia district court on October 26, 2026** (the day before the print), challenging 17 measures used in the 2027 Star Ratings on statutory-authority and notice-and-comment grounds, and quantifying ~**$448M** of lost 2028 quality bonus payments and rebates. Members in 4+ star plans fell to 12% from 16% (one sell-side source puts the decline at 18% → 9%; the two counts use different denominators — confirm on the call). Two contracts now carry low-performance indicators. Offsets: Centene priced **below benchmark in all 34 Part D regions**, setting up auto-assignment gains, and has cut ~3,000 plans and exited 344 counties targeting MA breakeven-or-better in 2027.

**What each requires operationally.** *Bull:* behavioral-health and fraud-detection cost initiatives flowing through, plus states granting mid-year acuity true-ups. *Base:* rate updates roughly offsetting a 5–5.5% Medicaid trend — the Molina template. *Bear:* the healthiest members leaving Medicaid and the marketplace faster than pricing assumed, so per-member cost rises while revenue falls.

## 6. Catalyst Checklist

1. **Medicaid HBR vs. 93.5%** — the entire margin-restoration thesis and the 70% YTD re-rating rest on this line converging toward target rather than drifting up.
2. **FY26 guide: ">$5.00" or better vs. a reaffirm at ">$4.80"** — a reaffirm after $5.88 of 1H EPS is a de facto Q4 warning.
3. **2027 EPS framework vs. ~$5.30 consensus** — including how much of 2026's ~$0.50 of non-recurring benefit management concedes, and whether work-requirement attrition is sized (Molina's –6% is the reference point).
4. **Stars quantification and litigation posture** — management's own 2028 dollar estimate against the ~$448M in the complaint, and whether any of it is assumed recoverable. Note two prior insurer suits (Alignment, Healthfirst) and a precedent ruling that found 20 measures unlawful, which CMS still applied to 2027 for everyone but Clover.
5. **Marketplace 2027 pricing/enrollment posture** — state-by-state margin-vs.-membership tradeoff, the 55-county expansion, and early open-enrollment signal (OEP opened Oct 15).
6. *Secondary:* capital deployment — buyback pace at ~$33.5B market cap, further debt paydown after Q1's $1.0B, and the CFO transition (Chris Neczypor effective January 1; Drew Asher retained through 2027 for 2028 bids).
