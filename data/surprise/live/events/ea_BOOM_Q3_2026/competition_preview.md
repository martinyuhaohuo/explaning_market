## 1. The one thing that matters

This is not a normal earnings print. The Munera family's put option on the **40% minority stake in Arcadia Products became exercisable September 6, 2026** — the first time in the deal's history. The redeemable noncontrolling interest sits on the balance sheet at **$187.1M**, versus DMC's entire **~$112M equity market cap**. A claim worth 1.7x the market cap just became live, and as of the Q2 call management said plainly they had "absolutely no idea" whether the partner would exercise.

No 8-K announcing an exercise has surfaced in public filings between September 6 and this print. That means Q3 is the first scheduled disclosure window where the status gets confirmed one way or the other. **The capital-structure disclosure will almost certainly dominate the operating result in the stock reaction.**

Settlement mechanics if exercised (per Q2 call, CFO Walter): either 100% cash, or 20% cash plus 80% convertible preferred. Preferred conversion is capped at 19.9% of outstanding shares absent a shareholder vote; mandatory redemption runs over three years and is contingent on a board solvency determination. The June 2025 credit amendment accommodates this — max leverage steps temporarily to 3.5x (from 3.0x) if the put or call is exercised, then 3.25x, then back to 3.0x, with a $50M delayed-draw term loan whose proceeds may be held in a restricted account for the purchase.

### ⚠️ The consensus EPS number is not comparable to anything management reports

This is the most important calibration point in the preview, and most screens get it wrong.

In Q2 FY2026 DMC reported net income attributable to DMC of **$507K** on 20.24M diluted shares — that is **$0.025/share**. Yet the reported GAAP diluted EPS was **$0.10**. The gap is a line item in the EPS reconciliation: **"Adjustment of redeemable noncontrolling interest," +$1,541K in Q2** (+$806K for the six months). Under redeemable-NCI accounting, changes in the Arcadia put's redemption value run through the EPS numerator. Adjusted diluted EPS for the same quarter was **$0.04**.

So BOOM's GAAP EPS is driven by a **non-cash, non-operating remeasurement of the Arcadia put** — the same instrument now in play. Consequences:

- A $0.11 "consensus EPS" is effectively unforecastable and will be met or missed for reasons unrelated to the business.
- On the adjusted basis management actually guides to, the $10–13M EBITDA range maps to roughly **$0.04–0.06 of adjusted diluted EPS** (bridge below) — i.e. an **in-line-to-good operating quarter still prints a ~50% headline "EPS miss."**
- **Watch adjusted EBITDA attributable to DMC against the $10–13M guide. Ignore the EPS headline for the first 30 minutes.**

*Adjusted EPS bridge, base case:* consolidated adj. EBITDA ~$15.1M (incl. the 40% of Arcadia excluded from the attributable figure) → less D&A ~$8.0M → EBIT ~$7.1M → less net interest ~$1.4M → pre-tax ~$5.7M → tax at ~38% → ~$3.5M → less ~$2.6M Arcadia NCI → **~$0.9M attributable ≈ $0.04–0.05 adj. diluted EPS.** This reconciles to Q2's actual $0.04 on $10.7M attributable EBITDA.

## 3. Segment model and what to watch

Q2 FY2026 exit rates (segment adj. EBITDA sums to $14.2M; corporate/unallocated drag ≈ –$3.5M):

| Segment | Q2 FY26 sales | Q1 FY26 | Q2 margin | Q1 margin | My Q3 est. | Q3 margin est. |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Arcadia Products | $67.4M (+9% YoY, +19% q/q) | $56.7M | 13.6% | 6.9% | $67M | 13.5% |
| DynaEnergetics | $67.4M (flat YoY, +13% q/q) | $59.5M | 8.4% | 4.6% | $70M | 8.5% |
| NobelClad | $22.2M (–17% YoY, +15% q/q) | $19.3M | 13.7% | 9.8% | $25M | 14.5% |
| **Total** | **$157.0M** | **$135.5M** | 9.1% cons. | 4.0% cons. | **~$162M** | **~$11.4M attrib.** |

**Ranked by importance to the print:**

1. **DynaEnergetics margin ex-tariff-refund.** Q2's 8.4% included a **$1.5M tariff refund** — roughly 2.2 points of segment margin. Underlying was ~6.2%. Management explicitly assumed **no further refunds in Q3** and compared their ability to forecast recovery to forecasting "whether the Straits of Hormuz will be open." Holding 8%+ without the refund is the single cleanest read on whether the recovery is operational or accounting.
2. **Arcadia margin durability against aluminum.** Management has flagged aluminum volatility as the standing headwind, and higher aluminum *helped* reported Q2 revenue (prices +79% YoY). In Q3 the direction reversed: LME 3M averaged ~$3,225/t after peaking at $3,651 in late May, and the **US Midwest premium fell from 108.7¢/lb (early July) to 95¢/lb in September (–8.2%)**, with October/November contracts down >12% post-tariff announcement. Falling input prices are a revenue headwind with an ambiguous near-term margin effect depending on inventory lag. Does 13.6% hold?
3. **Arcadia volume/share recovery.** O'Leary cited "five, going on six months" of consistently up daily storefront business and estimated they've recovered roughly **half** of the share lost in the 2024 disruptions. Watch for a seventh/eighth consecutive month and any quantification of the remaining half.
4. **NobelClad conversion.** Q2 was hurt by customers delaying delivery acceptance; management said delays would "abate" in Q3 and the backlog is "healthy" (unquantified). Guidance explicitly leans on these shipments. If NobelClad doesn't step up, the guide midpoint is hard to hit.
5. **Free cash flow and net debt.** Net debt rose to **$30.5M** from $18.7M at YE25; H1 operating cash flow was **–$10.3M** on working-capital build, capex just $3.4M. Deleveraging is O'Leary's stated priority and the precondition for absorbing any Arcadia settlement in cash. A third straight quarter of cash burn materially worsens the put math.

**Macro tailwind worth noting:** the North American completions market tightened through Q3. ~195 active frac spreads in October with Primary Vision expecting ~200 through November; Halliburton described a "very tight" NAM frac market with crews moving at better pricing; Liberty noted completions activity absorbing available capacity. That supports DynaEnergetics *volume*. Pricing is a different story — management called the fragmented perforating-gun market one where "pricing is still hard to come by" with "no price increases" available.

## 5. Trading setup

- **The entire Q2 pop has round-tripped.** BOOM closed at **$5.42 before** the July 29 print, spiked to $6.26, and trades at **~$5.42 today**. The market has fully given back a 131% EPS beat and a high-end-of-guide quarter.
- **Momentum is negative:** –13.5% over 1 month, –14.6% over 3 months, –26.5% over 12 months. The stock sits ~16% above its March 13 low of $4.69 and ~41% below its January 29 high of $9.20.
- **Options-implied move: not reliably obtainable.** At a ~$112M cap the BOOM chain is thin and strike coverage is sparse; I found elevated IV flagged in deep-ITM contracts but no credible straddle-implied move for the October expiry. **Use the ~13–14% realized average as the planning number** rather than a quoted implied move. Treat any single-strike IV reading on this name as noise.
- **Valuation hinges entirely on the put.** On the attributable run-rate (~$45M annualized adj. EBITDA), EV/EBITDA is ~**3.2x** ($112M cap + $30.5M net debt = ~$143M EV). Add the $187.1M redeemable NCI as a real claim and EV becomes ~$330M against ~$60M consolidated run-rate EBITDA — ~**5.5x**. That spread *is* the bull/bear debate. Nothing in the operating print resolves it; only the put disclosure does.
- **Asymmetry read:** estimates have been cut into the print (ESP –14.29%), the stock has given back all of its last beat, and consensus revenue sits below the guide midpoint — a low bar operationally. Against that, the binary capital-structure event carries left-tail risk that no operating beat can offset. The setup favors a positive operating surprise with a negative structural tail.

## 6. Catalyst checklist

1. **Arcadia put/call status** — exercised, not exercised, or renegotiated? If exercised: cash vs. 20/80 preferred, and the dilution path against the 19.9% cap. *Single largest driver of the reaction.*
2. **Adj. EBITDA attributable to DMC vs. the $10–13M guide** — the only P&L line that is clean and management-comparable. Not EPS.
3. **DynaEnergetics segment margin, bridged ex-tariff-refund** — is the recovery operational or was Q2 flattered by $1.5M?
4. **Q4 FY2026 guidance** — Q4 is seasonally weaker for both Arcadia (construction) and DynaEnergetics (operator budget exhaustion, holidays). A guide below ~$150M / ~$8M confirms the bear case.
5. **Operating cash flow and net debt** — reversal of the H1 working-capital build, or a third consecutive quarter of burn against a live $187M claim.
6. **Arcadia margin vs. aluminum** — whether 13.6% is a new floor or a Q2 peak, with MWP down ~13% from July.

## 7. Limitations and unverified items

- **Consensus is 3 analysts.** Two sources disagree on revenue ($161.1M vs. $164.3M) and EPS ($0.105 vs. $0.10). Dispersion this wide at this coverage level means "beat" and "miss" are soft concepts here.
- **No whisper number located.** Buy-side survey data is not available for a sub-$150M-cap name; none should be inferred.
- **Put-option status is inferred, not confirmed.** I found no 8-K disclosing an exercise between September 6 and October 27, but the absence of a filing in search results is weaker evidence than a direct EDGAR confirmation, and some exercise mechanics may not require immediate 8-K disclosure. Treat the status as genuinely unknown going in.
- **NobelClad backlog is unquantified** in all public materials reviewed; management describes it only as "healthy." The Q2 investor deck does not disclose backlog, leverage ratios, or formal cost-savings program figures.
- **No full 8-quarter reaction table.** The ±13.71% average and –33%/+36% range come from an aggregated options-data source rather than a per-quarter table I could reconstruct independently. Q2 FY2026's +15.5% is directly sourced.
- **Prior-year Q3 segment detail** was not available in the materials reviewed, so YoY segment comparisons below the revenue line are not included.
