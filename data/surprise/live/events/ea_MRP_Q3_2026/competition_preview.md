## 1. The setup in one paragraph

Millrose — the Lennar land-banking REIT spun off in February 2025 — has delivered six consecutive quarters of clean, on-model results: zero option terminations since inception, a 9.2% portfolio yield, and six straight dividend raises. The stock has nonetheless fallen ~17% since the Q2 print (Aug 4, $28.84) and now trades at **0.68x book and ~8.3x earnings with a ~13% dividend yield** — levels that say the market no longer believes the reported economics. The proximate cause is an **October 2 Hunterbrook Media short report** alleging Millrose quietly bought ~1,096 finished homes from Lennar (~$290M, 16 states) and rented them to the public at a net yield below its own cost of debt, after telling investors it would have "no tenants." Securities class actions followed (class period Feb 7, 2025 – Oct 2, 2026; lead plaintiff deadline Dec 8, 2026), Citizens and Citigroup downgraded to Market Perform, and **Millrose has not publicly rebutted the allegations.** This is therefore not a numbers print. The quarter itself will almost certainly be fine; the stock will trade on what management discloses about build-to-rent, leverage, and governance at 10:00 AM ET.

## 4. Key metrics to watch, ranked by what actually moves the stock

**1. Build-to-rent disclosure — by far the most important line in the release.**
What's needed: carrying value of BTR homes, unit count, occupancy/lease-up status, **net yield after taxes, insurance, maintenance and property management**, and whether there is a hard cap. Hunterbrook's math: 8.44% gross rent, **~5.92% after taxes and insurance**, against 6.50%/6.75% coupons on the notes issued October 6 — i.e. negative spread before corporate overhead. If management confirms a genuine rent-to-own program with leases signed *before* purchase (what Millrose reportedly told Goldman Sachs), the short thesis weakens sharply. If BTR is disclosed as a plain rental book, the "no tenants" disclosure problem becomes a reported fact.

**2. Q4 exit AFFO run-rate guidance, pro forma for Dream Finders/Beazer.** The $572.1M funded Oct 20 plus the remaining commitment should push the exit run-rate meaningfully above $0.80. Anything at or below $0.82 implies offsetting drag — interest expense, BTR dilution, or slower Lennar recycling.

**3. Leverage versus the 33% ceiling.** Q2 ended at ~30% debt-to-cap ($2.5B debt on ~$5.8B book equity). Layering on the $1.0B of senior notes and a $500M delayed-draw term loan — net of debt repayment from proceeds — plausibly takes gross debt to **$3.5–4.0B, or roughly 38–41% debt-to-cap, through the stated maximum.** *(This is my arithmetic from disclosed figures, not a reported number.)* Management already signaled on the Q2 call it was "thinking through what is an appropriate leverage target." Formally abandoning the 33% commitment while the stock sits at 0.68x book is a credibility event, not a technicality.

**4. Lennar concentration and the recycling engine.** Lennar was **72% of Q2 revenue**; the Lennar Master Program is 68% of invested capital at an 8.5% yield versus 10.6% on the 19-counterparty "other agreements" book. Lennar's own Q3 (Sept 16) was weak: **orders −9%, deliveries −3%, gross margin 15.8%, ASP down to $372K, FY26 deliveries cut to 80–81K from 82–83K.** Fewer Lennar starts means slower homesite takedowns, slower capital recycling, and longer option durations. Watch the net cash proceeds line (Q1: $726M; Q2: $1.0B) — a sequential decline is the leading indicator of the thesis cracking.

**5. Dividend action.** $0.77/qtr is ~96% of my AFFO estimate. A seventh consecutive raise is the expected signal of confidence; **holding flat would be read as a defensive crouch** and is probably worth several points on its own.

**6. Option terminations.** Zero since inception is the load-bearing statistic for the entire asset class. The first termination — or any disclosed option restructuring, extension, or price modification — changes the multiple, not the quarter.

**7. Capital allocation at 0.68x book.** Management has committed not to issue equity below book. With the dividend consuming nearly all AFFO, growth can only come from leverage and recycling. **The buyback question is the cleanest test of whether management believes its own book value.**

**8. Governance.** Any mention of a special committee, independent board review, conflicts process for related-party Lennar transactions, or litigation reserve.

| Scenario | Revenue | GAAP EPS / AFFO | Key driver | Stock reaction |

| **Bull (~25%)** | $207–212M | $0.80 / $0.83 | Detailed BTR disclosure with pre-signed leases and a hard cap; BTR <3% of invested capital; Q4 exit run-rate guided **>$0.85** on DFH/Beazer; 7th dividend raise; buyback authorized; credible point-by-point rebuttal of the short report | **+8% to +15%** |
| **Base (~50%)** | $201–206M | $0.77–0.79 / $0.80–0.81 | In-line-to-slightly-light revenue, small EPS beat; BTR acknowledged and quantified but economics left vague; dividend to ~$0.78; leverage target "under review"; zero terminations intact | **−3% to +5%** |
| **Bear (~25%)** | <$200M | ≤$0.75 / ≤$0.78 | BTR book larger than $290M and still growing with no cap; BTR impairment or fair-value mark; recycling proceeds fall sharply on Lennar's cut starts; dividend held flat; 33% leverage ceiling formally abandoned; litigation reserve; no substantive response to allegations | **−10% to −20%** |

**What management commentary signals each:** The bull case requires *numbers* on BTR — occupancy percentages, average lease terms, net yield, conversion-to-sale rates. The bear case is signaled by abstraction: "opportunistic," "small relative to the portfolio," "we don't comment on litigation." Given this management has not responded publicly in the 24 days since the report, **deflection on the call is the modal risk**, and the market has already shown it will not wait.

**Historical calibration:** MRP has been a famously quiet earnings stock — median news reaction ~+0.15% at 30 minutes, with half of all events landing between −0.45% and +1.07%; Q2 FY26 produced +0.66%. **Do not anchor on that history.** The relevant recent precedents are the non-earnings moves: **−8.5% on the Oct 2 short report** and **+4.7% on Oct 15**. The Q1 FY26 print is the better template for downside — −4.6% on day one and **−12.3% over the following week** — because it shows this holder base sells into ambiguity with a lag.

## 6. Catalyst checklist — the five things that decide the reaction

1. **BTR net yield, disclosed explicitly** vs. the 6.50%/6.75% note coupons. Anything below ~6.5% net confirms Hunterbrook's core economic claim and is very hard to walk back.
2. **Q4 exit AFFO run-rate** vs. the ~$0.80 Q2 exit. The buy-side needs **>$0.84** to believe the $1.25B Dream Finders/Beazer commitment is accretive rather than leverage-funded yield-chasing.
3. **Pro forma debt-to-capitalization** vs. the 33% maximum. My estimate of ~38–41% means management must either reaffirm the ceiling with a deleveraging path or explicitly raise it. Silence here is itself an answer.
4. **Net cash proceeds from homesite sales** (Q2: $1.0B) as the read-through on Lennar's −9% orders. A drop toward $700M or below reframes MRP from a self-funding compounder into a balance-sheet-constrained one.
5. **Governance and the short-report response.** Special committee, board review, or a point-by-point rebuttal. **The absence of any response is the single largest discretionary risk in this print** — it is the one variable fully within management's control.

- **Implied move (estimated, not quoted):** I could not source a live straddle. Based on realized single-day moves of −8.5% and +4.7% this month against a historical sub-1% earnings reaction, a **6–9% implied move** is a reasonable working assumption — far wider than anything MRP has printed on earnings before. If the options market is pricing materially *less* than 6%, that is a cheap straddle given the binary disclosure risk; if it is pricing more than 10%, premium selling into the call is defensible only if you believe management will engage substantively.
- **Short interest** of 5.05% of shares out is meaningful but not crowded — enough for a squeeze on a credible rebuttal, not enough to drive one by itself.
- **Positioning view:** The in-line-quarter / vague-call combination is the modal outcome and resolves roughly flat to modestly down. The distribution is wide in both directions and genuinely two-sided: 0.68x book with zero terminations and a covered dividend is a real floor *if* the BTR book is small and ring-fenced. Long exposure into the print is a bet on management choosing to engage — which it has conspicuously declined to do for three and a half weeks. **Sizing should reflect that this is a disclosure event, not an earnings event.**

## 8. Questions for the call

1. What is the total carrying value, unit count, and current occupancy of the BTR portfolio as of September 30 — and what is the net yield after taxes, insurance, maintenance, and management fees?
2. Was a tenant or rent-to-own buyer identified before purchase for each home, as reportedly represented to Goldman Sachs? For what percentage?
3. Is there a cap on BTR as a share of invested capital, and will it be reported as a separate segment?
4. Does the 33% maximum debt-to-capitalization commitment still stand pro forma for the $1.0B notes and $500M term loan?
5. Why were the August 27 Founder's Rights Agreement and Management Agreement amendments — which authorized BTR and redefined "Excluded Capital" as the first $2B raised — not accompanied by a strategy disclosure to shareholders?
6. With the stock at 0.68x book and the no-equity-below-book commitment in place, why is capital going into 5.9%-yielding rental homes rather than a buyback?
7. How do Lennar's −9% orders and reduced FY26 delivery target affect expected homesite takedown timing and 2027 recycling?
