## 2. Key metrics to watch — ranked

**1. Subscription revenue growth vs. the "at least 32%" FY bar.** Q1 printed +26.1% ($40.2M), *below* the full-year 32% target — the raise was explicitly back-half weighted. A Q2 subscription print of ~$44M (+32%) says the ramp is on schedule. Anything sub-30% forces the market to assume a very steep H2 and is the most likely source of a de-rating.

**2. FY27 guidance revision.** Agilysys has raised the revenue range at each of the last several prints. A *reiteration* of $368–373M would, in this setup, read as a soft disappointment. Watch whether subscription growth language moves to "at least 33–34%."

**3. Adj. EBITDA margin trajectory — the most under-appreciated item.** Q1 came in at 20.8% against a 24% full-year target. If Q2 lands at ~22%, H1 is ~21.4% and **H2 must average ~26.4%** to hit the FY number — a ~500bp half-over-half expansion. Management needs a credible bridge (subscription mix, services leverage, headcount). Gross margin direction also matters: 61.7% in Q2 FY26 → 63.5% in Q1 FY27.

**4. Marriott rollout cadence.** PMS is live at 2,000+ select-service properties; the cloud guest-room platform is in an initial 40 US/Canada hotels. Premium and luxury tiers — the higher-ARPU portion — have **not yet hit the financials**, and management has called the implementation "complex," spanning 18–24 months. Guidance embeds conservative cadence. Pull-forward is upside; a slip is a narrative problem more than a numbers problem this quarter.

**5. Bookings and backlog conversion.** Q1 was the best-ever Q1 for sales, including three seven-figure multi-product ecosystem wins; Needham has called the trailing six months the best bookings stretch in company history. Backlog conversion rate is the tell on FY28 subscription growth.

**6. International / RFP inclusion.** Management flagged difficulty getting included in European and APAC RFPs, plus price sensitivity across a large slice of the $16B TAM. APAC wins (e.g., the nine-property Australian resort group taken from the main competitor) are the proof points.

**7. FCF and capital allocation.** Q1 FCF swung to +$7.3M from –$5.0M; Q2 FY26 was $15.0M. Cash and marketable securities of $123.7M with no meaningful debt — M&A optionality is live and un-modeled.

- *Bull:* specific property counts and go-live dates for Marriott premium/luxury; "ahead of our internal plan" on subscription; an explicit, quantified H2 margin bridge.

- *Bear:* that same annual-basis framing used *without* a guidance raise, hedged language on the 24% EBITDA target, or implementation-capacity constraints cited as a revenue-recognition limiter.

**Asymmetry warning:** the downside tail is wider than the upside. At ~46x forward and ~80x trailing earnings, with the stock roughly doubled off a $61.50 52-week low, there is no valuation support under a decelerating-subscription print.

## 4. Catalyst checklist

1. **Subscription revenue ≥ ~$44M (+32%)** vs. an inferred whisper of ~$44.5M. The FY raise was built on back-half subscription acceleration; Q2 is the first checkpoint. *Highest-weight item.*
2. **FY27 revenue guide moved above $373M.** The buy-side is positioned for another raise — seven of eight analysts at Buy, Oppenheimer modeling 17–17.5% FY27 growth (the *top* of guidance). A reiteration disappoints a crowded long.
3. **Adj. EBITDA margin ≥22% with a credible H2 bridge to 24%.** Two consecutive ~21% quarters make the FY margin target arithmetically hard; the market will mark down the guide itself.
4. **Marriott premium/luxury tier timing.** First concrete commitment on the higher-value tiers is the FY28 growth unlock. Silence here caps the multiple.
5. **Adj. EPS vs. ~$0.58–0.60 whisper** (inferred, not surveyed — AGYS beat 6 of the last 8 quarters and beat Q1 by 22%: $0.49 vs. $0.4025). A $0.55 in-line print may trade as a miss against that pattern.
