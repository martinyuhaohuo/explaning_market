### The single most important setup fact

**The revenue bar sits at the top of the FY guide; the EPS bar does not.** H1'26 sales were $3,228.5M. FY guidance of +16–18% on FY25's ~$5.85B implies $6,789–6,905M, i.e. H2 of **$3,560–3,677M**. A $1.85B Q3 plus a normal seasonal Q4 (~flat to -3% q/q, as in 2025) gets you to ~$6,880M — **the high end**. My build lands $15–35M light of consensus.

Conversely, consensus EPS of $5.75 leaves $5.05–5.35 for Q4 against $4.73 last year (+7–13%) — comfortably achievable. **Expect a revenue "miss," EPS beat.** That is also exactly what happened in Q3'25 (sales $1.50B vs $1.53B; EPS $5.17 vs $4.98) and the stock fell on the revenue line, then rallied +3.8% the next session.

## 2. Key Metrics to Watch — Ranked

**1) Electrical Solutions adjusted operating margin (must be >21.5%, ideally >22%).** Management committed on the Q2 call to ES "return[ing] to adjusted operating margin expansion in the second half of 2026." Q2 ES adj. margin was 21.2%; Q3'25 was 20.8%. If ES prints below ~21.3%, the margin-recovery narrative breaks and the NSI integration gets re-underwritten.

**2) Consolidated adjusted operating margin vs. the FY 23.1–23.4% frame.** This is the quietly hard part. Q2 adj. OM was **23.9%, down 50 bps y/y** — the first contraction of the year. My math on H1 (≈21.95% adj. OM vs ≈21.6% in H1'25, only +30 bps) means **H2 must run 24.1–24.7% vs 23.7% in H2'25 — +40 to +100 bps** to hit the FY guide. Q3 alone needs ~24.3%. The back half carries almost all of the promised 40–70 bps of FY expansion.

**3) Utility Solutions organic growth and book-to-bill.** FY target is +7–9% organic with transmission & substation "double-digit in H2 as large projects ramp." H1 book-to-bill was ~1.2x with orders booking into 2027. Watch for (a) T&S actually hitting double digits, and (b) book-to-bill holding ≥1.0x. A sub-1.0x book-to-bill is the one disclosure that would genuinely damage the 2027 setup.

**4) Grid Automation inflection.** Went -18% in Q3'25, +1% in Q2'26. Meters/AMI "grew sequentially with strong orders." Q3 is the quarter where an easy comp should produce visible positive growth (+5% or better). This is the highest-beta line for a positive surprise.

**5) Data center growth rate.** +65% in Q2, FY target ~+50%. With the AI-capex debate in the tape, any deceleration below ~+40% will be read as a sector datapoint, not just a Hubbell one. Content gains and NSI's datacenter/network infrastructure exposure are the bull case here.

**6) Price/cost and the FY guide.** Pricing is tracking 3–4 pts for FY26 (April increase ~1 pt, July ~0.5 pt in H2) against copper/aluminum/steel inflation. Watch whether price is still fully covering material cost, and whether FY adj. EPS goes to **$20.50–$20.70** (narrow-and-raise) or merely narrows within $20.25–$20.55.

## 4. Catalyst Checklist

1. **ES adjusted operating margin vs. 21.2% (Q2) / 20.8% (Q3'25)** — the explicit management promise of H2 expansion. Binary credibility test.
2. **Consolidated adjusted OM vs. ~24.3% implied** — second consecutive y/y contraction would put the FY 23.1–23.4% frame out of reach and force 2027 estimate cuts.
3. **Utility book-to-bill and 2027 framing** — ≥1.0x with orders extending into 2027 keeps the multi-year story intact; sub-1.0x is the real bear trigger.
4. **FY26 adj. EPS guide: raise to ≥$20.70 vs. mere narrowing** — the whisper at $5.88 already assumes a raise, so a narrow-only is a de facto disappointment.
5. **Data center growth rate and NSI synergy commentary** — the $3.0B NSI deal (closed Jun 9, 2026; ~$570M 2026 revenue, 15.5x EBITDA, +$0.20 EPS in '26 / +$0.80 in '27) is the swing factor on both growth and the leverage critique. Any pull-forward of the $0.80 is a positive catalyst.

## 5. Trading Setup

- **Positioning is already defensive.** Stock is -4.4% over the past month, roughly in line with peers (-4.9%), and sits in the bottom quintile of its 52-week range. It traded as low as ~$445 last week before today's +3.1% bounce to $462. The 12-month return is ~+3% — a full year of multiple compression against double-digit earnings growth. That lowers the bar.
- **Options look expensive, with a caveat.** The widely quoted **±8.9%** figure is derived from the **Nov 20 expiry** straddle, which embeds ~3.5 weeks of post-earnings time value — it is *not* the earnings-day implied move. Backing that out, the event-specific implied move is more likely **~±4.5–5.5%**. Even that is rich versus a trailing average actual move of **2.1–2.7%**; options have over-priced HUBB earnings in 5 of the last 8 reports, with the stock finishing inside the implied band 63–75% of the time.
- **Implication:** the risk/reward favors **defined-risk or premium-selling structures** (short strangle/iron condor, or a put spread financed by an OTM call sale) over long straddles. For directional longs, selling an elevated upside call against stock captures the vol premium while the sub-$465 entry limits downside beta.
- **Peer read-through is mixed and unhelpful:** Allegion +9.6% revenue growth, beat by 3.3%, rallied +12.6%; Acuity Brands +2.9%, missed by 1.1%, flat. No clean sector signal.
- **Sentiment tell:** the board raised the dividend 7% on **Oct 23, four days before the print** — companies rarely do that into a guidance cut. Modest positive skew to the base case.

## Bottom Line

**Expect a modest revenue miss against a stretched $1.85B bar, an EPS beat to ~$5.85–5.95, and a narrow-to-slightly-higher FY guide.** The stock's reaction will be decided not by the top line but by **Electrical Solutions margin** and whether the consolidated adjusted operating margin can deliver the +40 to +100 bps of H2 expansion the FY guide requires after Q2's 50 bps contraction. With the shares near 52-week lows, options over-priced, and a dividend raise days before the print, the setup is **mildly constructive into the number** — but July'26 proved that a beat-and-raise is not sufficient when the market is focused on capex and $4.87B of net debt.
