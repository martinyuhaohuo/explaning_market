The expense assumption ties cleanly to management's H2 guide of $107–109M/quarter. **The provision does not.** On the Q2 call, management explicitly guided charge-offs toward a "reversion to the mean" of roughly **$10M a quarter**, up from the unusually low ~$5M run-rate. ## 3. Key Metrics to Watch — Ranked

### 1. NIM and the FY26 guide — the whole thesis sits here
Management guided FY26 NIM to **3.73–3.75%** assuming no Fed move. With Q1 at 3.70% and Q2 at 3.75%, hitting the full-year range mathematically requires H2 around **3.74–3.78%**. So the base case for Q3 is **3.76–3.78%**, and Zacks' rounded "3.8%" sits at or slightly above the top of what management's own guide implies.

The trap: **a flat 3.75% print is a soft miss**, even though it would extend the "no compression" narrative. Watch for the guide being raised toward 3.75–3.77% — that's the bull tell. Support comes from securities reinvestment yields and the sub-debt retirement; pressure comes from loan competition and deposit repricing, which management said would cause the margin to "stabilize."

### 2. Provision and classified-loan migration
Classified loans rose to **$524M (3.96% of loans)** at 6/30/26 from $498M, driven by CRE (+$29M), continued migration of acquired Penns Woods credits onto NWBI's grading standards, and **downgrades in the healthcare book, specifically nursing homes**. Management insisted NPAs were flat and expects no charge-off follow-through.

Q3 is the quarter that tests that claim. Three things to check, in order: (a) does the classified balance rise a third consecutive quarter; (b) does the healthcare/nursing-home cohort migrate from classified to nonaccrual; (c) is the provision at the ~$10M guided run-rate or above. Consensus NPLs of $90.3M assume essentially flat credit — no cushion for a negative surprise.

### 3. Loan growth composition — a C&I-only story now
Q2 delivered 5.3% annualized growth, with average C&I up $148M (+5.6% QoQ, **+32% YoY**) and nationwide business verticals now ~27% of the C&I book. But management guided **CRE to be "largely flat" in H2** while they "retool" that business with new hires and a revised go-to-market approach to offset elevated runoff.

So total loan growth = C&I production minus CRE runoff. Base case 4–6% annualized. If CRE runoff outruns C&I adds and the loan book is flat-to-down, the 2027 NII bridge gets materially harder and the multiple is exposed.

### 4. Expense run-rate against the $107–109M guide
Adjusted efficiency improved to 56.2% in Q2. Management flagged "upward pressure" from technology, branch, and talent investment — including the Columbus market build-out — and guided $107–109M quarterly for H2 versus $104.3M actual in Q2. A print at or above $109M breaks the four-quarter efficiency streak and will be read as the operating-leverage story topping out. Low end ($107M) is worth roughly a penny versus consensus.

### 5. Capital deployment — the live question post sub-debt
NWBI redeemed all **$115M of its 4.00% fixed-to-floating subordinated notes due 2030 on Sept 15, 2026** (announced Aug 17). Three implications: only ~15 days of Q3 interest-expense benefit, with the full ~2 bp NIM lift arriving in Q4; a possible small write-off of unamortized issuance costs in Q3; and a reduction in Tier 2 capital — total capital ratio was 15.17%, CET1 12.21%, both with ample room.

With that use of capital complete, the **$50M buyback authorized in Q1 2026 remains entirely unused**. Management called it inactive while prioritizing organic growth, the dividend, and sub-debt optimization. Two of those three are now resolved. Activation commentary on the call is a genuine incremental catalyst. The dividend has sat at $0.20 for 127+ consecutive quarters (~56% payout on FY26E $1.41) — a raise would be a real surprise and is not in the price.

## 4. Scenario Analysis

| Scenario | Revenue | EPS | NIM | Key Driver | Stock Reaction |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Bull** (~25%) | $187–190M | $0.38–0.40 | ≥3.80% | Loans +7%+ ann.; provision ≤$6M; expenses at $107M low end; fee income holds $34M+; FY NIM guide nudged up; buyback activated | **+4% to +7%** |
| **Base** (~50%) | $183–185M | $0.35–0.36 | 3.76–3.78% | Loans +4–6% ann.; provision $7–9M; NIE $107–108M; FY NIM guide reaffirmed at 3.73–3.75%; buyback still idle | **–1% to +2%** |
| **Bear** (~25%) | $178–182M | $0.30–0.33 | 3.73–3.75% | CRE runoff > C&I adds, loans flat/down; provision ≥$12M on healthcare migration; classifieds up a 3rd straight quarter; NIE ≥$109M | **–6% to –10%** |

**Bull — what must happen operationally.** C&I verticals sustain the Q2 pace without CRE deteriorating further; securities reinvestment plus the sub-debt retirement push NIM through 3.80% ahead of schedule; classified loans *decline* as Penns Woods migration completes, letting the provision stay near the old $5–6M run-rate. *Management tell:* explicitly calling the Penns Woods credit migration "substantially complete," raising the FY NIM range, and language shifting from buyback "inactive" to opportunistic. *Historical precedent:* Q1 2026 beat $0.35 vs. $0.30 and the stock rose **+4.97%** to $14.16 — but it was trading at ~1.4x TBV then, not 1.6x.

**Base — what must happen.** Everything runs to the guide: margin stabilizes, expenses step up as flagged, provision normalizes toward $10M, loan growth mid-single-digit annualized. *Management tell:* "fifth consecutive quarter" framing on NIM and efficiency, reaffirmed FY ranges, no change to capital priorities. Given the stock is 2.6% from its 52-week high with a consensus price target of $16.29 (+3.5%), an in-line print is more likely to produce a flat-to-down tape than a rally. Note post-earnings drift has historically been muted: **+0.6% over the 91 days** following the Q2 release.

**Bear — what must happen.** The healthcare/nursing-home book is the fault line. A migration from classified into nonaccrual forces a provision well above the $10M guide, and because consensus is only carrying $6–8M, the EPS gap compounds fast — a $14M provision alone is ~$0.04. Pair that with flat loans and expenses at $109M and you get $0.30. *Management tell:* vaguer credit language, a widened FY charge-off range, or declining to reaffirm the FY NIM guide. At 1.59x TBV and +31% YTD, a credit-driven miss de-rates this stock harder than the fundamentals alone would imply.

## 5. Catalyst Checklist

1. **NIM vs. ~3.78% (my implied-guide number, above the 3.75% Q2 print but at/below Zacks' rounded 3.80%)** — four straight quarters of expansion is the core of the re-rating from 11x to 1.6x TBV. The first flat quarter changes the story even if the absolute level is fine.
2. **Provision vs. consensus ~$6–8M against management's own ~$10M guide** — the clearest identifiable gap between the sell-side model and what the company told investors in July. Resolution direction determines whether this is a $0.33 or $0.37 quarter.
3. **Classified loans vs. $524M / 3.96%** — a third consecutive increase, especially if healthcare-driven, overrides a good EPS print. A decline validates the "migration, not deterioration" framing and is the quietest bull catalyst on the list.
4. **Total loan growth and the CRE line specifically** — buy-side wants confirmation that "largely flat" CRE means flat, not down, and that the retooled CRE team plus 27%-of-book nationwide C&I verticals can carry 5%+ total growth into 2027.
5. **Capital deployment framing on the Oct 27 call** — with sub-debt retired, the $50M authorization is the obvious next lever. Also listen for: first 2027 NIM/outlook framing, M&A appetite (Penns Woods hit static TBV earnback in **11 months versus a 2.9-year projection**, with crossover expected by end-Q4 2026 — a result that makes another deal credible), and any update on the Columbus expansion's expense trajectory.
