**Key gap:** Consensus EPS sits about $0.11 (roughly 5%) above the guidance midpoint of $2.31. Unless estimates have already come down, the print needs to beat the top of guidance to meet the Street. Estimate revisions into the print will tell you whether that gap has closed.

## Key Metrics to Watch (ranked)

1. **Q3 comparable RevPAR vs. ~4.0% guide.** This is the core driver. The World Cup and calendar shifts are the stated Q3 tailwinds, so check whether the lift showed up in U.S. rates or only in volume.
2. **Adj. EPS vs. $2.28–$2.34 guide and ~$2.42 consensus.** Look for a beat above the top of the range.
3. **Q4 and FY2026 guidance.** Management flagged Q4 headwinds from unfavorable calendar shifts and midterm elections. The FY RevPAR range (+3.0% to +3.5%) is the number to watch. A raise is the bull signal, and holding it is neutral.
4. **U.S. vs. international RevPAR.** In Q2, U.S. comparable RevPAR rose 5.4% Y/Y, while Middle East & Africa fell about 30%. Any further Middle East deterioration is a risk.
5. **Adj. EBITDA and margins.** Compare against the $1.035B–$1.055B guide. *[unverified]* Watch for flow-through on fee revenue and any cost commentary.
6. **Capital return.** Guidance excludes future buybacks, so any buyback update would be additive.

## Catalyst Checklist

1. **Q3 RevPAR vs. ~4.0% guide** — the main driver of the top-line and fee outlook. Bulls need a beat here.
2. **Adj. EPS vs. $2.42 consensus (top of the $2.34 guide)** — the Street sits above guidance, so a beat only at the top of the range may not be enough.
3. **FY2026 RevPAR and EPS guidance** — a raise would offset a soft Q4 outlook. A hold after the Q3 raise may read as cautious.
4. **Q4 calendar and midterm commentary** — the stated headwind. Management tone here will drive the post-print move.
5. **International and Middle East exposure** — a drag in Q2 (about −30% RevPAR in MEA) that could widen.
