## 1. The Setup in One Paragraph

Hilton guided Q3 conservatively in July (~4% RevPAR, adj. EPS $2.28–2.34) and the stock sold off on it. Since then the US lodging data has been the best in three years — July RevPAR +8.2%, September +11.2%, and IHG's Q3 US RevPAR landed at **+6.9%** with occupancy at a nine-year high. Hilton is almost certainly going to beat its own RevPAR guide. The problem is that everyone knows: the stock hit a 52-week high of $359.62 on Oct 20, sell-side targets have been marching up (JPM to $387, MS to $349, WFC to $383), Wells Fargo has HLT as its **#1 lodging pick** into the print, and published consensus adj. EPS of $2.34–2.36 already sits **at or above the top of guidance**. This is a "good news is in the price" setup. The swing factor is not Q3 — it's the Q4 guide and the first real framing of 2027, into third-party forecasts (CoStar +2.1%, CBRE +2.1%) that call for sharp RevPAR deceleration next year.

## 3. Key Metrics to Watch — Ranked

1. **US comparable RevPAR.** The guide implied mid-single digits; the industry ran ~+7% and IHG printed +6.9% US. **Buy-side bar is ~+6%, not +4%.** A US number starting with a 5 is a disappointment regardless of the EPS line.
2. **Q4 guide and the FY26 raise.** FY EBITDA needs to go to ~$4.08B+ to satisfy. Management said in July that Q4 RevPAR would run *below* the 3.0–3.5% FY range on event timing — probe this, because the Q4'25 comp was depressed by the Oct 1–Nov 12, 2025 federal shutdown (~$650M of lost hotel business by Oct 22 alone). An unchanged "below the range" Q4 framing against that comp would read as genuine conservatism or genuine softness; the market will not know which, and will sell first.
3. **Net unit growth + pipeline.** Pipeline was a record 541,300 rooms / 3,853 hotels at Q2; Q2 signings of 42,900 rooms were the second-largest ever. Watch NUG tracking (6.3% WFC est.; 6.0–7.0% FY guide) and any first-pass 2027 NUG commentary. HLT's premium is a unit-growth-plus-FCF premium — this is the line item that defends the multiple.
4. **Chain-scale mix drag.** Q3 industry strength was heavily luxury/upper-upscale (+17.8% combined RevPAR) while economy RevPAR *fell* 2.7%. Hilton's weighting toward Hampton / Hilton Garden Inn / Tru means the industry headline overstates its read-through. Watch brand-level RevPAR disclosure.
5. **Known drags, quantified.** Middle East & Africa guided down high-single to low-double digits FY (>$20M EBITDA impact); China down low-single digits; ownership-segment renovations (consensus has ownership revenue *down* 3%). Any worsening here is what turns a RevPAR beat into an EBITDA miss.
6. **Owner economics / RISE and the loyalty-fee reduction.** Hilton is targeting 75–100bps of owner margin improvement. Watch whether this is costing Hilton effective fee rate — fee rate compression is the quiet bear case for an asset-light multiple.
7. **Capital return cadence.** ~$3.5B FY target; confirm pace and the share count trajectory (225.1M shares out).

## 5. Catalyst Checklist

- [ ] **US RevPAR ≥ +6%** (vs. ~+4% guide, ~+6.9% IHG read-through) — the single most important number; determines whether the industry data converted.
- [ ] **FY26 adj. EBITDA guide ≥ $4.08B** (top of current $4,040–4,080M) — anything at or below the old midpoint reads as a quarter of upside being banked, not earned.
- [ ] **Q4 RevPAR framing vs. the shutdown-depressed Q4'25 comp** — management previously said Q4 runs below the FY range; reiterating that against an easy comp is the most likely source of a negative reaction.
- [ ] **2027 RevPAR and NUG commentary** vs. CoStar/CBRE at ~+2.1% — the gap between 2026's ~4.4% industry RevPAR and a 2% 2027 is the core bear thesis for the group at these multiples.
- [ ] **Pipeline > 541,300 rooms and NUG tracking ≥ 6.3%** — the growth-premium defense.
- [ ] **Fee rate / RISE program economics** — confirm owner-margin giveback isn't structural.
