| Aug 3, 2026 | Q2 ongoing EPS $(0.21) vs $(0.06) est.; sales $3,517M (−6.8%) | **$2.50–$3.00** (sales ~$15.0B, EBIT margin ~4.0%, FCF >$300M, interest $350M) |

**The arithmetic that defines this print.** 1H26 ongoing EPS is **$(0.77)** (Q1 $(0.56) + Q2 $(0.21)). To reach the low end of the $2.50–$3.00 guide, 2H must deliver **$3.27–$3.77**. If Q3 prints the $0.78 consensus, **Q4 must then deliver $2.49–$2.99 — versus $1.10 actual in Q4'25**, in a demonstrably weaker demand environment, with 200bp of tariff drag and 24% more shares.

That is not a forecast; it is a placeholder. **Sell-side has already stopped believing it:** the aggregate FY26 analyst estimate is **~$1.13**, implying a Q4 of ~$1.12 — roughly flat with last year's Q4 and **$1.40–$1.90 per share below management's own implied Q4**.

> **Key implication:** a formal FY guidance cut is ~80% pre-discounted in published estimates. The stock is *not* primarily a guidance-cut short into this print. What is **not** discounted is a deterioration in free cash flow and the deleveraging path. That is where the asymmetry sits.

## 3. Key Metrics to Watch — Ranked

**1. Free cash flow and net debt (highest weight).** FY guide is >$300M FCF. Q1 FCF was **−$896M**; management described ~**−$1.1B** of cash consumption through Q2. That implies **2H FCF of roughly +$1.4B** — against FY2025's *full-year* FCF of $78M. Net debt was $5.8B at Q2 with a stated target of **<$5.0B by year-end**, requiring ~$800M of 2H reduction. WHR is seasonally Q4-weighted on cash, but this is an extraordinary ask. A reiterated net-debt target with visible Q3 working-capital progress is the single most bullish thing in the release; a quiet walk-back is the most bearish.

**2. Ongoing EBIT margin — the sequential ramp.** Q2 was **1.8%**. Consensus EPS of $0.78 on $3.91B back-solves to roughly **4.1% ongoing EBIT margin** — a ~230bp sequential jump in one quarter. ($0.99 consensus implies ~4.6%.) Management guided to sequential improvement of a magnitude similar to North America's 240bp Q1→Q2 step, so consensus is tracking guidance, but the bar is genuinely high.

*Margin → EPS bridge (71.3M diluted shares, 25% tax, ~$87.5M quarterly interest, $3.91B sales):*

|---|---|---|
| 1.8% (flat w/ Q2) | $70M | $(0.18) |
| 3.0% | $117M | $0.31 |
| **4.1%** | **$160M** | **$0.77** ← consensus |
| 5.0% | $196M | $1.14 |
| 5.5% | $215M | $1.34 |

**3. Price realization vs. trade-down.** Whirlpool pushed a >10% promotional price increase in late April, a 4% list increase in July, and builder increases in Q3. Q2 showed 225bp of favorable price impact and "largely flat" North America share. Q3 is the first clean quarter to test whether MAP discipline holds across the channel or whether competitors break ranks and consumers trade down. **Watch: North America price/mix contribution and any share commentary.**

**4. MDA North America EBIT margin.** $2.4B of the ~$3.9B revenue base. Q2 margin was 2.7% (−320bp y/y) off 240bp of sequential improvement. This segment alone determines whether the consolidated 4%+ is reachable.

**5. Latin America and SDA Global.** LatAm faced intense Brazilian promotional pressure (Q2 margin 3.0%, −300bp); ~5% August price actions are the fix. SDA (KitchenAid) ran 11.9–12% EBIT margin with 16% US sell-through growth, masked in Q2 by a trade-inventory burn that management said would normalize in Q3 — consensus has SDA +11.8%, so a miss here would be a credibility hit on management's own near-term call.

**6. Section 232 tariffs.** A 200bp headwind in Q2; management guided "similar levels in Q3 and Q4, plus/minus." Any escalation is unmodeled downside; mitigation/exclusion progress is unmodeled upside.

**7. Securities-law investigations.** Disclosed investigations have been referenced in third-party coverage without public specificity. Check the 10-Q legal-proceedings and contingencies footnotes for any new disclosure, reserve, or scope expansion — a low-probability, high-impact item for a stub equity.

**What management commentary signals each:**
- *Bull:* specific Q4 FCF bridge with named working-capital sources; net debt restated as "<$5.0B"; "price increases fully realized"; Amana/Rio Claro/Ramos footprint savings confirmed as landing in Q4.
- *Base:* "sequential improvement continues," FY range narrowed to the low end, FCF described as "approximately $300 million" rather than "more than."
- *Bear:* FCF reframed to "positive for the year," net-debt target softened to "progress toward," any mention of covenant headroom, or language about "elevated promotional environment persisting into 2027."

**Historical reaction calibration.** This stock does not trade on the EPS line:
- **Aug 3, 2026 (Q2):** *missed* EPS by $0.15 — stock **+13.8%** close-to-close ($39.21 → $44.62), +14.7% max intraday, on balance-sheet actions and a reaffirmed guide.
- **May 7, 2026 (Q1):** dividend suspension + guide cut from ~$6.00 to $3.00–3.50 — stock sold off hard.
- **Oct 2025 (Q3'25):** $2.09 vs. $1.39 — a +50% beat.
- Zacks cites an average −53.9% surprise over the trailing four quarters, though that average is heavily skewed by the Q1'26 blowup.

The Q2 precedent is the most instructive: **a miss paired with credible balance-sheet news produced a +14% rally.** Conversely, Q1's in-line-ish operations paired with a dividend cut produced a rout. Cash and capital structure dominate the reaction function.

## 5. Catalyst Checklist

1. **2H free cash flow bridge vs. the ~$1.4B required** — the only line that changes the solvency narrative. Reiterating ">$300M" without a Q3 cash proof point is a yellow flag, not a green one.
2. **Net debt vs. the <$5.0B year-end target** (from $5.8B at Q2) — measures whether the March recapitalization bought a runway or just time.
3. **Ongoing EBIT margin ≥4.0%** and the North America sequential step — tests whether the pricing-led margin recovery plan is real or a modeling artifact.
4. **FY26 ongoing EPS guidance resolution** — a cut to $1.75–$2.25 is largely in sell-side numbers (consensus FY ~$1.13 vs. guide $2.50–3.00); a cut *below* $1.50, or withdrawal of guidance entirely, is not.
5. **Price realization and MAP discipline commentary** — the >10% April + 4% July increases are the entire bull case; any evidence of rollback or share donation breaks the thesis.
6. **Any new securities-investigation disclosure in the 10-Q.**

**Reading the implied move.** The 15.8–18.4% figure is derived from the November 20 monthly, ~3.5 weeks out, so it embeds post-earnings drift and is an *upper* bound on the pure single-day event move. **Positioning read.** With a 21% equity-to-EV ratio, this is a levered stub: every ~$150M of annualized EBIT (1 margin point on $15B of sales) is worth roughly 9% of the market cap. That convexity cuts both ways and explains both the −50% six-month drawdown and why a *miss* rallied 14% in August. Note also the configuration into the print — fresh 52-week low on downgrade-driven selling, zero Buy ratings, and an average price target 35% above spot. That is capitulated positioning, which mechanically raises the payoff on any credible cash-flow reassurance.

**Net view.** The EPS line is close to irrelevant here. The distribution is bimodal around the free-cash-flow and net-debt disclosures, with the guidance cut largely pre-funded in estimates. Base case is a modest in-line-to-soft operational print with a formal FY reset that the market mostly shrugs at, and a reaction driven almost entirely by the 2H cash bridge. Given the 16-hour gap between the release and the call, the overnight tape will price the press release's cash-flow wording before management gets a chance to frame it.
