## 1. The Setup in One Paragraph

This is a **de-rating story meeting a profitability inflection**. CSGP is down ~50–55% YTD and ~68% over twelve months, having traded as low as $25.89 in late September before a ~20% bounce into the print. The bear case — that Homes.com is an open-ended cash furnace with an unknowable payoff window — collapsed the multiple to ~20.5x forward earnings, a level CSGP has not seen in its public history. Meanwhile the fundamentals inflected: Q2 adjusted EBITDA doubled to $184M, and the residential segment turned EBITDA-positive (+$12M) for the first time since Homes.com launched in Q1 2024. The market is no longer paying for revenue growth here; it is paying for **evidence that the EBITDA ramp is durable and that bookings have stopped deteriorating**. Those are the two things that matter tonight, and only one of them is currently trending the right way.

## 3. The Zonda Wedge — The Most Mispriced Detail in This Print

CoStar closed its **$800M all-cash acquisition of Zonda on August 21, 2026** — *after* Q3 guidance was issued on July 28. Zonda did ~$170M of 2025 revenue at a 23% adjusted EBITDA margin, serving 3,000+ homebuilding customers, and folds into the **residential** segment.

Roughly six weeks of Zonda sits inside Q3, worth approximately **$19–20M of revenue (~2% of the quarter)** and ~$4M of EBITDA. This matters three ways:

1. It largely explains why consensus is ~$6–11M above the guide midpoint. The "beat" may be substantially **bought, not earned**.
2. A headline revenue print of $950M with Zonda is only ~$930M organic — i.e. **below the low end of the organic guide**. Insist on the organic split.
3. It inflates residential segment growth optics precisely where the bull case needs clean evidence.

If management does not disclose the Zonda contribution, treat the revenue beat as low-quality.

**Operationally, what each requires:**

- **Bull** needs the Homes.com salesforce cut to prove *accretive* — fewer reps selling more, with the activist "noise" (Florance's word) gone from the sales motion. Listen for subscriber adds re-accelerating sequentially and price realization from the May 1 increase flowing through.
- **Base** is a quarter where EBITDA is fine and bookings are not. Expect management to lead with margin and 2028 targets while handling bookings questions as timing/seasonality. The stock chops because the multiple already discounts this.
- **Bear** is signaled by any *second* consecutive FY revenue reduction. CoStar cut FY26 revenue on the July call and held EBITDA — doing that twice converts a "cost discipline" narrative into a "demand problem" narrative, and the market will not pay for EBITDA bought with shrinking top line.

## 6. Catalyst Checklist

1. **Net new bookings vs. ~$75M** — the number that decides the quarter. Below $69M (no sequential progress) and the cost story stops mattering.
2. **FY26 revenue guidance: narrowed, held, or cut again?** A second cut is the clearest path to the bear case. Holding $3.715–3.755B while raising EBITDA toward $820M is the base case.
3. **First FY2027 commentary** — the buy-side wants a bridge from ~$800M (2026) to $1.25B (2028). Any FY27 EBITDA framing at/above $1.0B validates the path; silence or hedging reads as a soft reset.
4. **New CFO's first print.** Christian Lown departed July 31, 2026; **Robin Rossmann** (formerly Europe chief) now holds the seat. This is his first quarter owning guidance. New CFOs have both incentive and license to reset the bar — a kitchen-sink risk that is genuinely underpriced here. Baird's July downgrade explicitly cited the CFO exit *without a guidance reaffirmation* as conviction-lowering.
5. **Homes.com strategic posture.** Third Point and D.E. Shaw campaigned in January 2026 for a board overhaul and a Homes.com divestment; Third Point exited and Florance declared the fight behind him. Any softening toward a separation, partnership, or further investment cut would be a major narrative shift — and, at this multiple, likely a positive one.
6. **Zonda disclosure quality** — organic split, and whether FY26 guidance is being raised for the acquisition or absorbing it.

## 7. Trading Setup

**Recent performance:** −50–55% YTD, −68% 1-year, but **+~20–25% off the September low of $25.89** over the past month. The stock is fading ~4% into the print today, giving back part of that bounce. Forward P/E ~20.5x.

**Street positioning:** 12 Buy / 8 Hold / 1 Sell (21 analysts); mean target **$37.65** (~17% upside). Notably, the two most recent published targets — **Baird $34 (Neutral)** and **RBC $34 (Sector Perform)** — sit well below the mean, so the average is stale and skewed by un-revised bullish targets. Buy ratings have fallen from 12 to 8 over the past year per TIKR. Sell-side conviction is eroding, not building.

**Implied move — read this carefully.** Syndicated articles currently circulating cite implied moves of **5.9% ($5.02)** and **6.2% ($4.77)**. Both are **stale**: those dollar amounts imply an $77–85 underlying, i.e. they are from prior quarters. Do not use them. The reported 8-quarter **median realized move is ~5.1%**, but realized moves have gapped far wider recently (Q2's −12–13% after hours). Given a $32 underlying, a 60% drawdown, elevated event risk from a new CFO, and recent realized vol, I would expect options to be pricing roughly **8–11%** — but I could not verify a current straddle, so **confirm on a terminal before sizing**. A similarly-sourced claim of pre-earnings volume at 3.5x normal with puts leading calls 19:5 is also undated and should be treated as unverified.

**How I'd frame it:** The asymmetry is better than it looks because expectations are genuinely low and the multiple is trough — but the catalyst path runs through *bookings*, which have been deteriorating for two quarters, and through a *new CFO's first guide*, which is the single most common setting for a negative reset. Long-biased expressions are more attractive **after** the bookings number than before it. If implied move is ≥10%, premium selling into the event is defensible given the Q2 precedent of a sharp gap that fully mean-reverted within a session; if ≤7%, owning optionality is cheap relative to the realized distribution.
