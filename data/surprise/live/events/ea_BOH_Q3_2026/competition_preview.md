## 1. Setup in one paragraph

BOH goes into this print as a **broken-momentum, washed-out-positioning name**: the stock is ~21% below its 52-week high ($86.31), roughly $9 above the 52-week low ($59.36), and trading well under both its 50-day (~$74–76) and 200-day (~$77) averages after falling ~13% in a month and ~17% in a quarter. The de-rating started with the July 27 Q2 print — an EPS beat ($1.47 vs. $1.46) overwhelmed by a revenue miss ($196.9M vs. ~$199.5M) and guidance for **average earning assets to decline $100–200M in Q3** on 10–15% runoff in public deposits — and was compounded by the October regional-bank credit scare (KRE −6.2% on Oct 16 on Zions/Western Alliance loan losses) and, on October 23, **First Hawaiian's Q3 miss** ($0.52 vs. $0.59, $20.3M provision including a $12.5M downtown Seattle office charge-off, loan growth guidance cut to 1–2% from 3–4%). The asymmetry has shifted: the fundamental story (ten straight quarters of NIM expansion, pristine credit, 4.2% yield) is now priced for disappointment, so the swing factor is **credit confirmation**, not EPS.

## 3. Key metrics to watch (ranked)

1. **Credit: provision, NCOs, and the criticized-asset ratio (2.81% in Q2).** The single borrower relationship that drove the Q2 criticized increase was described as performing and well-secured — any migration to NPA or a specific reserve resets the narrative. With FHB taking a $12.5M office charge-off and NCOs spiking to 60bp annualized, the market will extrapolate across the Hawai'i comp set. BOH's bar: hold NCOs near 10bp and NPAs near 8bp.
2. **NIM and the 2.90% December exit rate.** Ten consecutive quarters of expansion is the core long thesis. Q2 guidance assumed a 25bp **hike** in mid-September — which the Fed delivered (to 3.75–4.00%, first hike since 2023, with another 25bp in the September dot plot by year-end). Rising short rates cut both ways: faster fixed-asset repricing vs. renewed deposit-cost pressure. Watch whether CFO Satenberg reaffirms the 2.90% exit and extends the +5bp/quarter cadence into 2027.
3. **Deposits and earning-asset trajectory.** Did public deposit runoff land inside 10–15%? Was the 3.5–4.0%-cost money replaced, shrunk, or swapped into borrowings? Noninterest-bearing mix (27% in Q2) is the quality tell. A bigger-than-guided AEA decline is the most likely source of a revenue shortfall.
4. **Loan growth guidance.** FY guide is low-to-mid single digits; FHB just cut to 1–2% citing "unexpected refinancings and maturities." If BOH trims, the 2027 NII bridge gets harder and $6.35 FY27 consensus is at risk.
5. **Expenses: $112.5M normalized in Q3, ~$448M FY26 (+3%).** Plus any commentary on the Guam/Saipan branch closures during Super Typhoon Champi (closed Oct 16; Tamuning/Harmon reopened Oct 18) — a Q4 event, so expect commentary rather than a Q3 charge.
6. **Capital return and TBV.** $20M of buyback was planned for each of Q3 and Q4 with $89M remaining authorization; executing at 52-week lows (~1.8x TBV of $38.01) is a credibility signal. Watch TBV/share — the September hike and higher long rates are an AOCI headwind to book value (CET1 of 12.12% should be insulated if BOH carries the standard AOCI opt-out election; verify on the release).

**What management commentary would signal each:** Bull = "margin trajectory intact into 2027" plus unprompted detail that the criticized relationship has upgraded or paid down. **Historical calibration:** BOH has beaten EPS consensus in 3 of the last 4 quarters, yet the Q2 beat produced a **−4.7%** day-one move on the revenue miss plus AEA guidance. Contrast Q3 2025 (Oct 27, 2025), when a beat on $182.6M revenue and a 2.46% NIM sent shares higher. **The pattern is clear: EPS beats don't pay here; revenue/NII and balance-sheet direction set the tape.**

## 5. Catalyst checklist

1. **Provision and criticized assets vs. the FHB read-across** — the single biggest determinant of the reaction. Hawai'i-specific credit contagion is the live bear case and it is partially priced.
2. **NIM print vs. ~2.83% and reaffirmation of the 2.90% December exit** — the entire 2027 EPS bridge ($6.35 consensus) rests on this cadence continuing with the Fed now hiking rather than cutting.
3. **Revenue vs. $202.6M consensus** — likely a miss on a bottom-up basis; the question is whether the buy-side has already marked to ~$199M. If the sell-side number is what trades, expect a knee-jerk red headline worth a few percent.
4. **Average earning assets / deposit runoff vs. the $100–200M guide** — the cleanest check on management credibility for the new leadership pair (Jim Polk became President & CEO on March 31, 2026; Brad Satenberg is in his second year as CFO).
5. **Capital return and any 2027 framing** — buyback execution at ~1.8x TBV, plus first indications on 2027 expenses and loan growth. First-year CEO prints are where strategic resets get announced; watch for any wealth-management or efficiency initiative.
