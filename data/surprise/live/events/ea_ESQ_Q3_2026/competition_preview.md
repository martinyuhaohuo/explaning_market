## The One Thing That Matters

**This is the first reporting period that includes Signature Bancorporation, and it is a two-month stub, not a full quarter.** The deal closed **August 1, 2026**, so Q3 contains **two of three months** of a franchise that roughly *doubled* the company (Signature: $1.95B assets, $1.36B loans, $1.70B deposits at 3/31/26, vs. ESQ standalone ~$2.5B).

Three mechanical consequences dominate this print, and they matter more than any operating trend:

1. **Share count is a blended average.** ESQ issued **3,446,992 shares** at a final exchange ratio of **2.671x** (base 2.63x, collar 2.50–2.80x, settled by a 62.0% actual recovery on ~$70M of "Schedule A" loans vs. 50% assumed). Weighted-average diluted shares should land near **~11.0M** (vs. 8.72M in Q2 and ~12.2M exiting), not 12.2M.
2. **GAAP will be ugly and largely meaningless.** The deal deck's tangible-book reconciliation carries **$27.9M of after-tax restructuring expense** (~$37M pre-tax). Only **$2.3M pre-tax** had been recognized through 1H26 ($1.07M in Q2). The bulk — call it $20M+ after-tax — should land in Q3. That alone is ~**$1.85/share of GAAP drag**, plus a likely day-two CECL provision on acquired non-PCD loans and **$49.3M of core deposit intangible** beginning to amortize. **GAAP EPS could print near zero or negative.**
3. **Purchase-accounting accretion is the whole beat/miss.** Pro forma 2027E assumed **$9.3M/yr of rate-mark accretion** (~$2.3M/qtr straight-line, but accretion is typically front-loaded) against **$(4.6)M/yr of intangible amortization** and only **$0.8M after-tax of cost savings** (50% realization of a deliberately conservative 5% cost-save assumption).

**Trade the adjusted number and the NIM. Ignore the headline.** The 90-minute gap between an 8:30 a.m. release and a 10:00 a.m. call is where mispricing happens.

**Operationally, what each requires:**
- **Bull** needs NIM to beat *on core spread*, not accretion, plus evidence the Chicago litigation pipeline is converting. Listen for Sagliocca naming specific Chicago contingency-fee firms onboarded — the deck's thesis was that Chicago is a top-3 market by contingency-fee firm count.
- **Base** is simply "guidance delivered." The tell is management reaffirming the ~545bps day-one NIM and the path back toward 6%.
- **Bear** is triggered by any of: deposit costs at Signature running hotter than modeled, a credit mark that looks thin, or management walking back the 2027 accretion math. Watch for hedging language on "timing of cost savings."

## Catalyst Checklist

1. **NIM vs. the 540–545bps guide — with the accretion split disclosed.** The whisper is effectively "at or above 545." Below 535bps re-rates the deal thesis, because the marketed 23% 2027 GAAP EPS accretion depends on it.
2. **Adjusted EPS vs. a consensus I believe is mechanically high.** Given the stub-period share count, treat **~$1.70–1.80 as the real bar** and KBW's $1.83 as the better-informed print. A headline "miss vs. $1.89" that still clears ~$1.75 adjusted is a buyable overreaction — *if* the bridge is clean.
3. **Size and phasing of restructuring + any non-PCD CECL provision.** Investors need to know how much of the $27.9M after-tax is now behind them vs. still to come in Q4. Unquantified "additional charges expected" language is the single biggest downside trigger.
4. **Litigation vertical growth and the concentration glidepath.** The deal was sold partly as de-risking: litigation concentration falling from **70%+ to below 50%**. Confirming that while litigation balances still compound 20%+ is the bull case's core.
5. **First combined-company outlook commentary.** ESQ does not give formal EPS guidance, so the call is the only venue. Any upgrade to the conservative 5% cost-save assumption, or first mention of Chicago revenue synergies (explicitly *excluded* from the pro forma), is upside not in estimates.
