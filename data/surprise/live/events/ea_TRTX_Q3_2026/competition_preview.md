## 1. The Setup in One Paragraph

TRTX goes into this print with a portfolio that looks fine on paper — 100% performing, weighted-average risk rating 3.0, office down to 4.3% of commitments — and an equity price that says the market doesn't believe it. The stock is ~33% off its 52-week high ($9.37) and trades at 0.57x stated book. The de-rating is mostly *sector beta, not company news*: the 10-year hit 5.27% in September, mortgage REITs fell 13.7% that month, and office CMBS delinquencies re-spiked to 12.2%. Meanwhile the debt market is voting the other way — TRTX priced the $1.0B **TRTX 2026-FL8** CRE CLO on Oct 2 at an ~87.4% advance rate and SOFR+143, non-mark-to-market. **That contradiction is the trade.** Q3 is the quarter where management has to show that book value is defensible and that the earnings power step-up from Q2's late-closing originations is real.

## 4. My Estimate — I'm Below Consensus

Q2 DE of $0.23 was depressed by timing: "chunky" repayments landed in the first three weeks of Q2, while ~70% of the $466M of new originations closed **in the final three days**. So Q3 inherits a higher average earning-asset balance, plus $72M closed post-quarter and ~$380M of executed term sheets pending.

Walking it forward from $0.23:

| Driver | Est. impact |
|---|---|
| Higher avg earning assets (~+$250M at ~2.0% net spread) | **+$0.016** |
| Lower share count (78.0M → ~76.9M diluted avg) | **+$0.003** |
| Funding-cost air pocket — FL5 CLO ($597.8M) redeemed in Q2, **FL8 didn't close until Oct 2**, so Q3 ran on warehouse lines plus the new $400M Term Loan B at SOFR+275 | **−$0.01 to −$0.02** |
| **Q3'26 DE estimate** | **$0.24–$0.25** |

**That's a ~1–2c miss vs. the $0.26 consensus.** The single biggest unknown is Q3 repayment volume, which I can't model from public data — if repayments were light, $0.26 is reachable. TRTX has beaten EPS only ~25% of the time over the past two years and missed Q2 by 11.6%.

**FL8 is a Q4 story, not a Q3 one.** Don't let a soft Q3 funding cost obscure it: an 87.4% advance rate at SOFR+143 on non-recourse, non-MTM terms with a 30-month reinvestment period is a materially better liability than the warehouse debt it replaced. That's the forward earnings bridge, and it's also third-party validation of collateral quality from people who underwrote the loans.

## 5. Key Metrics to Watch — Ranked

1. **Book value per share.** At 0.57x book, this *is* the stock. Flat-to-up ≥$10.95 breaks the three-quarter erosion and re-anchors the discount debate. Below $10.75 and the bear case ("stated book isn't real book") gets the mic.
2. **CECL reserve and risk-rating migration.** Reserve has sat at 179bp for two quarters. Watch the "4"-rated bucket (3 loans / $172.7M) for additions, any first "5"-rated loan, and whether REO count goes from 5 to 6. A reserve build through ~200bp would signal management's own view has changed.
3. **Buyback authorization.** Only **$9.3M remained** at the end of Q2. At $6.28 vs. $10.95 book, every $10M repurchased adds roughly **$0.07/share of book value** — by far the highest-return use of capital available. A fresh/expanded authorization is the cleanest signal management believes its own marks. Its *absence* is informative too.
4. **Q4 dividend posture.** The Q3 dividend is banked. A ~15% yield means the market is handicapping a cut in the mid-December declaration. Any language on "dividend policy under review" vs. an affirmation of earnings-power recovery will move the stock more than the EPS line.
5. **Net interest margin / cost of funds.** Quantified FL8 accretion, the TLB drag, and the share of non-MTM borrowings (85.2% in Q2, should rise with FL8).
6. **Leverage.** Total leverage rose to 3.32x from ~3.1x (D/E 2.94x). Above ~3.4x with a widening discount to book invites a balance-sheet-capacity question.

## 7. Catalyst Checklist

1. **BVPS vs. $10.95** — the discount narrative lives or dies here. Confirms or refutes whether the 0.57x multiple reflects reality or sector contagion.
2. **DE vs. $0.26 consensus** — I'm at $0.24–0.25. A 1–2c miss is *already partly discounted*; the reaction function depends far more on #1 and #3 than on this line.
3. **Credit migration: 4-rated bucket, first 5-rated loan, 6th REO** — in a market where office CMBS delinquency is 12.2% and lenders are writing down loans by up to 85%, a clean credit slate is the thesis. One crack is worth more than 2c of EPS.
4. **New share repurchase authorization** — with $9.3M left and the stock at 0.57x book, this is the highest-conviction value-accretive lever management holds. Watch for it.
5. **Q4 dividend language** — the market is pricing a cut; management isn't obligated to address it, and silence will be read as ambiguity.
