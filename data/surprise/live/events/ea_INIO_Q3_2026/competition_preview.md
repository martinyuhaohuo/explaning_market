## The Setup in One Paragraph

INNIO is the rare name where the operating business and the stock are telling opposite stories. Orders are at records ($6.6B equipment backlog, +279% YoY; 2027 sold out; >15 GW of backlog plus slot reservations stretching to 2030–31), yet the shares sit **~62% below the 52-week high and ~39% below the IPO price**. Three things explain the gap, and none of them are about Q3 demand: (1) a sector-wide unwind of the AI-power trade — since the S&P 500's Oct. 6 record, data-center build-out suppliers are down a median ~23%, with GE Vernova -11%, Vertiv -14% and Bloom -15% in a single week on Meta capex-financing and rate worries; (2) **margin compression** — equipment segment margin fell to ~14% from 19% YoY as INNIO deliberately spends to triple capacity; and (3) a **structural overhang** — the IPO was 100% secondary (Advent took all $2.43B of proceeds; the company got none), Advent still holds ~660M of 750M shares (~88%, ~12% float), and the **180-day lock-up expires Dec. 1, 2026**. Short interest has gone vertical into the print: ~21.1M shares as of Oct. 13 (+25% w/w, +70% m/m), roughly **22.8% of float**, with easy borrow at ~1.2%. This is a high-convexity, thin-float event, not a steady-compounder print.

## The Guidance Math — Why Q3 Is a Setup Quarter, Not the Decider

H1 FY26 delivered revenue of **$1,606.5M** (+39%) and adjusted EBITDA of **$294.8M (18.4%)**. Against the full-year guide, that leaves:

| | H1 FY26 Actual | H2 FY26 Implied (at guide) | Required Δ |
|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue | $1,606.5M | **$2,194–2,294M** | +37% to +43% H/H |
| Adj. EBITDA | $294.8M | **$425–445M** | +44% to +51% H/H |
| Adj. EBITDA margin | 18.4% | **~19.4%** | +100bps |

If Q3 lands at the consensus $1.05B, **Q4 must print $1.14–1.24B** — which would be, by a wide margin, the largest quarter in company history. Management flagged exactly this on the Q2 call ("further acceleration in the second half," stronger Q4 on shipment cadence and capacity ramp). So consensus is internally consistent with the guide, but it back-loads almost all of the risk into a quarter the company won't report until February.

The practical consequence: **a Q3 revenue beat is nearly worthless on its own**, and a small Q3 miss is survivable if Q4 commentary holds. What matters is whether the margin bridge is tracking.

**The equipment margin bridge (the crux).** Backing service out of H1 adjusted segment EBITDA of $321.3M (service revenue $714.7M at the ~30% management cited ≈ $214M) leaves roughly **$107M on $891.8M of equipment revenue — about 12% for H1**, with Q2 at ~13.8% (equipment revenue $569.3M, adj. segment EBITDA $78.6M). Management guided equipment margin to **"high teens by year-end."** Getting from ~14% to ~18% in two quarters is a genuine ask. Q3 needs to show **15–16.5%** for the year-end target to be credible. Below 14.5% and the FY EBITDA guide is arithmetically strained no matter how good the orders look.

## Key Metrics to Watch — Ranked

1. **Equipment segment adjusted EBITDA margin.** Q2: ~13.8%. Need **15–16.5%**. This is the number the bears own and the number that broke the stock. Containerized solutions (the J624 units going to data centers) carry different scope economics than legacy packaged engines, so mix is a live variable, not just cost absorption.
2. **FY26 adjusted EBITDA guidance treatment.** Reaffirm $720–740M is the base case. A narrowing to the **low end** reads as a cut. A raise is the bull trigger and, given order momentum, is not impossible — but management has every incentive to under-promise on its second-ever print.
3. **Q3 equipment order intake and backlog.** Q1 $1,617.5M, Q2 $2,300M, backlog $6.6B. **Important timing nuance that I expect will be mishandled on the tape:** the 620 MW follow-on order (Oct. 22), the Aggreko extension through 2031 (Oct. 9), and the Enerflex APAC aftermarket close (Oct. 3) are all **Q4 events**. Q3 standalone orders could look pedestrian versus Q2's 1.1 GW-inflated $2.3B, and backlog may read roughly flat. If management doesn't explicitly pre-frame the ~$2B+ of already-announced Q4 bookings on the call, a mechanical "orders decelerating" headline is a real risk — and a buying opportunity if the rest of the print holds.
4. **Free cash flow and capex.** Q2 FCF was $205M (+352%), structurally supported by customer down payments that keep equipment cash-positive from order to commissioning (H1 operating cash flow $443.6M; cash $1,039.9M vs. $689.5M at year-end). The capacity ramp from 3.5 GW (2025) to ~10 GW (2030) across Austria, Wisconsin, New Jersey and Texas is supposed to be **self-funded from operating cash flow**. Any quarter where capex breaks that promise — against $2,618.7M of total debt — changes the story from "capital-light annuity" to "capital-intensive cyclical."
5. **RPO conversion and 2027 color.** $8.8B of unsatisfied performance obligations with ~79% recognizable within 24 months. With 2027 described as sold out, any early 2027 revenue framing becomes the de facto guide and will matter more than the Q3 numbers.
6. **Lock-up and Advent.** Not a reported metric, but the highest-stakes question on the call. See catalysts.

| **Base** (50%) | $1.03–1.08B | $0.10–0.14 | 15–16.5% | Guide reaffirmed; margin bridge intact but back-loaded; orders optically soft on Q2 comp | **-5% to +12%**, pop-and-fade likely |
| **Bear** (25%) | <$1.00B | ≤$0.08 | ≤14.5% | Guide trimmed to low end; equipment margin flat or down; capex-driven FCF reversal; evasive on Advent | **-15% to -28%**, new lows <$14.58 |

**What has to happen operationally, by case.** The bull case requires the Q4 ramp to be visibly de-risked — units already shipped or in commissioning, not scheduled. Listen for shipment-cadence specifics and whether the Enerflex APAC aftermarket deal adds to service margin immediately. The bear case doesn't require a demand problem at all; it only requires that the cost of tripling capacity is running ahead of the revenue it unlocks. That is the honest bear thesis, and it is an execution question, not a cancellation question.

**Management commentary that signals each.** Bullish tells: repeating "high teens by year-end" **with a Q3 datapoint that supports it**; naming specific Q4 shipment volumes; reiterating accretive pricing on newer backlog and higher service intensity. Bearish tells: shifting the margin-recovery language from "by year-end" to "into 2027"; attributing margin to "scope changes" without quantifying; declining to discuss Q4 order timing.

## Catalyst Checklist

1. **Equipment segment margin vs. the ~15–16% whisper.** Published consensus has no equipment-margin line, which is precisely why it's the swing factor. The buy-side number is "mid-teens and rising." Miss it and the FY guide loses credibility regardless of the revenue headline.
2. **FY26 adjusted EBITDA guidance ($720–740M) — reaffirm, raise, or narrow.** With consensus already at the low end of the revenue range, a narrowing to $720M is the asymmetric risk. The buy-side wants the midpoint defended with Q4 shipment specifics, not assertions.
3. **The Dec. 1 lock-up — any signal on Advent's intentions.** ~646–660M shares come free in roughly five weeks. Management will likely decline to comment, but the question will be asked. A marketed secondary at these levels, or even a hint of one, overwhelms any operational beat. Conversely, a voluntary lock-up extension, an Advent standstill, or a company buyback authorization would be the most powerful positive catalyst available — arguably worth more than a guidance raise, because it addresses the actual reason the multiple compressed.
4. **Q4 order pre-framing.** Whether management explicitly bridges Q3 reported backlog to the announced 620 MW (cumulative 1.72 GW from that single customer) and Aggreko capacity reservation. This determines whether "orders decelerating" becomes the headline.
5. **FCF and the self-funding claim.** The Sept. 22 credit-rating upgrades and reaffirmed financial policy set a bar. Capex guidance for the 10 GW ramp against $2.6B of debt and ~$60M/quarter of interest is the quiet solvency-of-the-story question.

## Trading Setup

**Recent performance.** Down ~16.4% over the past month and ~62% from the $42.95 high; new 52-week lows on **Oct. 14** and **Oct. 20** on analyst actions, then a **-10.7% day on Oct. 22 — the same day the 620 MW order was announced.** Good news not being paid for is the clearest read on positioning available: this stock is currently trading as an AI-capex beta instrument, not on its own order book. The +8.0% bounce on Oct. 26 into the print is consistent with short covering ahead of a binary event in a ~90M-share float.

**Implied move.** No quoted ATM straddle was obtainable for INIO. **IV30 is 96.6%** (Oct. 23), with a 1-standard-deviation *daily* range of roughly $14.38–$16.04 (±5.5%). Backing a post-event baseline vol of 75–80% out of the 30-day variance implies an earnings-day move of roughly **±15–18%**. **This is my derivation, not a market-quoted number** — and option liquidity is thin (200 contracts traded vs. a 1,495 30-day average), so the straddle is wide and the estimate is soft. Directionally, the market is priced for a very large move, and my base case (-5% to +12%) is *narrower* than implied — which argues for selling premium over buying it, if you can get fills. Put/call ratio of 4.21, in line with its 20-day average, indicates persistent defensive positioning rather than fresh panic.

**Short interest.** ~21.1M shares (Oct. 13), **~22.8% of float**, +70% in a month, borrow at ~1.23% with 405% of short interest available to borrow. There is no squeeze mechanic from borrow scarcity, but 22.8% of a 12% float is a genuine fuel source on a clean beat-and-raise. This cuts both ways: it also means a lot of informed capital has done this work and concluded the margin bridge breaks.

**Sell-side.** 8 of 10 Buy; mean target ~$35–38 (10 analysts) against a ~$16 tape — a ~115%+ implied upside that is itself a warning sign, since targets this stale almost always move toward price. Recent revisions are uniformly lower: **JPMorgan $40→$33 (Overweight maintained)**, **Morgan Stanley $47→$40 (Overweight)**, **Citigroup $30→$22**, Weiss to Sell (D-) on Oct. 14. Expect further target cuts on almost any outcome short of a raise.

**Historical reaction.** Only one prior print exists. On **July 28**, INNIO beat on both lines (revenue $937.7M vs. ~$885M; EPS $0.08 vs. $0.05, a 60% upside surprise) with record orders, and **shares still fell ~2.9%** on the day as the $81.2M of IPO costs drove a $16.9M net loss. Ortex characterizes the July event as producing a **~19% post-earnings decline**, which I read as the multi-week drift rather than the day-one move — the two figures are not reconcilable as same-day prints, so treat the day-one -2.9% as the firmer datapoint. Either way, the lesson holds and it is the most important calibration available: **n=1, and the one observation says a beat does not get paid.** With one quarter of history, no reliable reaction distribution exists; size accordingly.

## Bottom Line

The base case is a modest revenue beat, an in-line-to-slightly-soft GAAP EPS, equipment margin improving but not yet convincingly, and a reaffirmed guide — a print the market grades on the margin bridge and the Q4 ramp rather than the headline. Risk/reward into the event is roughly symmetric and uncomfortably wide: the operating story is genuinely strong and the valuation has already de-rated hard, but **the two things most likely to determine the next 20% — the AI-capex tape and the Dec. 1 lock-up — are both things this earnings report cannot fix.** A beat-and-raise with a credible margin datapoint buys a sharp squeeze; it does not remove ~646M shares of pending supply five weeks out. The cleanest expression here is not directional into the print but post-print, after the margin line resolves the main fundamental debate and ahead of a lock-up event the market has, in my read, already partly discounted.
