## 3. Key metrics to watch (ranked)

1. **Nonaccruals and the $11.4M CRA loan.** NPLs went $3.1M (Dec'25) → $2.8M (Q1) → **$13.4M / 0.30%** (Q2), almost entirely one Community Reinvestment Act credit placed on nonaccrual. Equally important is the migration *behind* it: loans designated watch rose $101.9M → **$154.6M** and special mention $37.5M → **$44.9M** in 1H26. This is the number that sets the stock.
2. **NIM and the rate pivot.** 74% of loans are adjustable/floating. The Fed's first hike in three years (Sept 16) makes FSBC's asset sensitivity a Q4 tailwind — but raises deposit betas and CRE borrower debt-service stress simultaneously.
3. **Capital deployment pace.** $129.9M of fresh equity supports roughly $1.2–1.3B of asset growth. Loans grew 29% annualized in Q2. Does the pace hold, and at what spread?
4. **Purchased consumer loans.** Q2 included **$124.5M of purchased consumer loans** — off-pattern for a relationship CRE/commercial lender and a new credit vector with no seasoning. Size, yield, FICO, and whether it repeats.
5. **Expense trajectory.** Efficiency 38.57% → 40.91% on the Walnut Creek, Newport Beach, and Lodi build-out. Does it stabilize below 41%?

## 5. Catalyst checklist

1. **Nonaccrual direction** vs. the 0.30% Q2 base — the dominant variable. A print above ~0.50%, or a *second* named CRE relationship, overwhelms any EPS beat.
2. **Q4 NIM guidance** — the buy-side wants 3.70%+ exiting 2026 on the back of the September hike and the free-equity benefit. Silence will be read as weakness.
3. **Deployment plan for the $129.9M** — at ~10.8% TCE/TA (my estimate) FSBC is over-capitalized for the first time. Growth cadence, and whether buyback enters the conversation below ~1.8x TBV.
4. **Purchased consumer loan disclosure** — recurring strategy or one-off? This is the least-understood item in the portfolio and the easiest place for a negative surprise.
5. **The Q&A itself.** Last quarter's call had no Q&A because the offering hadn't settled. Two quarters of questions — the CRA credit, consumer pools, capital — land in a single session on Oct 27.

## 6. Trading setup

- **Price/valuation:** $44.48, ~11% below the $50.26 high. 12.7–13.2x trailing, **12.8x forward**, ~**1.8x pro-forma TBV** (my estimate, post-raise), 2.25% yield.
- **Dividend:** $0.25 declared Oct 16 (payable Nov 9, record Nov 2) — **held flat** despite the capital raise, consistent with capital retained for growth.
- **Insiders:** 142,727 shares (~$6.28M) purchased in the trailing 90 days — a genuine positive, though the window overlaps the July offering, so some is likely participation rather than open-market conviction.
- **Options-implied move: not reliably observable.** FSBC's chains are thin and wide; the one public IV datapoint is a single out-of-the-money Aug'26 $50 call screening on high IV, which is not a usable straddle. **Use realized history instead: ±3–5% next-day, with a materially fatter left tail than right.**
- **Two-day event risk.** The print is Monday after close, the call is Tuesday 1:00 PM ET, and the **FOMC decision is Wednesday, Oct 28, 2:00 PM ET** (~91% priced for no change, ~9% for another hike). Any Monday/Tuesday move in a 0.55-beta bank can be overwritten by Wednesday's rate decision and regional-bank factor move. Single-day expressions of a view here are poorly compensated.

**Setup, in one line:** consensus looks beatable on revenue and roughly fair on net income, and FSBC has beaten EPS four quarters running — but the payoff is asymmetric to the downside, because the stock is trading on CRE credit migration and not on the $0.02–0.03 of EPS that share-count convention alone can create.
